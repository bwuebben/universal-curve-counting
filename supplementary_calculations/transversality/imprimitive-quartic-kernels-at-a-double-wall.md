# G1. Kernels on imprimitive quartic covers at a prescribed double wall

Let $C$ be a smooth closed curve of genus $g\ge2$ and $N$ a complex plane bundle of degree $2g-2$. Let $D$ be a real Cauchy–Riemann operator on $N$, invertible on $C$. Fix a smooth connected branched double $f:Y\to C$ such that $\ker f^*D=\mathbb R\xi$ is odd and its graph is embedded. Assume that the branch and time derivative at this lower crossing is an isomorphism. At one simple ramification point assume that $B(c)v\notin\mathbb Cv$, where $B$ is the antilinear term in the lower equation and $v$ is the nonzero vertical derivative of $\xi$. As in [Calculation E1](../polar/the-normal-twist-at-a-ramified-quartic-tower.md), there is a disk of regular lower values with a sheet of nonzero height $s$ on which $B s\notin\mathbb C s$. In fact $s=zv+O(|z|^2)$ in $w=c+z^2$, so this assertion holds above sufficiently small nonzero $w-c$. Fix a relatively compact subdisk with this property.

Use smooth real zeroth-order variations supported in the closure of this subdisk and annihilating the real vector $s$ pointwise. These form a closed linear subspace in the smooth topology and also kill the opposite sheet height. In a small neighborhood they preserve $f,\xi$, the one-dimensional lower kernel, transversality, base invertibility, and the condition on the antilinear part on a common smaller disk. A time cutoff inserts such a change at the lower crossing. A residual set below refers to this neighborhood in the smooth perturbation space.

**Theorem.** For a residual set of such perturbations, simultaneously in every source genus, let $u:X\to C$ be a connected finite holomorphic cover with smooth source. If its degree is two, or if its degree is four and $u$ factors through an intermediate double, then
$$
 \ker u^*D=
 \begin{cases}
    \mathbb R\,p^*\xi,& u=fp\text{ for a map }p:X\to Y,\\
    0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                               \tag{1}
$$
The two choices of $p$ have opposite pullback generators. Every connected degree-three cover has zero kernel for the same residual set. The ramification indices and coincidences of branch values of $u$ are unrestricted.

## Equivariant operator and branch parameters

Let $\pi:\widetilde X\to C$ be a connected Galois cover with deck group $G$, and $V$ an irreducible real representation with a $G$-invariant inner product. Consider the real operator on $G$-invariant sections and forms with coefficients in $\pi^*N\otimes_{\mathbb R} V^*$, obtained by applying $\pi^*D$ in the first factor. Its kernel is $\operatorname{Hom}_G(V,\ker\pi^*D)$. It is Fredholm on $W^{1,p}\to L^p$, $p>2$, by the finite group projections on the two Banach spaces. For each branch value $c$ let $I_c$ be an inertia group on its fiber. Then
$$
       \operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_V
                  =-2\sum_c\big(\dim_{\mathbb R}V-\dim_{\mathbb R}V^{I_c}\big).
                                                                             \tag{2}
$$
For the index, homotope to a pulled-back Dolbeault operator. The direct image on $C$ of the equivariant complex is the Dolbeault cohomology of $N\otimes E_V$, where $E_V=(\pi_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}\otimes_{\mathbb R}V^*)^G$. Finite direct image and the exact invariant projection give this cohomology identification. The bundle $E_V$ has rank $k=\dim_{\mathbb R}V$. Its pulled-back evaluation into the trivial bundle with fiber $V^*_{\mathbb C}$ is an isomorphism off ramification. At an inertia group of order $e$, write $w=z^e$ and diagonalize a generator. The invariant generators in an eigenbasis are $z^{j_i}e_i$ for the required residues $0\le j_i<e$. A conjugate pair of nonreal eigenvalues contributes $e$ to $\sum_i j_i$, a negative eigenvalue contributes $e/2$, and a fixed vector contributes zero. Hence the evaluation cokernel has length $e(k-\dim V^{I_c})/2$ at each of the $|G|/e$ points over $c$. Taking degree gives $\deg E_V=-\sum_c(k-\dim V^{I_c})/2$. Riemann–Roch for the rank $2k$ bundle $N\otimes E_V$ gives (2). This argument also covers a real representation of complex type. [Equivariant pullback operators, Bai–Swaminathan, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4#S2.SS1).

On a fixed monodromy stratum let $r$ be the number of *distinct* branch values of the Galois cover. The stratum has $r$ complex parameters. Locally mark the values, transport their monodromies by an isotopy, cover the complementary punctured surface, and fill in with $z\mapsto z^e$ at each puncture. This construction gives all nearby covers of that type by uniqueness of the holomorphic extension. There are only finitely many monodromy homomorphisms to a fixed $G$ for fixed marked branch data. In particular two ramification points over one value in a given stratum contribute one moving value there. If $V$ is faithful, every one of these $r$ inertia groups acts nontrivially, so
$$
              \operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_V+2r-1\le -1.                   \tag{3}
$$
The subtraction by one projectivizes a nonzero real solution. If an inertia group is invisible in a representation, one can instead form the operator and the parameter count on its faithful quotient cover. This distinction is needed for the intermediate double characters.

## The test on a regular disk

We prove surjectivity of the universal projective kernel equation in the cases needed for (1). Suppose $V$ is a faithful real plane, or is the sign line of a double different from $f$. A nonzero equivariant solution is an injective real $G$-map from $V$ into the space of global solutions on $\widetilde X$, by irreducibility. Choose a real basis, and denote its images by $u_1,u_2$ in the plane case and by $u_1$ in the line case. Form a connected common Galois cover of $\widetilde X$ and $Y$, using the intersection of the monodromy kernels on the complement of their branch divisors and normalizing over the removed points. The lifted solution space contains the irreducible $V$-module spanned by the $u_i$ and the lower sign line spanned by $s$ (globally, the pullback of $\xi$). These modules are inequivalent. For two double monodromies, equivalent sign characters on the common punctured curve would give the same completed double cover. Consequently $s,u_1$ or $s,u_1,u_2$, as applicable, are linearly independent global solutions over $\mathbb R$.

In the fixed testing disk choose a smaller disk missing the branch values of this cover. Identify one lift with the disk. If $u_1=a s$ throughout an open disk there, for a complex-valued coefficient $a$, the product rule modulo $\mathbb C s$ gives $(\bar a-a)[Bs]=0$. Thus $a$ is real and $\bar\partial a=0$. Unique continuation would give a global constant dependence. There is therefore a subdisk on which $s,u_1$ are complex independent. In the plane case, if $u_2=a s+b u_1$ throughout this subdisk for real functions $a,b$, these functions are smooth and the real Leibniz rule gives $(\bar\partial a)s+(\bar\partial b)u_1=0$. Complex independence forces both to be constant, again contradicting unique continuation. On a further disk the three displayed vectors are pointwise real independent.

An arbitrary smooth real tensor supported on this disk and killing $s$ can therefore prescribe arbitrary smooth compactly supported images of the one or two vectors $u_i$. Choose a smooth complement to their real span together with $s$ to extend the prescription. Testing an adjoint annihilator of the universal derivative with these tensors kills the adjoint on this lift. Indeed its equivariant coefficient has one value in the dual slot for each chosen basis element, and the pairings summed over deck translates on the Galois cover are equal by equivariance and the invariant inner product. Variation of the section in the invariant domain gives the homogeneous adjoint equation, since projecting any test to its invariant part preserves its pairing with the equivariant adjoint. Unique continuation kills the adjoint everywhere on the connected Galois cover. The cokernel of the section derivative is finite dimensional, so the universal derivative is onto. Projectivizing preserves transversality at a zero by real homogeneity. For a cyclic plane this argument uses two real basis elements even though its commuting algebra is complex.

Apply Sard–Smale to this universal equation on each smooth stratum. After fixing a smooth identification of the Galois covers locally, the branch parameters occur in a smooth family of elliptic operators, using sufficiently high finite differentiability for the perturbations. The projective projection has index (3), so a generic perturbation has no solution in the indicated representation on that stratum. To pass to the smooth topology, exhaust each stratum away from the excluded lower double (where relevant) by countably many compact subsets of its charts. Absence of a kernel on a compact subset is open by uniform elliptic stabilization. It is dense by the finite-differentiability result at arbitrarily high regularity and smooth approximation within the bundle of allowed tensors. For a fixed number of branch values and a fixed finite group there are countably many charts and monodromy data; taking their countable intersection also permits all numbers of branch values at once. This gives the claimed residual set. The argument is local on the smooth strata and does not assert uniform estimates over a sequence of unbounded genera.

## Constituents and pullback through the prescribed double

Let $G$ be the monodromy of a smooth connected $d$-sheeted map, acting faithfully on its sheets, and $H$ a sheet stabilizer. Pullback identifies a downstairs homogeneous solution with an $H$-invariant solution on the Galois closure. For the converse, invariance gives descent off the ramification points of the map to $X$; boundedness and elliptic removability extend the descended solution. By finite group reciprocity the only possible irreducibles contributing to these $H$-fixed vectors are the constituents of $\mathbb R[G/H]$.

For an imprimitive quartic the transitive group preserves a partition into two pairs and embeds in the symmetries of a square. Its order is divisible by four and divides eight. At order four its action is regular and it is cyclic or Klein four. At order eight it is the dihedral group of order eight acting on the four vertices. The constituents are as follows, with $\mathbf1$ the trivial summand.

| Monodromy | Permutation representation |
|---|---|
| $C_4$ | $\mathbf1$, its unique nontrivial sign, and the faithful rotation plane |
| $C_2\times C_2$ | $\mathbf1$ and the three nontrivial signs |
| $D_4$ of order eight | $\mathbf1$, the sign of the exchange of the two blocks, and the faithful plane |

For the dihedral row, functions constant on each block give the first two summands. The differences within each block span the plane, on which the independent sign changes and interchange of the two coordinates act faithfully and irreducibly. The trivial part has zero kernel by the base hypothesis. Each nontrivial sign in the table descends to its own quadratic quotient, so it is tested on the family of that quotient using only the branch values at which its sign is negative. Formula (2) gives index $-2r$ there. Projectivization thus gives index $-1$ whatever the number of branch values, including zero. The disk test excludes all of these kernels except on the specified double $f$. In the faithful plane the full quartic branch locus is visible; (2) charges a reflection one complex parameter and a nonidentity rotation two. Thus (3) applies even when ramification over a given point is not simple. In degree three the transitive groups are $C_3$ and $S_3$. Their permutation representations both consist of $\mathbf1$ and a faithful irreducible real plane, so the same argument proves the cubic assertion.

A sign from $f$ is a summand with $H$-fixed vectors precisely when $H$ is contained in its kernel in the monodromy quotient. This is exactly the criterion that the map from $X$ lift through $Y$ over $C$, by applying the covering criterion off the union of the branch divisors and extending over the punctures. When this occurs, its solution line is the descended pullback of $\xi$ and all other summands have zero kernel. This proves (1).

For a connected nodal stable map of total degree four whose positive normalizations have degrees at most three or are imprimitive quartics, restrictions to the positive normalizations are now determined by (1) and the cubic assertion. Each nonzero positive section is a real multiple of the pullback of $\xi$. On a contracted normalization the section is constant, and the remaining equations are equality of the values at the two flags of each node. Thus these maps have precisely the positive images considered in [Calculation E2](../polar/first-quartic-contribution-at-a-ramified-double-wall.md) whenever they carry a nonzero section. A primitive quartic normalization has monodromy $A_4$ or $S_4$ and its nontrivial constituent has real dimension three; the preceding disk test treats dimensions one and two.
