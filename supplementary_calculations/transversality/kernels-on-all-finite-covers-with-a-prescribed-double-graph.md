# G3. Kernels on finite covers with one prescribed double graph

Let $C$ be a closed Riemann surface of genus $g\ge2$, $N\to C$ a complex rank-two bundle of degree $2g-2$, and $D$ a smooth real Cauchy–Riemann operator with its usual complex symbol. Fix a connected double $f:Y\to C$ and an odd section $\xi\ne0$ such that $\ker f^*D=\mathbb R\xi$, $\ker D=0$. Suppose the lower branch and time derivatives give a transverse crossing for a chosen path. For an unramified double the transverse derivative consists of the time column alone. Let $V\Subset C$ be a disk of regular values with nonzero lower sheet height on its closure. On this disk choose one sheet and use $\xi$ also for its section of $N$. The opposite sheet has height $-\xi$. Perturb $D$ by smooth real zeroth-order tensors supported in a fixed compact subset of $V$ with nonempty interior, annihilating $\xi$ there. Work in a small open neighborhood where the base invertibility, simple prescribed kernel and transverse crossing persist. A cutoff in time incorporates the perturbation in the original path.

**Theorem.** For a residual set of these perturbations, simultaneously for every smooth connected finite cover $a:X\to C$ of arbitrary degree and genus,
$$
 \ker a^*D=
 \begin{cases}
     \mathbb R\,p^*\xi & \text{if }a=fp\text{ for a holomorphic }p:X\to Y,\\
     0 & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                               \tag{1}
$$
We denote the perturbed operator also by $D$. The two choices of a lift $p$ in the first line exchange the sign of its generator. The argument applies to both ramified and unramified doubles. The assertion is residual at the prescribed graph. It gives no single open interval of neighboring operators that works in all degrees.

The local input is Wendl's infinite-jet Petri theorem in the formulation of Doan and Walpuski. We explain how to use unrestricted jets when allowed variations annihilate a fixed nonzero solution. The argument below works on any test subdisk on which that solution is nonzero. [Doan–Walpuski, Definition 1.6.3 and Theorems 1.6.17, 2.5.3](https://walpu.ski/Research/EquivariantBrillNoetherSuperRigidity.pdf) · [Wendl, Corollary 5.10](https://www2.mathematik.hu-berlin.de/~wendl/pub/superrigidity14_final.pdf).

## A Petri jet in the constrained space

For the real operator on the regular disk the infinite-jet condition at $x$ says that multiplication of formal solution and formal adjoint-solution jets is injective on their algebraic real tensor product. The adjoint uses the dual bundle with density values; thus the condition is invariant under smooth complex bundle gauge, using the corresponding output gauge and the dual change of variables for the adjoint. In the space of *unrestricted* smooth zeroth-order perturbations of a fixed Cauchy–Riemann symbol, operators with this condition at a chosen $x$ are dense. Indeed localized smooth perturbations realize every finite jet, and that symbol satisfies Wendl's condition. One can use the smooth-coefficient Banach parameter neighborhoods in the cited theorem inside any prescribed finite set of smooth-norm tolerances.

Fix $x$ in the interior of the support region and a smaller disk about $x$. Choose an unrestricted nearby operator $D'$ with a Petri jet there. It has a local solution $s'$ close to $\xi$ on a still smaller disk. In a complex trivialization write $D'=\bar\partial+M'$, with $M'$ real linear. On a sufficiently small disk the Cauchy transform composed with multiplication by $M'$ has sup operator norm less than one, uniformly for $D'$ near $D$. Solving $D'(s'-\xi)=-D'\xi$ by this inverse gives the sup estimate, and interior elliptic estimates give arbitrary prescribed finite-order closeness on a smaller disk by taking $D'$ sufficiently close. The solution is smooth. Choose a complex-linear covector field $l$ with $l(\xi)=1$. Then $G=1+(s'-\xi)\otimes l$ is a smooth complex gauge close to the identity sending $\xi$ to $s'$. The operator $G^{-1}D'G$ has the original symbol, the required Petri jet at $x$, and annihilates $\xi$. Its difference from $D$ now annihilates $\xi$ pointwise. Multiply that difference by a cutoff supported in the construction disk and equal to one near $x$. This yields the asserted density of a good jet at this point by allowed smooth perturbations. Only this density is used.

## A constrained adjoint test

Choose an irreducible real orthogonal finite-monodromy local system $W$ on the complement of a finite set of points of $C$. At the punctures use the orbifold extensions of orders equal to the orders of the respective monodromies. Omit every point where that monodromy is trivial. The corresponding twisted operator is the equivariant operator on a finite smooth normal cover, or equivalently the operator on orbifold sections. Assume $W$ differs from the sign system of $f$ after passing to the common punctured curve. A connected compact normal cover dominating the two systems is obtained from the kernel of their joint finite representation and normalization across the punctures. The trivial system is harmless by base invertibility.

Let $s\ne0$ be an orbifold solution of $D^W$. In a real orthonormal flat frame over a regular test disk write $s=(s_1,\ldots,s_r)$, where $r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}W$. On the common normal cover these components are the images of a basis under a nonzero equivariant map from $W^*$ to the real solution space. Its kernel is an invariant subspace, so it is injective. The prescribed lower solution transforms by a different irreducible character. Hence $s_1,\ldots,s_r,\xi$ are real linearly independent on the common cover. Any real linear relation on their infinite jets at a chosen regular point is a solution vanishing to infinite order, hence vanishes everywhere by strong unique continuation. Their jets there are real linearly independent.

Suppose an adjoint $\eta$ annihilates the derivative of the kernel equation on nonzero sections under all allowed operator variations and all section variations. The latter imply $(D^W)^\dagger\eta=0$. On a test disk disjoint from the punctures $\eta$ is smooth with components $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_r$. Testing against localized real tensors $K$ with $K\xi=0$ gives the pointwise real tensor identity
$$
            \sum_{i=1}^r s_i\otimes\eta_i=\xi\otimes\alpha
$$
for some smooth dual output density $\alpha$. This is the annihilator of the subspace of maps vanishing on $\xi$. The right-hand factor is itself a local adjoint solution. In fact let $\Phi$ be any smooth compactly supported complex-linear endomorphism of $N$ on this disk. Its action on the output also acts trivially on the cotangent factor. Its commutator with $D$ is zeroth order by complex linearity of the symbol. Pair the *identity* with this commutator. On the left,
$$
      \sum_i\int\langle\eta_i,[D,\Phi]s_i\rangle
        =\sum_i\int\langle\eta_i,D(\Phi s_i)\rangle=0.
$$
The right side gives $\int\langle\alpha,D(\Phi\xi)\rangle=0$. The fields $\Phi\xi$ include every compactly supported smooth section of $N$ on the disk, by the same complex covector $l$ used above. Thus $D^\dagger\alpha=0$ there. The commutator need not be an allowed perturbation; it tests a tensor identity already established using the allowed ones.

At a Petri jet in this disk the last pointwise relation induces a zero element in the tensor product of the formal solution and adjoint solution spaces. The independence of the input jets implies that each $\eta_i$ has zero infinite jet. Adjoint strong unique continuation on the connected normal cover gives $\eta=0$. This proves surjectivity of the universal equation at such an operator and nonzero solution. Equivariance is accounted for by contraction in the flat frame. The proof uses real projectivization, which suffices also for irreducibles of complex or quaternionic type.

## Moving the effective branch points

Consider a marked chart of systems of this fixed finite representation type with exactly $s_0$ effective punctures. Their distinct positions are its only continuous parameters, of real dimension $2s_0$. Over a small simply connected configuration chart the finite normal covers form a smooth family, by the prescribed monodromy and the local maps $z\mapsto z^e$. For local monodromy $I_x$ put $c_x=r-\dim_{\mathbb R}W^{I_x}$. The real Fredholm index is
$$
                         \operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D^W=-2\sum_x c_x.
$$
For example, deform to a complex-linear reference. In a local inertia eigenframe of $W_{\mathbb C}$ of order $e$ the exponents of the invariant direct image pair as $k,e-k$ on the nonreal eigenvalues and equal $e/2$ for $-1$. Their sum is $e c_x/2$. Pullback of this direct image on the normal cover therefore has total degree loss $|G|c_x/2$ over $x$, where $G$ is its finite monodromy group. The smooth direct image of invariant sections at the Dolbeault reference has degree $-\sum c_x/2$ in the flat factor. Tensor with the index-zero rank-two bundle on $C$ and apply Riemann–Roch. This gives the displayed real index, including the unramified case. It is also [Doan–Walpuski, Proposition 2.8.6](https://walpu.ski/Research/EquivariantBrillNoetherSuperRigidity.pdf).

Including the branch positions and removing the real scalar gives projection index
$$
                 2s_0-1-2\sum_x c_x\le-1,                                    \tag{2}
$$
since every retained monodromy acts nontrivially. Punctures visible to a larger cover but invisible to this representation play no role. Projectivize locally by a normalization transverse to the kernel scaling; the discarded radial direction maps to zero in the linearized equation.

Here are the topology details connecting the jet test with (2). Use countably many relatively compact subcharts in the configuration spaces and finite monodromy data, with compact exhaustions. Distinct finite groups and their real irreducibles, and homomorphisms from each finitely generated punctured surface group to a finite group, constitute countably many choices up to equivalence. For systems that could equal the character of $f$, remove the data actually equal to that system before taking the compact exhaustions. The equality condition there fixes its branch positions and discrete monodromy, hence is closed in its fixed smooth chart. Refine the charts so that each compact block of branch configurations misses a test subdisk in the interior of the chosen variation support. This is possible locally at every configuration of finitely many points.

For each block absence of a kernel is open by compactness and elliptic estimates on the smooth family of normal covers. To prove density, begin at any operator in the small constrained neighborhood and insert a good Petri jet at an $x$ on that block's test disk, as above. At this smooth operator the normalized solutions over the compact block form a compact set; one may use unit $L^2$ normalization in smooth local bundle identifications. All universal kernel equations there are surjective by the adjoint test. Finitely many smooth allowed tensor directions suffice on neighborhoods covering this compact set, by finite-dimensional cokernels and openness of surjectivity. Nearby zeros over the block stay in those neighborhoods by the same compactness. With this finite-dimensional operator parameter space, the Fredholm implicit function theorem and (2) give local solution manifolds of dimension at most the operator parameter dimension minus one. A generic arbitrarily small parameter misses their projections. Take open chart neighborhoods when applying Sard and then restrict to the compact block. Thus absence is dense for this block even though the individual infinite-jet condition need not be open. Intersect the countably many open dense sets in the smooth constrained parameter space, a Baire space with the usual smooth seminorms. The prescribed kernel line persists by construction and upper semicontinuity on its fixed cover.

## Descent and matching

Given any connected finite $a:X\to C$, pass to a connected normal cover dominating $a$ and $f$ and write $G$ for its deck group and $H$ for a sheet stabilizer of $X$. Pullback identifies the kernel on $X$ with the $H$-invariants in the normal-cover kernel. Conversely a bounded invariant solution descends on the punctured quotient and across its finite branch set by removability for the pulled-back equation. Each irreducible in the real normal-cover kernel defines a twisted solution as above. Removing an ineffective inertia point preserves all such solutions by descent through the corresponding kernel subgroup and removability. Hence the only possible representation is the sign character of $f$. Its multiplicity is exactly one: all vectors in its isotypic part are invariant under the kernel of the sign character and descend to $Y$, where the kernel is the prescribed line. Its $H$-invariant space is the line exactly when $H$ lies in that kernel subgroup, equivalently exactly when $a$ lifts to $Y$. This proves (1).

For a nodal stable map to $C$ apply (1) on each positive normalization. Nonliftable components carry zero height and liftable ones carry a real multiple of the lifted section. On every connected contracted block the operator is the ordinary Dolbeault operator on a trivial vector bundle, and matching makes the height constant across the block. The kernel consists precisely of assignments of these heights agreeing at the nodes, including two flags on the same normalization. Thus at this central operator every nonzero positive image lies on a real dilation of the original graph. Distinct dilations can meet over lower ramification points. Their incidence and localized Euler weights still require a gluing calculation in each degree and genus under study.
