# G2. Kernels on primitive quartic covers at a prescribed double wall

Let $C$ be a closed Riemann surface of genus $g\ge 2$, and let $N$ be a complex plane bundle of degree $2g-2$. Let $D$ be a real Cauchy–Riemann operator on $N$, invertible on $C$. Fix a connected branched double $f:Y\to C$ with $\ker f^*D=\mathbb R\xi$ odd. Suppose there is a disk $U$ of regular values on which one height $s$ is nonzero and $Bs\notin\mathbb C s$, in a coordinate trivializing the form factor of the antilinear term $B$ of $D$. At a simple ramification point with $w=c+z^2$, $\xi=zv+O(|z|^2)$ and $B(c)v\notin\mathbb Cv$, every sufficiently close regular value has this property. Take smooth real zeroth-order variations supported in $\overline U$ which annihilate $s$ pointwise. They also annihilate $-s$ on the other sheet. Work in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $D$ so that the disk condition persists on a fixed smaller disk, the base remains invertible, and the lower kernel stays simple. If the given double wall is transverse in a path, these variations may be inserted with a time cutoff while preserving transversality.

**Proposition.** For a residual set of these variations the pullback operator on every smooth connected primitive quartic cover of $C$ is injective, simultaneously in all source genera. Consequently on any smooth connected cover $u:X\to C$ of degree at most four the kernel is zero unless $u=fp$. In that case it is the line $\mathbb R p^*\xi$.

The second assertion also follows by adjoining the imprimitive, cubic and double cases of [Calculation G1](imprimitive-quartic-kernels-at-a-double-wall.md). We prove the dimension-three assertion directly, including the test on adjoints which is needed when the three quartic heights and the prescribed lower height are pointwise real dependent.

**Twisted equation.** Let $V$ be an irreducible orthogonal real representation of finite monodromy on a punctured $C$, of dimension $k\le3$. Fill in the punctures with trivial monodromy. The normalized connected Galois cover of the remaining punctured curve with group the monodromy image has a compactification $\pi:Z\to C$ by the local power maps. On $Z$, apply $\pi^*D$ to $G$-equivariant $V$-valued sections of $\pi^*N$. Equivalently, use the twist $D_V$ on the orbifold $C_\nu$ whose isotropy orders are the orders of the local monodromies. On a regular disk the equation in a parallel orthonormal frame of $V$ is $D$ on each coefficient. In particular a nonzero kernel gives a $G$-equivariant map from $V$ to the space of solutions on the connected regular cover, injective by irreducibility.

For an actual finite cover, take its permutation local system on the complement of its branch values. The orbifold pushforward of the trivial real local system splits orthogonally into its irreducible summands, and the twisted operator splits accordingly. Its kernel identifies with the ordinary pullback kernel on the smooth source. Indeed the lifts of solutions satisfy the equation in the power charts. In the reverse direction invariant solutions descend away from the branch locus, are bounded there, and extend on the coarse source by removal of singularities for its ordinary smooth real Cauchy–Riemann operator. This argument concerns solutions. The adjoints below are taken in the invariant complex on $Z$ with its smooth invariant densities; all coefficient pairings are made away from the branch locus. [Doan–Walpuski, proof of Proposition 2.8.5](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2022.104).

Let $\chi$ be the lower sign local system. To compare $V$ and $\chi$, remove the union of their branch loci and use the connected regular cover of this complement for the intersection of the two monodromy kernels. Restriction to this cover means pulling both solutions back to the same equation; the deck group maps onto the monodromy group of each system. Suppose $V$ and $\chi$ are inequivalent representations of this common deck group. If $t_1,\ldots,t_k$ are the coefficients of a nonzero $D_V$-solution in a parallel orthonormal basis, the $k+1$ sections $s,t_1,\ldots,t_k$ are linearly independent **over the real constants on each test disk**. Indeed the equivariant maps from $\chi$ and from $V$ into the solution space are injective and have disjoint images, and a constant relation on a disk extends throughout the connected regular cover by unique continuation. This applies also when ramification values of the two systems coincide.

**Testing the two sides of the tensor.** Choose a disk inside the allowed support, disjoint from the two branch loci, on which $Bs\notin\mathbb Cs$. If $t_1=a s$ everywhere there with $a$ complex, applying $D$ modulo $\mathbb C s$ gives $(\bar a-a)[Bs]=0$. Hence $a$ is real and $(\bar\partial a)s=0$, contrary to independence over the constants. Thus there is a subdisk on which $s,t_1$ are independent over $\mathbb C$. For $k\ge2$, if $t_2=a s+b t_1$ throughout this disk with real $a,b$, the coefficients are smooth and the equation gives $(\bar\partial a)s+(\bar\partial b)t_1=0$. Both would be constant. On a further subdisk $s,t_1,t_2$ are therefore independent over $\mathbb R$. With $P:N_{\mathbb R}\to N_{\mathbb R}/\mathbb Rs$ and $T:V\to N_{\mathbb R}$ the coefficient evaluation $e_j\mapsto t_j$, we have obtained
$$
                    \operatorname{rank}(PT)\ \ge\ \min(k,2)
$$
on a nonempty open disk.

At a solution consider a covector annihilating the section and allowed operator derivatives of $D_Vt=0$. The section derivative gives the homogeneous adjoint equation. Projectivizing the kernel line makes no difference to this test, since the omitted vector is already in the kernel. An invariant annihilator on $Z$ satisfies the ordinary adjoint equation there, by averaging arbitrary test sections. Write its coefficients on the disk as $p_j$ in the real dual of the form fiber $F=\Lambda^{0,1}T^*C\otimes_{\mathbb C}N$, using one common positive base density. In this description the coefficients solve the same local dual equation. Tracing the pairings on all sheets gives a common positive factor times
$$
          \int \sum_{j=1}^k p_j(A t_j)
$$
for each allowed operator variation $A$. Indeed on any other lift the two coefficient arrays are transformed by the same orthogonal matrix, which leaves the contraction invariant. Expressing all dual densities relative to the lifted base density on this disk accounts for its coordinate Jacobian. The available $A$ are precisely the compactly supported smooth maps factoring through $P$. If $Q:V\to F^*_{\mathbb R}$ sends $e_j$ to $p_j$, pointwise tensor duality gives
$$
                (PT)Q^\top=0,\qquad
                Q^\top:F_{\mathbb R}\longrightarrow V.
$$
For $k\le2$ this forces $Q=0$. For $k=3$ it gives $\operatorname{rank}Q\le1$ on the disk. A nonzero adjoint would give an injective equivariant map from $V$ to the dual solutions on the connected regular cover, again by irreducibility. On a subdisk where $p_1\ne0$ the rank bound would then give $p_2=a p_1$ for a smooth real function $a$. The adjoint product rule gives $\sigma_{D^\dagger}(da)p_1=0$; ellipticity forces $da=0$. Unique continuation contradicts the independence of the two adjoint solutions. Thus in all cases the annihilator is zero. The section derivative has closed image of finite codimension, so the universal derivative is onto. This uses neither pointwise independence of all four heights nor regularity of any adjoint divided by a base differential at a branch value.

**Dimension and genericity.** Let $r$ be the number of distinct branch values of the faithful monodromy cover of $V$, and let $I_x$ denote the inertia group at $x$. The real index of the twisted equation is
$$
          \operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_V
          =-2\sum_x\bigl(k-\dim_{\mathbb R}V^{I_x}\bigr).
$$
Here $\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D=0$ and $\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb C}N=2$. This is the orbifold index formula, also computed from the invariant direct image in Calculation G1. The faithfulness convention ensures that every displayed summand is at least one. Ordering and moving the $r$ distinct values gives $r$ complex parameters in the fixed monodromy stratum. Projectivization by real scaling gives incidence index at most $2r-2r-1=-1$ over the allowed perturbations. Values of a larger cover where $V$ has trivial inertia are absent from this count; its twisted kernel descends across those values. [Doan–Walpuski, Propositions 2.8.5–2.8.6](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2022.104).

In each relatively compact smooth Hurwitz chart use normalized solutions before dividing by sign. Over a compact subset of the chart their set at a fixed operator is compact by the uniform elliptic estimates on the smooth family of compact Galois covers. At each such solution the surjectivity just proved uses finitely many smooth allowed tensors in addition to the section variables. Finitely many such neighborhoods suffice, and surjectivity persists there. Elliptic compactness puts all solutions over the compact subset for nearby operators in these neighborhoods. Ordinary parametric transversality in the resulting finite-dimensional family gives density of operators without kernels over this compact subset, and compactness gives openness. Compact chart exhaustions, ramification types and finite monodromy representations form a countable collection up to equivalence. Its Baire intersection proves the assertion for all $V$ inequivalent to $\chi$ of dimension at most three. In passing to the faithful monodromy chart, equivalence to $\chi$ is a closed condition (its branch divisor must equal that of $f$, and the remaining monodromy is discrete). The complement is exhausted by compact subsets as above.

For a transitive quartic cover the permutation space is $\mathbb R\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf1^\perp$ with $\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1^\perp=3$. For primitive monodromy the group is $A_4$ or $S_4$ and $\mathbf1^\perp$ is irreducible: $A_4$ has no nontrivial real sign character, the sum-zero space has no fixed vector, and an invariant proper subspace would give an invariant line by orthogonality. The base summand is invertible. This proves primitive injectivity. For every transitive permutation representation of degree at most four all the remaining irreducibles likewise have dimension at most three. The lower character occurs exactly when the sheet stabilizer on the common punctured base lies in $\ker\chi$, and then with multiplicity one: a sign-equivariant function on the sheets is determined by one value and exists precisely under this stabilizer condition. The resulting covering map to the lower double extends across the punctures by the local power models. The $\chi$-kernel is then the given odd line. This proves the proposition.

**Paths.** In the space of unrestricted fixed-symbol paths relative to regular endpoints, a generic path has no simultaneous simply ramified double wall with the disk condition above and a nonzero kernel on a twist $V\not\simeq\chi$ of real rank at most three. Indeed, use two separate Hurwitz and section variables and a common variation of $D$. On the joint incidence, testing first with variations killing the current $s$ forces the second adjoint to vanish by the disk argument. Then arbitrary tensors on a regular disk test the lower odd adjoint, whose pairing with $As$ vanishes for every $A$ only if that adjoint vanishes. The two equations are therefore jointly regular. The lower projectivized incidence has index $-1$ over operators at a simply ramified double, and the other incidence has index at most $-1$. Adding the common time still leaves negative index. Countable relative transversality gives the stated avoidance. For a prescribed central crossing one can instead make the graph-preserving perturbation in the proposition.

There is also a restriction on smooth primitive quartic crossings along an unrestricted generic path at times where the base operator is invertible. In the preceding tensor test one may now take $P=1$. Two coefficients of a nonzero standard solution are real independent on a subdisk: real proportionality on a disk where the first is nonzero would force a constant ratio. Thus $\operatorname{rank}T\ge2$ there and the same adjoint argument proves universal regularity. Write $\rho_x$ for the permutation around a distinct branch value $x$ and $d_x=4-\#\mathrm{cycles}(\rho_x)$, counting fixed letters as cycles. Its fixed space on the standard summand has codimension $d_x$. The projectivized incidence along the path has index $-2\sum_x(d_x-1)$. Hence every such crossing has only simple branching at distinct values, or is unramified, and each kernel is a real line since the regular incidence is zero-dimensional. With positive branching the primitive group must be $S_4$. These conclusions concern smooth finite covers. A compactified moduli space also includes nodal maps, for which the normalization kernels must satisfy the separate matching conditions.
