# F6. Regularity of the finite double covers of the embedded graph

Let $f:Y\to C$ be the odd double wall of [Calculation F3](the-two-endpoint-degrees-at-a-ramified-double-wall.md), with two simple branch points $r_1,r_2$ on $Y$, lower involution $\tau$, and $h=g(Y)=2g(C)$. Write $\gamma=(f,\xi)$, $A=D^N_\gamma$, $V=N^\gamma$ and $\sigma=[(0,\xi)]$. Assume both projected jets in Calculation F3 are nonzero. Let $\mathcal H_Y$ be the compact stack of connected finite doubles $p:X\to Y$ in genus $2h$, including its split and connected nodal normalizations. A double with two distinct branch values is smooth. On the diagonal its normalization is unramified and the two points over the collision value are identified. These are the square-root charts of [Calculation E4](../polar/the-marked-double-cover-surface-at-a-quartic-join.md), with target $Y$. Write $A_p^-$ for the upper-deck odd part of $p^*A$.

**Proposition.** After an arbitrarily small generic smooth fixed-symbol perturbation of the lower operator preserving $f,\xi$ and the transverse crossing, $\ker A_p^-=0$ over $\mathcal H_Y$ except at the two split nodal doubles joined at $r_1,r_2$. Each exceptional odd kernel is the line given on the two normalizations by $(\sigma,-\sigma)$.

**Allowed variations.** Fiber negation induces the lower symmetry on $V$ over $\tau$, preserving $A$ and the radial section. On a disk of regular lower values choose one graph sheet. There the vertical identification gives the ordinary lower equation and identifies $\sigma$ with $\xi$. On a sufficiently small disk near a lower ramification, the projected jet gives $B\sigma\notin\mathbb C\sigma$, as in [Calculation E1](../polar/the-normal-twist-at-a-ramified-quartic-tower.md). Use arbitrary smooth real zeroth-order variations supported in this disk that kill $\sigma$ pointwise. They kill the opposite sheet height as well. They preserve the graph, both ramification jets, and, after shrinking, invertibility on the base and transversality of the lower crossing. In the vertical identification their normal variation is exactly the selected tensor, with the value on the other sheet transported by the symmetry. This follows by differentiating a graph at fixed regular projection. One can choose a compact support region with nonempty interior and do the tests on smaller subdisks in its interior. A cutoff in the time of the original lower path incorporates the perturbation. In a small neighborhood of these choices $B\sigma\notin\mathbb C\sigma$ still holds on the test disk (using the antilinear part of the current normal equation).

**Smooth doubles without a lifted involution.** Delete the branch divisor of $p$ and its $\tau$-translate, and use the finite common cover trivializing the two sign local systems. Write $\chi$ for the sign character of $p$ on this punctured curve. If $\chi\ne\tau^*\chi$, an odd solution $u\ne0$, its lower-symmetry translate $u'$ and $\sigma$ give three globally independent real solutions there. Their characters are $\chi,\tau^*\chi,1$. Moving branch divisors are included. Distinct divisors give distinct characters by monodromy around their points of difference.

A subdisk can be chosen disjoint from those finitely many branch values and their translates. If $u=a\sigma$ there with complex $a$, the equation modulo $\mathbb C\sigma$ gives $(\bar a-a)[B\sigma]=0$. Then $a$ is real and holomorphic, hence constant, contrary to continuation of the two characters. Thus $u,\sigma$ are complex independent on a smaller disk. If $u'$ belonged everywhere there to their real span, its two real coefficients would satisfy the Cauchy–Riemann equation by that complex independence. Their constancy gives the same contradiction. On a further subdisk $\sigma,u,u'$ are real independent. Tensors killing $\sigma$ can assign arbitrary independent values to $u$ and $u'$ there. In the universal equation an adjoint annihilator is homogeneous by variation of the section. Pairing with these tensors forces it to vanish on the test sheets, hence everywhere by unique continuation, including across the omitted branch points. All pairings use the upper odd system, so the two points related by the upper deck give equal summands. This proves surjectivity. The argument in the presence of a complex-linear zeroth-order part uses the same product rule.

The real index of the odd operator is $-4$. At an equivariant Dolbeault reference its complex index is the index on $V\otimes L^{-1}$, with $L^2=\mathcal O_Y(D)$ for the branch divisor $D$ of degree two. Thus $\deg(V\otimes L^{-1})=2h-4$. The two freely moving unordered branch points contribute four real parameters and projectivizing a nonzero section removes one, giving projection index $-1$ to the allowed lower perturbations. Generic perturbations therefore have no such solution.

**Doubles admitting a lift of the lower involution.** Equality of the characters gives a lift of $\tau$ on the punctured double, extending uniquely across its punctures once chosen on one sheet. Its branch divisor is invariant. If $D=y+\tau y$ with $y$ nonfixed, the divisor varies in one complex parameter. The lift has square one since it permutes the two points over $r_1$ and its square fixes both. The odd operator splits into two real eigensystems for this lift. Each has index $-2$. Indeed on $V$ the two complex fiber weights at a lower fixed point are $+,-$: the horizontal line is positive, the two ambient vertical lines are negative, and the tangent of the graph is a negative line. Twisting by $L^{-1}$ leaves one of each weight at both fixed points. Deform equivariantly to a Dolbeault operator. Evaluation of the pullback of either eigendirect image on $C$ has length-one cokernel at each of the two fixed points (generators $e_+,z e_-$, or $z e_+,e_-$). Each rank-two direct image has degree $(2h-4-2)/2=2g(C)-3$, giving the asserted real index. In each eigensystem the transported values of both the solution and its adjoint acquire the same sign, so their two test pairings add. Complex independence of $u,\sigma$ on a subdisk follows as above using the upper sign. Allowed variations can prescribe the image of $u$ there. This gives surjectivity in the given eigensystem, and the projective index with the moving orbit is $-2+2-1=-1$.

The only remaining distinct invariant divisor is $r_1+r_2$. If it has a lift, that lift squares to the upper deck: locally a square root of the parameter on $Y$ is multiplied by $i$ or $-i$. On odd solutions the lift $J$ has $J^2=-1$, whereas $\sigma$ is upper-even. The three solutions $\sigma,u,Ju$ are globally real independent. The same real-span argument therefore supplies all paired values and proves surjectivity for the full odd equation. The divisor has no moving parameter, so this projective index is $-5$. If the lift does not exist the earlier character test applies.

Use smooth-domain Hurwitz charts, an $L^s_1$ domain for $s>2$, and a sufficiently differentiable Banach completion for the operator variations. For distinct branch points the family of smooth double covers is a submersion locally in its parameters and the pulled-back equations are differentiable in a smooth identification of the domains. The adjoint tests above are within the allowed perturbation space. Sard–Smale on the indicated strata gives residual avoidance. For the smooth topology exhaust each stratum by countably many compact subsets in its parameter charts. An empty kernel on such a subset is open by continuous Fredholm stabilization and compactness. Density there follows by applying the finite differentiability result at arbitrarily high differentiability and smoothing within the bundle of tensors killing $\sigma$. The intersection of the resulting open dense subsets is residual in the smooth perturbation space. The finite collection of nontrivial unramified twists can simultaneously be made invertible by Calculation E1 using these same variations.

**The nodal doubles.** For a connected normalization the odd kernel injects, by restriction, into the kernel of one of those nontrivial unramified twists. For a split normalization an odd section is $(a\sigma,-a\sigma)$ and matching is equivalent to $a\sigma(y)=0$. The two double zeros of $\sigma$ found in Calculation F3 give exactly the stated exceptions. The nodal odd operators form a continuous stabilized family, either by normalization or the bounded-coefficient finite double model in Calculation F3. On every compact subset disjoint from the two exceptional points injectivity is uniform in the sense that it persists in a neighborhood of that subset. This proves the proposition.

## Uniform isolation away from the two split radial joins

Use the common perturbation of [Calculation F4](separating-the-two-heights-on-a-common-parameter.md) with lower path $D_t=D_0+tK$ and take the signed normalization $I_4=1$. On it the ordinary target equation adds $\delta\chi(|U|^2/k^2)KU$, where $k(w)^2=|\xi_i(w)|^2>0$ on the test disk and $\chi(1)=0$. If $P\subset\mathcal H_Y$ is compact and omits the two exceptional covers, there is a neighborhood of its signed central family and of $(t,\delta)=(0,0)$ in which every normalized solution is a finite double into $\gamma$ at $t=0$. These doubles are solutions for every $\delta$ in this neighborhood. This assertion includes upper nodes with connected or split normalization and upper ramification in the test disk.

**Proof.** Write $J_{t,\delta}$ for the just-described ordinary graph structure. Introduce complex parameter disks $T,\mathcal D$. On the product of a tube of $\gamma$ with $T\times\mathcal D$ use the block-diagonal structure $J_{\operatorname{Re}t,\operatorname{Re}\delta}\oplus i\oplus i$. The submanifold
$$
                    S=\gamma(Y)\times\{0\}\times\mathcal D
$$
is almost complex, since at time zero the graph solves the equation for every displacement. An actual graph at real parameters becomes a holomorphic stable map into the product, constant in both parameter coordinates. For the compact-target formulation one may use the compact symplectic tube extension of Calculation F2, complete both disks to closed complex curves and cut off the small family of target structures outside the parameter charts. Along $t=0$ extend within structures preserving $\gamma$ (equivalently cut off the real displacement parameter before evaluating the prescribed family). The product structure is tamed by the product form after shrinking. These extensions do not affect the local maps.

Suppose a sequence approaching the central family has image outside $S$. By compactness pass to a limit $(\gamma p,0,0)$ with $p\in P$. Apply the normal rescaling of [Zinger, Proposition 3.2, especially (3.7)](https://arxiv.org/html/0807.0805v3#S3.SS2). In a regularized tube of $S$, let $e_n>0$ be the supremum norm of the normal coordinate. The maps with normal coordinate divided by $e_n$ converge, after a subsequence, to the graph of a **nonzero** normal section of the limiting map, with normalization and matching on a nodal source. In particular their images converge to the image of this graph. The time coordinate of this field is a real constant $b$: the time projection vanishes on $S$, so its normal differential on the rescaled limit is the limit of $t_n/e_n$, and each $t_n$ is real. The displacement coordinate is tangent to $S$.

Use the natural splitting of the normal at the center as $V\oplus T_0T$, and denote the first component of the section by $v$. Linearizing the product structure there gives
$$
                       p^*A\,v+b\,p^*\theta=0.
$$
Here $\theta$ is the ordinary inhomogeneous original-time column of the lower graph equation, represented before normal projection by $K\xi$. This follows by differentiating the family $J_{t,0}$ at the map $\gamma$, then composing with $p$; the time-coordinate equation itself is the ordinary Dolbeault equation. All rows are evaluated on the normal quotient, since a tangent variation stays tangent to the almost complex submanifold. The class of $\theta$ generates $\operatorname{coker}A$ by transversality of $W_f$ and the graph deformation sequence. Pullback identifies this line with the invariant upper cokernel, by descent of the invariant stabilized operator, including matching at a node ([Calculation E5](../polar/polar-kernels-on-two-branched-double-covers.md)). The invariant projection forces $b=0$. The proposition now gives $v=a\,p^*\sigma$ for a real constant $a$.

On a neighborhood of the compact support of the area form in the regular test disk put $\phi(w,U)=|U|^2/k(w)^2-1$. Its restriction to $S$ is zero, so its first derivative there depends only on the normal class. On the radial field it equals $2$, computed on $(0,\xi)$. Thus on the rescaled limiting graph its linearization is the constant $2a$ over the entire test support. The quantities $\phi/e_n$ on the corresponding portions of the unscaled maps converge uniformly to $2a$. Indeed a failure produces points on the rescaled images which, after taking a subsequence, converge to a point on the graph of the limiting field over the support. Taylor expansion in the bounded rescaled normal coordinate contradicts the failure. This uses image convergence and does not require the test-disk preimages to be disjoint unramified sheets. But $I_4=1$ gives $\int\phi\,u_n^*\omega=0$ and $\int u_n^*\omega=4$. Positivity gives $a=0$, contrary to the nonzero rescaled section. This excludes any sequence outside $S$.

A map contained in $S$ is at original time zero and factors through $\gamma$. In genus $2h$ the other stable degree-two maps to $Y$ have an unramified normalization with a one-ended contracted genus-one block. That proper locus is disjoint from the proper finite-cover surface, by the description of double collisions. Therefore maps contained in $S$ sufficiently near $P$ are themselves finite doubles. This proves the assertion. The proof uses the normal operator of the entire graph, with time and the norm constraint; it requires no separate kernel bound for the vertical equation at fixed composite $fp$. It is independent of differentiability in a plumbing coordinate and makes no assertion at the two excluded nodes.

## A displacement on the fixed graph

Let $A_\delta$ be a smooth transverse path through the resulting $A$ on the *fixed* graph, and $\eta_A$ its increasing crossing sign. For all sufficiently small $\delta\ne0$ the base $A_\delta$ is invertible and every odd operator on $\mathcal H_Y$ is injective. Indeed the proposition excludes a limiting odd kernel away from the two exceptional points. At those points Calculation F3 gives isolation in the full operator-parameter and projective-kernel family. The finite-stack odd number therefore satisfies
$$
 \left[\epsilon(A_\delta)F_2(A_\delta)\right]_{\delta<0}^{\delta>0}
       =-6\eta_A.
$$
Here $F_2$ is the absolutely oriented full normal Euler integral on the finite-cover surface. The invariant determinant is the base determinant by descent, through a split or connected nodal normalization as well as on a smooth double. Multiplication by its sign converts to the odd orientation. Each exceptional split double has upper stabilizer of order two, already included in the local number $-3\eta_A$. The formula is a difference of chamber numbers and does not determine either separately.

For the actual common perturbation use [Calculation F4](separating-the-two-heights-on-a-common-parameter.md), with its transverse representative $D_t=D_0+tK$, disk, metric, $k(w)^2=|\xi_i(w)|^2$, area form $\omega$ and function $\chi$. The ordinary target equation obtained by setting $I=1$ adds $\delta\chi(|U|^2/k^2)KU$ on the disk. It commutes with fiber negation on both sheets. The value at $\xi$ vanishes at $t=0$, and its radial derivative there is $2\delta\chi'(1)K\xi$. Thus it realizes a transverse path $A_\delta$ on $\gamma$ with $\eta_A=\eta_f$ by the determinant identification of Calculation F4. At each sufficiently small fixed nonzero $\delta$ the ordinary local degree of its **finite double family**, in genus $2h$, is $F_2(A_\delta)$. To see the compact comparison directly, a genus-$2h$ stable double to $Y$ outside the finite family has a connected unramified normalization in genus $2h-1$ with a one-ended contracted genus-one block. This follows from Riemann–Hurwitz, genus additivity and stability. The finite family and the latter families are proper and disjoint. The normal operator is injective on the latter by unramified twist regularity, base invertibility, restriction and matching. It is injective on the entire finite family by the preceding paragraph. Apply [Zinger, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/0807.0805v3) to the embedded graph using the compact symplectic tube extension of Calculation F2, and restrict to the finite component. The finite Hurwitz surface has its ordinary class (including at its smooth diagonal charts). Thus the comparison gives exactly the stated integral on the stack and retains the upper deck weight.

For the normalized displaced equation, first retain the sign of the height and compute on $I=1$ upstairs. Put $I_d(u,s)=d^{-1}\int |s|^2u^*(\omega/k^2)$ for $d=2,4$, so $I=I_4$ in Calculation F4. For any finite double $p$ and section on the lower graph, the unaveraged integral doubles on composition, including ramification in the test disk and the diagonal nodal maps. Therefore $I_4(fp,p^*s)=I_2(f,s)$. At fixed $\delta\ne0$ the moving ordinary lower graph has the exact form $(f,a(t)\xi)$ with $a(t)>0$ near one and
$$
                  t+\delta\chi(a(t)^2)=0.
$$
Indeed this formula solves the equation on the disk, and elsewhere it is a real multiple of the original homogeneous solution. Invertibility of $A_\delta$ gives local uniqueness. On the entire moving finite double family $I_4=a(t)^2$ since $\int_C\omega=1$. The norm constraint singles out $t=0$ and its un-oriented scalar derivative is $-1/(\delta\chi'(1))$.

The ordinary comparison is uniform on a short interval in $t$ at fixed displacement. Identify the moving graphs with the fixed curve by the smooth family of real fiber dilations on a tube. Extend the resulting target structures to a complex parameter disk by dependence on its real coordinate and take the block-diagonal product almost complex structure. A closed map has constant disk coordinate. The submanifold $Y\times\mathrm{disk}$ is almost complex, and the normal operator on a central map is the original fiber normal operator. The local compactness argument of [Zinger, Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/0807.0805v3#S3.SS2) gives containment in the moving graphs near the compact central family. Equivalently, for compact targets in this argument use the tube extension above, extend the small family of structures by a cutoff to be constant outside the parameter disk in a closed complex parameter curve, and take the product symplectic form. The fixed submanifold there is the product with the parameter curve. On a common stabilization interpolate the norm row to $a(t)^2-1$, since they agree on the ordinary zero set, as in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B.

The scalar cut has the same orientation factor in degrees two and four. The norm derivatives in time are identical and the horizontal and normal Fredholm indices passed by this row are even. Calibrate the scalar orientation in degree two: the normalized bare lower crossing has sign $\eta_f$ by transverse continuation, and its ordinary fixed-time degree is $\epsilon(A_\delta)$. This compares the oriented products without assigning the scalar sign from its coordinate derivative alone. The normalized height stack over the finite central family is $\mathcal H_Y$, because the signed height determines the map into the embedded graph. Negating the height acts by $p\mapsto\tau p$ before the projective quotient, including when $\tau p$ and $p$ are isomorphic. The signed-height covering has stack degree two, cancelling the factor two from the projective Euler link. Its upper deck weights remain in $F_2$. The resulting finite tower contribution of the common perturbation at fixed $\delta$ is
$$
                          \eta_f\,\epsilon(A_\delta)F_2(A_\delta).
$$
For increasing $\delta$ its total chamber difference is $-6$. This uses the actual common parameter from Calculation F4 and the absence of any other upper odd events from the proposition. Excision at nonzero displacement gives no identification of either chamber value with a contribution of the original equal-height family alone. The adjacent unequal-height intervals meet it at the split radial zeros. A comparison over the full homotopy must control any additional solutions approaching these attachments. The neighborhood of the isolated finite towers at fixed $\delta$ can shrink with $|\delta|$.
