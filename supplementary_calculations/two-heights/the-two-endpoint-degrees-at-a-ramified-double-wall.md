# F3. The two endpoint degrees at a ramified double wall

Use the transverse embedded odd double graph $f:Y\to C$, $\gamma=(f,\xi)$ and absolute crossing sign $\eta_f$ of [Calculation F1](the-smoothing-at-an-interior-two-copy-height.md). The lower double has two simple branch values. Assume the base operator is invertible and that the vertical derivative $v$ at each ramification point satisfies $B(c)v\notin\mathbb Cv$, in a frame holomorphic for the complex-linear part of the lower operator. At the two ends of each projective interval there are concrete families of double covers. We compute local Euler changes in these families as their own operator parameters vary.

## Zero height

Fix a ramification point $r$, put $c=f(r)$ and $\ell=\mathbb Cv$, and freeze the lower sign and flag. Vary the *upper* operator through $D_0$ along the original increasing path. The local variation at the upper cover $u=f$ in the ordinary surface Euler number $F_2$ of [Calculation E5](../polar/polar-kernels-on-two-branched-double-covers.md) is $\eta_f$. Its local variation in the prescribed-pole Euler number $E(D;c,\ell)$ is also $\eta_f$. In particular the change of the regular upper expression in [Calculation E6](../polar/the-quartic-contribution-at-a-marked-double-cover.md) due to this event is
$$
             \Delta_f\big[-\eta_f(2F_2+E(D;c,\ell))\big]=-3.                 \tag{1}
$$

Indeed, write $P_f$ for the ordinary cokernel and $S$ for the complex plane of permitted simple poles. The selected branch-velocity column of $W_f$ sends $\delta$ to the class of the principal part with coefficient $-\delta v/2$ in the coordinate $w=c+z^2$. This follows by differentiating the lower solution at fixed target off the moving branch and extending the regular remainder, as in Calculation F1. Hence the pole map $S\to P_f$ is injective. The augmented operator has the same one-dimensional kernel, represented by the smooth section with zero residue, and cokernel $P_f/P(S)$. Varying the other branch and time gives the quotient isomorphism of $W_f$. Multiplication by $-1/2$ in a complex line and the removal of two real dimensions preserve the relative crossing sign. Both links have even sphere dimension and both have deck stabilizer of order two, so the ordinary and augmented Euler changes each equal one signed unit. The base determinant line supplies the same absolute sign in both. This proves (1) locally, also on a path where unrelated upper events may occur elsewhere.

## The radial section on the embedded graph

Let $A=D^N_\gamma$ be the index-zero normal operator on $Y$. The fixed-time graph deformation sequence from [Calculation E1](../polar/the-normal-twist-at-a-ramified-quartic-tower.md) has middle row
$$
 0\longrightarrow\ker f^*D_0\longrightarrow\ker A\longrightarrow T_f
 \xrightarrow{W_f|_{T_f}}\operatorname{coker}f^*D_0
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}A\longrightarrow0.
$$
Here $T_f$ is the fixed-target Hurwitz tangent. Since adjoining time gives an isomorphism, $\ker A$ is generated by the radial normal field $\sigma=[(0,\xi)]$ and $\operatorname{coker}A$ is a real line. Over an unramified point vertical projection to normal is an isomorphism, and oddness and embeddedness give $\xi\ne0$ there.

At a ramification write $f=c+z^2$. In the ambient complex coframe $(dw,dU+B(w)U\,d\bar w)$ the tangent generator is $(2z,\xi_z)$. Choose a complex covector $l$ with $l(v)=1$, and write $\pi$ for projection to $\ker l$ along $v$. Oddness and the pullback equation give
$$
 \xi=vz+Kz^3-\tfrac23 B(c)v\,\bar z^3+O(|z|^5).
$$
The smooth Taylor remainder can be differentiated once at the corresponding order. Put $j=l(\xi)/l(\xi_z)$. A representative of the radial section in the smooth complex normal frame with $l$-component zero is $(-2zj,\xi-j\xi_z)$. Its horizontal entry is $-2z^2+O(|z|^4)$. The antiholomorphic cubic coefficient of its vertical entry is $-\tfrac23\pi B(c)v$. Thus $\sigma=Hz^2+O(|z|^3)$ with $H=(-2,0)$. Writing $A=\bar\partial+L_A+B_A$ in this frame, the $\bar z^2$ coefficient of $A\sigma$ gives
$$
         [B_A(r)H]=2[\pi B(c)v]\ne0
              \quad\text{in } N^\gamma_r/\mathbb CH.
$$
A change to a frame holomorphic for the complex-linear part preserves this nonvanishing. This proves that the radial field has precisely two zeros, each of order two and with the indicated nonzero projected antilinear term.

## An odd Euler change at a double zero

The following local calculation applies to any smooth fixed-symbol path $A_t$ of real Cauchy–Riemann operators on a complex rank-two bundle of degree $2h-2$ over a fixed curve of genus $h$. Assume $A_0=A$ has one-dimensional kernel and cokernel, with normalized generator $\sigma$, and that increasing time crosses its kernel transversely. Use the determinant convention of Calculation E5 to denote by $\eta_A$ the sign of the isomorphism from time to $\operatorname{Hom}(\ker A,\operatorname{coker}A)$. At a point with coordinate $w=0$ assume, in a frame holomorphic for the complex-linear part of $A$,
$$
       \sigma(w)=Hw^2+O(|w|^3),\qquad B(0)H\notin\mathbb CH,\qquad
       A=\bar\partial+B(w)\,d\bar w.
$$
Here and below $B$ acts antilinearly. Consider the **odd** Fredholm family on the compact surface of two-branch doubles of this fixed target, through its split nodal double joined at $w=0$. The central kernel is isolated in the local parameter–kernel incidence, and its contribution to the odd Euler change is $-3\eta_A$. For an invertible base at a regular endpoint this odd Euler number is $\epsilon(A_t)F_2(A_t)$. A path of operators on the fixed normal bundle of $\gamma$ with the required transverse derivative exists by pairing a regular-disk zeroth-order perturbation against the radial section and a cokernel generator. This path is an independent choice of parameter.

On the gerbe chart of [Calculation E4](../polar/the-marked-double-cover-surface-at-a-quartic-join.md) write the local cover as
$$
          d^2=(w-p)^2-\lambda.
$$
Outside the collision disk it is the union of two labeled copies of the punctured target. The odd coefficient in the disk is the section after division by $d$. Its homogeneous equation at time zero has zeroth order term $(\bar d/d)B(w)$, with the value at the finite set of zeros immaterial. Use a fixed smooth trivialization on the complement and the holomorphic transition $d$ on the overlap, where a branch is chosen by $d\sim w-p$. The coefficients are uniformly bounded and converge in every finite $L^s$ when the parameters converge. In a fixed smooth bundle identification over the chart the collar transition contributes only smooth bounded zeroth-order terms. This gives continuity $W^{1,s}\to L^s$, $s>2$. This model computes the true finite-cover family. For homogeneous equations near the branch disks a smooth odd solution upstairs vanishes at a fixed point and division by its branch coordinate is bounded. At the split node it is a smooth section on one normalization vanishing at the join, hence division by $w$ is bounded. The divided equation and removability give $W^{1,s}$ in either case. Conversely multiplication by $d$ recovers a solution extending smoothly on each normalization and satisfying matching there. The same argument applies to thick stabilizing forms supported off the special values. Such forms span the nodal cokernel by the normalization sequence and unique continuation (the cutoff of a local solution realizes the matching class). Common stabilizations and the complex orientation at a Dolbeault reference identify the oriented odd reductions.

At the center the odd kernel is generated by the two opposite copies of $\sigma$. Its cokernel fits into the oriented sequence
$$
       0\longrightarrow N_0\longrightarrow
          \operatorname{coker}A_{\rm odd,0}\longrightarrow
          \operatorname{coker}A\longrightarrow0.
$$
Here $N_0$ is the matching fiber of complex rank two. Fix a kernel normalization test and complementary forms on the thick complement. For a trial section with this normalization, pair its residual with the annihilator of the range on sections of zero normalization. These five functionals give the exact reduced obstruction up to an invertible target frame, since subtracting any normalized solution of the supplemented equation changes the residual by a section in that range. They can be chosen to converge uniformly on the collision disk. Indeed normalization and the supplements give continuous finite-dimensional projectors by the operator-norm convergence above. On the disk the annihilators solve an elliptic adjoint with uniformly bounded coefficient. Starting in $L^{s/(s-1)}$, interior elliptic estimates followed by Sobolev embedding bootstrap them to bounded $W^{1,s}$ on a smaller disk. Compact embedding and uniqueness of the normalized limit give the asserted uniform convergence. In particular their central evaluations detect the matching class of a point mass in the odd coefficient. One can see the identification by cutting off a principal term $D(w)e/w$, where $D(0)=1$ is a real frame of local $A$-solutions acting on $e$. Multiplication by $d=\pm w$ on the normalization gives opposite boundary values $\pm e$. The identity $\bar\partial(1/w)=\pi\delta_0$ identifies this point mass with the matching fiber up to a common nonzero real scalar. Both complex planes in the fiber carry their usual complex orientation.

Set $\Delta=p^2+\lambda/2$ and $\mu=\max(|p|,\sqrt{|\lambda|})$. For $w\ne0$ let $U(w)=\sigma(w)/w^2$. Its extension with $U(0)=H$ is Lipschitz by the differentiated Taylor remainder. On the disk take the odd trial section $d(w+p)U$, in the complement take $\pm\sigma$, and interpolate in a fixed regular collar so the section agrees with its normalization. At time zero its residual in the odd frame on the disk is
$$
    \left((\bar d/d)(\bar w+\bar p)
             -(w+p)\bar w^2/w^2\right) B(w)U(w).
$$
The integral of its absolute value on $|w|\le K\mu$ is $O(\mu^3)$ for fixed large $K$. On the annulus outside this circle,
$$
              d(w+p)=w^2-\Delta-\lambda p/w+O(\mu^4/|w|^2).
$$
Inserting this in the numerator of the residual and dividing by $d$ gives the integrable linear term $(\Delta\bar w^2/w^3-\bar\Delta/w)B(w)U(w)$. The corresponding collar term comes from the cutoff of $-\Delta U/w$ in the central odd coefficient. Together they represent the off-pole image of that section, hence the matching class of $-\Delta H$ up to a common real sign and normalization. In this identification one may subtract a local homogeneous section with the same value $-\Delta H$ before division by $w$. The remaining bounded section has integrable derivative and gives zero in adjoint pairing by integration on a punctured disk and passage to the limit.

At the following order the terms are
$$
 \left(-\frac{\overline{\lambda p}}{|w|^2}
        +\lambda p\frac{\bar w^2}{w^4}\right) B(w)U(w).
$$
The second term has zero angular mean. Replacing $B(w)U(w)$ and a limiting adjoint by their values at zero costs $o(\mu^3\log(1/\mu))$, by continuity (in the terms displayed here the sharper bound is $o(|\lambda p|\log(1/\mu))$). The difference between the true adjoints and their limits has the same little-oh bound. The $1/d$ correction on the linear term is $O(|\Delta|\mu\log(1/\mu))$ in $L^1$, and the rest integrates to $O(\mu^3)$. The collar error after its linear term is also $O(\mu^3)$. All linear terms may be tested against the limiting adjoints with error $o(|\Delta|)$. Changing time adds its central derivative times $t$ up to $o(|t|)$: the bounded-coefficient change divided by $t$ applied to the trial converges in $L^1$, using the smooth operator path, the smooth collar identification, and dominated convergence.

Write $M:N_0\to\operatorname{coker}A_{\rm odd,0}$ for the inclusion, choosing the common nonzero real normalization of its point-mass representative. The resulting relative expansion of the five real rows has the form
$$
    Tt+M\left(c_1\Delta H+
            c_2\bar p^3\log(1/|p|)\,B(0)H\right)
      +o\!\left(|t|+|\Delta|+|p|^3\log(1/|p|)\right),                       \tag{2}
$$
where $c_1,c_2$ are nonzero real constants and $T$ projects to the derivative of the base crossing on $\sigma$. To check uniformity, $\mu^2\le C(|\Delta|+|p|^2)$ implies $\mu^3=o(|\Delta|+|p|^3\log(1/|p|))$. In the averaged term use $-\lambda p=2p^3-2p\Delta$. The error involving $p\Delta\log(1/\mu)$ is $o(|\Delta|)$. When $\mu$ is comparable to $|p|$ the two logarithms differ by $O(1)$. When $\mu/|p|$ is large, $|\Delta|$ controls $\mu^2$ and absorbs $|p|^3\log(1/|p|)$. These bounds include $\lambda=0$ and the limiting values $p=0$.

The three summands in (2) span a real line and two independent complex planes. The leading map bounds the sum of their norms from below. Straight interpolation therefore preserves boundary nonvanishing near the origin and proves isolation. The complex coordinate change $(p,\lambda)\mapsto(p,\Delta)$ preserves orientation. The linear $\Delta$ block preserves its complex orientation, and the map from the $p$ plane to the remaining complex line has degree $-3$. Permuting two real planes gives positive sign. The time row contributes $\eta_A$ by the determinant normalization sequence. The Euler integral on a small sphere in this five-dimensional chart is twice the resulting degree. Dividing by the order-two deck group gives $-3\eta_A$.

Applied to the graph operator at the equal-height end, this gives one such local contribution at each of its two radial double zeros. Other odd incidences along an operator path would enter a global Euler difference separately. Equations (1) and (2) involve two different choices of upper parameter. Together with the transverse degree in Calculation F1, they give constraints on a single quartic comparison. Its absolute residual weight $R$ in [Calculation F2](contracted-expansions-of-a-regular-unramified-tower.md) requires a coherent relative-time perturbation over the entire projective incidence and its two attachments.
