# F9. Smooth double-cover crossings near a ramified graph

Let $f:Y\to C$ be a connected double cover of a genus-$g$ curve, $g\ge2$, with $b>0$ simple branch values and $h=g(Y)=2g-1+b/2$. Let an odd section $\xi$ span the pullback kernel at a transverse lower crossing of a real fixed-symbol Cauchy–Riemann operator. Assume that its base operator is invertible, its graph $\gamma$ is embedded and the projected antilinear jet at each ramification point is nonzero. Put $A=D^N_\gamma$ on the fixed curve $Y$. The upper covers below have degree two over $Y$ and source genus $2h$.

**Theorem.** After an arbitrarily small generic perturbation of the lower operator preserving $f,\xi$, take any transverse fixed-symbol path $A_t$ through $A$ on $Y$, with crossing sign $\eta_A$. On a sufficiently short interval its endpoints are regular for the upper odd two-branch family. The difference of their odd Euler numbers is
$$
   \epsilon(A_{t_+})F_2(A_{t_+})-\epsilon(A_{t_-})F_2(A_{t_-})
                        =-3b\,\eta_A.
$$
Consequently every generic comparison path on $Y$ between these endpoints has at least $3b$ smooth two-branch cover–time pairs carrying an odd kernel. For a sufficiently close generic perturbation of the short path, there are at least three such pairs in each of $b$ disjoint neighborhoods, one about each split nodal double joined at a ramification point of $f$. The endpoints may be arbitrarily close to $A$. This generic comparison allows the operator at the original central time to move.

The lower graph deformation sequence identifies both kernel and cokernel of $A$ with real lines, since the lower branch velocities together with time give an isomorphism. Its radial normal section $\sigma=[(0,\xi)]$ has a double zero at each ramification and no other zero. The local expansion and the nonzero projected antilinear coefficient at each double zero are the calculation of [Calculation F3](the-two-endpoint-degrees-at-a-ramified-double-wall.md), which takes place in a single ramification disk. The same note computes the local odd Euler change $-3\eta_A$ in the split root chart, separately at every one of these points.

We check the global regularity on the upper finite surface. Use variations on a regular lower disk annihilating $\xi$ as in [Calculation F6](regularity-of-the-upper-double-family-on-the-graph.md). When the upper sign character and its translate by the lower involution $\tau$ differ on the complement of their branch values, that argument gives transversality for the odd incidence. Its real projective index with moving branch positions is $-4+4-1=-1$. The connected étale twists are regular using the same variations by [Calculation E1](../polar/the-normal-twist-at-a-ramified-quartic-tower.md).

If the upper double admits a lift of $\tau$ and its distinct branch values form a nonfixed pair $y,\tau y$, the lift squares to the identity because of the fiber over a lower fixed point. The upper odd part splits into two eigensystems. At each of the $b$ lower fixed points its rank-two bundle at the Dolbeault reference, $N^\gamma\otimes L^{-1}$ for the upper root $L$, has one positive and one negative weight. Pullback of either eigendirect image thus has length-one quotient at each of those $b$ points. The degree of either rank-two eigendirect image on $C$ is
$$
                    \frac{2h-4-b}{2}=2g-3.
$$
Its real index is $-2$, the moving orbit has two real parameters, and projectivization again gives index $-1$. Paired-sheet testing supplies transversality in each eigensystem as in Calculation F6. For a divisor consisting of two distinct lower fixed points, a lift must square to the nontrivial upper deck by its action in the local square-root coordinate. When $b>2$, the square fixes the two-point fiber over a third lower fixed point, a contradiction. The possible order-four case at $b=2$ is covered in Calculation F6. This exhausts the distinct branch divisors.

On a nodal upper double with connected normalization the odd kernel embeds in a regular étale twist. On a split normalization an odd kernel is $(a\sigma,-a\sigma)$ and matching requires $a\sigma(y)=0$. We have therefore identified the entire compact upper odd kernel incidence at time zero. At each exceptional point the local degree calculation also gives isolation as time varies through zero. This makes the endpoints regular throughout a sufficiently small punctured time interval, by compactness. The usual projective transversality arguments used here work first in finite differentiability. Openness away from the finite exceptional neighborhoods, and the local lower bound by the linear and conjugate-cubic terms of Calculation F3 inside them, give the smooth perturbations with the same conclusions. Equivalently one may work on a countable exhaustion of the smooth cover charts in the smooth parameter space, using localized finite-dimensional tensor variations and ordinary parametric transversality. Summing the local odd degrees proves the formula.

For the smooth count use unrestricted real fixed-symbol perturbations on $Y$. At a nodal double with sign twist $\alpha$ on its normalization, the odd equation would require a nonzero solution of $A_t^\alpha$ vanishing at a freely moving point $y$. With the index-zero twist, real projectivization, time and $y$, the projection index is $0-1+1+2-4=-2$. Testing away from the mark with arbitrary localized variations gives ordinary transversality and the remaining evaluation is tested by varying the smooth section at the mark. Thus generic paths avoid these nodal kernels. On the smooth locus the odd two-branch projective problem has path index zero, is transverse for generic paths, and each point contributes one signed unit after the even-sphere Euler factor and the upper deck order cancel. The compact surface and regular endpoints give finiteness. The signed count is the endpoint odd Euler difference along every such path by [Calculation E5](../polar/polar-kernels-on-two-branched-double-covers.md). For a sufficiently close perturbation relative to the fixed endpoints the boundary of each of the chosen exceptional neighborhoods stays free of kernels. Local degree invariance then gives signed count $-3\eta_A$ separately in each neighborhood. Distinct cover–time pairs need not occur at distinct times.

**Examples over one genus-two curve.** For each $j\ge1$ the change of positive orthogonal axes by degree offset $2j$ in Calculation E5 has nonzero top ordinary crossing count in genus $h=4+2j$. Choose a generic comparison path on the fixed genus-two curve. An odd crossing can be taken with base invertible: imposing a simultaneous base and odd solution has path index $-1$ after both lines are projectivized. Real tensor variations on a regular disk can test their two values independently. Otherwise one would be a real multiple of the other on the disk, hence a constant multiple by the equation, in contradiction with their distinct characters on the common cover. On the smooth double this crossing is transverse with simple odd kernel. At an unramified moving point a zero of its generator is avoided by ordinary section evaluation, of real codimension four with two real parameters for the point. The odd equation ensures opposite nonzero heights on every regular fiber. At ramification the nonzero derivative and projected jet hold generically by the joint branch-jet transversality in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B, applied locally at the base-invertible crossing. These properties give the embedded graph and jets required above. A small graph-preserving perturbation supplies the upper regularity without changing $f$ or the lower transversality.

This constructs lower covers with $b=2+4j$ simple branch values over the same genus-two target. On their respective source curves $Y$, arbitrarily close endpoints in the normal-operator space force at least $6+12j$ smooth two-branch upper cover–time pairs of source genus $8+4j$. For $j=1$ this gives at least eighteen pairs over a genus-six curve. Composition gives holomorphic maps of degree four over $C$; the kernel and its signed count in this statement are for the ordinary upper equation on $Y$.
