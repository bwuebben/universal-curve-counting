# F4. Separating the two heights on a common parameter

In the setting of [Calculation F1](the-smoothing-at-an-interior-two-copy-height.md), choose a disk of regular values disjoint from the two branch disks. On it the two heights of the lower kernel are $\pm\xi_i(w)$ and their squared Hermitian norm $k(w)^2$ is positive. Choose a smooth real zeroth-order tensor $K$ supported in this disk so that the time direction in $D_t=D_0+tK$ completes the lower branch derivative to an isomorphism $W_f$ with the prescribed orientation. To obtain such a tensor, test against a nonzero odd adjoint annihilating the branch columns. Its restriction to either lift is nonzero on an open subdisk by unique continuation. An arbitrary real zeroth-order tensor acts freely on the nonzero section there, and the two odd pairings have the same sign. The disk and tensor can therefore be chosen together. All assertions below concern this transverse representative.

For a degree-four map $u$ and a nonzero section $s$ near the central projective incidence, take a nonnegative area form $\omega$ of integral one supported in the regular disk, and put
$$
 I(u,s)=\tfrac14\int_{\Sigma}|s|^2u^*(\omega/k^2).
$$
Use the bundle metric pulled back from the base. This is positive near the incidence considered here and scales quadratically. Contracted components have zero pullback area. The formula also applies when another cover branches in the disk, since it integrates the pullback form. Fix the real scale by $I=1$, leaving simultaneous sign. Choose a smooth bounded function $\chi$ constant near $0$ and $2$, negative on $[0,1)$, positive on $(1,2]$, with $\chi(1)=0$, $\chi'(1)>0$. On the support of $K$, add to the section equation the term
$$
      \delta\,\chi\!\left(\frac{|s|^2}{k^2 I(u,s)}\right)(u^*K)s.             \tag{1}
$$
It is equivariant for real scaling, source automorphisms and sheet exchange, and zero off the test disk. On the normalized slice it is a smooth local zeroth-order perturbation in the target variables. We use (1) on the finite-cover charts. No comparison of virtual classes on the full stable-map boundary is used below.

**Interior removal.** For each compact subinterval of the open projective height interval at either ramification join, no solutions with $\delta\ne0$ tend to that subinterval as $\delta\to0$, with all other parameters allowed to vary locally. On the interval itself write the normalized amplitudes as $a,b$, with $(a^2+b^2)/2=1$ and $0<b<a$. The two effective times in (1) are $t+\delta\chi(a^2)$ and $t+\delta\chi(b^2)$, whose difference has nonzero coefficient. Control of the off-interval terms is needed for the assertion.

To prove it, use the framing, true linear stabilizations and coordinates of Calculation F1 on a hypothetical convergent sequence. For nearby sections take $a,b$ to be their actual thick tests in the $I=1$ slice. They approach the normalized central amplitudes just described. Over the support of $K$ the four sheets separate into the two labeled pairs of the local chart. On each pair freeze the coefficient of (1) at $\chi(a_i^2)$, where $a_1=a,a_2=b$. The frozen problem is linear. The two component times are independent inputs $t_i=t+\delta\chi(a_i^2)$ sharing the original geometric parameters. All plumbing and coefficient identities of Calculation F1 apply with these two time inputs: the coefficients on the neck and on its surgery collars are identical, and on each thick piece one uses the smooth family of the corresponding normalized component operator. The local matching supplement and its cutoff can be chosen disjoint from the support of $K$ and from the normalization tests.

Let $d$ be the maximum discrepancy on the test disk between the actual section and the corresponding signed reference value $a_i\xi_i$ on the labeled sheets, measured over the same base value. Put $\varepsilon=\max(|p|,|s_0|)$ for the two attaching coordinates ($s_0$ is the coordinate denoted $s$ in Calculation F1). Write $\lambda_i=(x-p_i^2,u_i,t_i)$ for the branch, other-branch and time coordinates on the two components, with $p_1=p,p_2=s_0$, and put $\Lambda=\max_i|\lambda_i|$. The difference between true and frozen forcing is bounded by $C|\delta|d$ in the stabilized target norm since it is supported on a fixed thick set. The frozen solution with the same two normalization tests and its obstruction rows differ from the true solution and zero rows by $O(|\delta|d)$, by the uniform stabilized inverse. On the test disk the matching cutoff vanishes. The two component solutions there differ from their reference values by $O(\Lambda)$. The glued trial estimate (5) of Calculation F1 consequently gives
$$
     d\le C(\Lambda+|q|+\varepsilon\sqrt{|q|}+|\delta|d).                    \tag{2}
$$
No division by $q$ is involved in this estimate.

On this compact subinterval the two frozen values of $\chi$ stay separated, so $|\delta|\le C\Lambda$. The component rows (2)–(3) of Calculation F1, now using both time rows, give
$$
             \Lambda\le C(|q|+\Lambda^2+|\delta|d).
$$
Since $d\to0$, this implies $\Lambda=O(|q|)$ and $|\delta|=O(|q|)$. If $q=0$ these bounds force $\delta=0$. For $q\ne0$ the first two branch rows now have errors $o(|q|)$. Subtract them to get
$$
          s_0^2-p^2=2(b/a-a/b)q+o(|q|).
$$
Thus $|q|=O(\varepsilon^2)$, (2) gives $d=O(\varepsilon^2)$, and all frozen obstruction rows are $O(\varepsilon^4)$. The first matching row forces $s_0/p\to a/b$ in the sense of vanishing difference when the heights vary, and $q/p^2+a/(2b)\to0$. On this comparable scale the remaining matching plane has a nonzero leading multiple of $\bar p^3\log(1/|p|)$ uniformly on the compact interval, by (8)–(10) of Calculation F1. Its size contradicts the $O(\varepsilon^4)$ bound. This proves the removal assertion.

**Signs at the endpoints.** At zero height the nonzero normalized amplitude is $\sqrt2$. On the two pieces (1) is exactly constant on the support of $K$ throughout sufficiently small neighborhoods in the finite charts, by the plateaux of $\chi$. Holding the lower crossing at its effective time zero gives $t=-\delta\chi(2)$. The upper time then runs as $\delta(\chi(0)-\chi(2))$, which decreases with $\delta$. Thus the contribution to the *change of the regular upper expression* from the distinguished upper event at each flag is $+3$ in the direction of increasing $\delta$, by (1) of [Calculation F3](the-two-endpoint-degrees-at-a-ramified-double-wall.md).

At equal height the normalized nonzero sheet is $\xi$ and $t=0$ preserves its graph for all $\delta$. On the normalized chart, before the scalar normalization equation is imposed, use the local graph equation obtained by setting $I=1$ in (1). Let $A_\delta$ be its normal operator on this embedded graph. Differentiating its added term first in the radial direction represented by $(0,\xi)$ and then in $\delta$ gives $2\chi'(1)K\xi$ on the test disk. This statement concerns the graph equation on the fixed slice extended as a local equation on the target. The derivative of the unrestricted scale-equivariant equation on a common radial rescaling would vanish. Projection of this forcing to $\operatorname{coker}A$ agrees under the graph deformation sequence with its class in $\operatorname{coker}f^*D_0/W_f(T_f)$. Hence the normal-operator crossing has sign $\eta_A=\eta_f$. Indeed its kernel is identified with the lower kernel by the radial generator, the time class is a positive multiple of the class of $K\xi$, and the canceled branch block has the complex orientation of the four real Hurwitz directions. These determinant identifications agree with the absolute orientations by deformation of the triangular complexes to their complex-linear parts, as in [Calculation E1](../polar/the-normal-twist-at-a-ramified-quartic-tower.md).

In this common parameter the local variation of the fixed-$Y$ odd Euler number at each split join is therefore $-3\eta_f$. Multiplying this number by the frozen lower sign gives $-3$, opposite to the $+3$ just obtained from the marked expression. The two signs test compatibility of the endpoint models. They do not by themselves identify the upper Euler numbers with the entire quartic obstruction class. In particular the split root gerbe over the fixed $Y$ and the equal-height quartic join have different stabilizers. Sequences whose height ratios tend to an endpoint escape the compact-subinterval estimate. A formula for the absolute residual $R$ requires a uniform comparison on precisely these sequences, with the scalar equation, automorphisms and any additional nearby smooth solutions included.
