# H2. Parity at the first join of a ramified cubic Galois closure

Let $f:Y\to C$ be an ordinary transverse cubic crossing on a path $D_t$ of real Cauchy–Riemann operators on a complex plane bundle $N$ of degree $2g-2$ over a fixed smooth curve of genus $g\ge2$. The base operator is invertible. The cubic has monodromy $S_3$, an embedded kernel graph and $b=2l>0$ distinct simple branch values. Write $Z$ for its Galois closure, $T=Z/A_3$ for its discriminant double and $H_d=1+d(g-1)$. We use the regular evaluation ranks, a disk of complex independence of two sheet heights, and the simultaneous central kernel description of [Calculation H1](kernels-in-all-degrees-at-a-cubic-wall.md). All dimensions of kernels and cokernels below are real.

**Theorem.** After a generic arbitrarily small perturbation preserving the given cubic graph on such a disk, the following holds. Fix $d=6+m$ with $m\ge1$ and $3\nmid d$. At genus $k=H_d+3l+1$, choose one branch value $x$ and a connected degree-$m$ unramified cover with one distinguished point over $x$, up to pointed isomorphism. The chart of the corresponding join has **odd integral localized Euler change** between sufficiently close regular ends on opposite sides, in the sense of the neighborhoods specified below. The subtotal over a given $x$ is odd precisely when $m$ is a square or twice a square. The full connected change is an even integer and equals the change of the elementary exponent at super-rigid ends. For a genus-two base with two branch values, $d=7$ and $k=12$, each of the two branch neighborhoods has nonzero odd change. Their signed sum can be zero on the evidence of this parity calculation.

The same conclusions hold at ordinary ramified cubic crossings on generic paths, with regular ends chosen coefficientwise. The integer assigned to a neighborhood includes other zeros approaching the join in the comparison interval. The theorem fixes its parity on arbitrarily small such neighborhoods. An isolated signed degree of the central nodal map requires more information about smoothing.

## The odd operator on the discriminant

At a chosen join let $\tau$ denote the inertia transposition. Write $V$ for the real standard plane. Of the three ramification points of $Z$ over each lower branch value $y$, exactly one, $r_y$, is fixed by $\tau$. The others are interchanged. A vector spanning $V^{-\tau}$, the negative eigenline, gives a section $s$ on $Z$. In compatible sheet labels it is the difference of the two heights exchanged by $\tau$. At $r_y$ its value is zero and in a coordinate $w-y=z^2$ its first derivative $v_y=\partial_zs(r_y)$ is nonzero. Indeed the two heights there have the expansions of the lower kernel section at $z$ and $-z$ on its ramifying sheet. Their first derivatives differ by twice the derivative of the embedded graph in the vertical direction. The disk descends isomorphically from $Z$ to $T$, since $A_3$ acts freely. Thus the complex residue line $\mathbb C v_y$ is specified at the unique ramification point of $T$ above $y$, with the tangent factor understood for the coefficient of $1/z$.

Let $F^-$ be the odd part of the pullback operator on $T$. Its kernel vanishes by the central classification. Its real index is $-2b$, by Riemann–Roch on $T$ and index zero on the invariant part. The latter index follows by deformation to a complex-linear operator and descent of the invariant finite pushforward. Adjoin to its $W^{1,p}$ domain, $p>2$, the $b$ complex planes of cutoff poles $\chi_y a_y v_y/z$, where the cutoffs are even. Apply the operator off the poles. In a pulled-back frame holomorphic for the complex-linear part the antilinear coefficient has a factor $\bar z$, so the image of each column extends smoothly across the puncture. Use an odd coordinate and a frame pulled back from $C$ there. Changes of these choices leave the polar class unchanged up to a change of residue basis and a regular domain section. The resulting odd Fredholm operator $\widehat F^-$ has index zero. This is the ramified-point augmentation of [Calculation E5](../polar/polar-kernels-on-two-branched-double-covers.md), here with one column at every branch value.

**Regularity.** Invertibility of $\widehat F^-$ is open and dense among small disk perturbations killing two real lower heights $s_1,s_2$ which form a complex frame. Keep the graph and the pole data fixed. For a nonzero augmented solution $h$ choose a branch of it on the regular disk. On some subdisk its value lies outside the pointwise real span of $s_1,s_2$. Otherwise $h=a_1s_1+a_2s_2$ there with smooth real coefficients. Their common equation gives $(\bar\partial a_1)s_1+(\bar\partial a_2)s_2=0$ and forces both to be constant. On the connected complement of the special fibers in $Z$, unique continuation would identify a section of sign type with a linear combination of the standard sections. The sign and standard subrepresentations intersect trivially, also in the space of solutions on the punctured cover. A zero section away from the poles has zero residues as well. This proves the assertion about values.

On this subdisk a real zeroth-order perturbation killing the two heights can prescribe its value on $h$ arbitrarily. The odd adjoint pairings on the two sheets add. An annihilator of the derivative of the universal augmented equation therefore vanishes there, and vanishes on the punctured double by adjoint unique continuation. Its $L^{p/(p-1)}$ representative vanishes everywhere. Projectivizing the nonzero solution gives Fredholm projection index $-1$ and hence generic injectivity. Equivalently one can use finite-dimensional perturbations against kernel and cokernel at a fixed operator and compactness of its projectivized kernel. Openness permits smooth approximation in the subbundle of permitted perturbations. There are only three choices of $\tau$, related by deck transformations. Intersect with the residual conditions of Calculation H1. For a varying transverse cubic, branch disks, residue lines and odd operators continue smoothly on marked Hurwitz charts. The same disk variations are tangent to the lower incidence. Adding a projectivized polar solution lowers its projection index by one, so its composite with the index-minus-one lower incidence has index $-2$. A generic path avoids these charts. This argument uses the smooth covers $Y,Z,T$ throughout.

## The fixed directions at the join

By Calculation H1 the entire central kernel locus in degree $d$ at genus $k$ consists of a copy of $Z$ joined to a connected unramified zero-height component $U\to C$ at a ramification flag of $Z$. The pullback to $U$ is invertible. The three flags over a given $x$ form a single deck orbit in $Z$. The stabilizer of the pointed map on $U$ is trivial, and the two components are distinguished by their ramification. The automorphism group of the join is exactly $\langle\tau\rangle$. Normalization and matching give kernel $K=\mathbb R s$ and
$$
  0\longrightarrow N_x/\operatorname{ev}_{r_x}(V)
    \longrightarrow P\longrightarrow \operatorname{coker}D_Z
    \longrightarrow 0.                                                    \tag{1}
$$
Here $D_Z$ is the pulled-back normal operator, $P$ is the cokernel on the join and the evaluation image is one real line. The fiber quotient is a trivial $\tau$-representation of real dimension three. Hence the fixed part of $\operatorname{Hom}(K,P)$ identifies with the **negative** eigenspace of $\operatorname{coker}D_Z$ upon evaluating on $s$.

The smooth finite-map chart has $3b$ complex branch coordinates for $Z$, together with the complex attaching and smoothing coordinates $p,q$ at the join. The local algebra there is $zy=q$, $w=w_0+(z+p)^2+y$ in translated coordinates (the letter $y$ here denotes the coordinate on the zero-height branch). All the other simple ramification disks are separate factors in the source, even at coincident branch values. Their relative tangent sheaves have length one each. The node has length three, the other étale sheets have length zero, and the relative deformation complex has no obstruction. This gives complex dimension $3b+2=2k-2-d(2g-2)$ and the ordinary fundamental class. The involution negates $p,q$, fixes the branch shift at each $r_y$ and exchanges the shifts at the other two flags over $y$. The fixed chart including time has real dimension $4b+1$. It keeps the node and the attaching mark at the ramification point. There the negative equation is entirely on the $\tau$-equivariant deformation of the smooth sextic. Negative sections vanish automatically at its fixed flag, and on $U$ the involution acts trivially. This identifies the fixed column smoothly by normalization. Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B · [Smooth maps and nodal pullback operators, Bai–Swaminathan, Remark 1.5 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4).

We compute the derivative on this fixed chart. For each lower branch value take one common shift of all three flags and a further independent shift of the $\tau$-fixed flag only. Common shifts and time preserve the full Galois cover construction. Their derivative from the kernel copy $V$ to $\operatorname{coker}D_Z$ is $S_3$-equivariant. The cokernel of trivial type vanishes. Its sign part is the odd cokernel on $T$, of dimension $2b$. Its standard multiplicity space identifies with the cokernel on the cubic $Z/\langle\tau\rangle$, of dimension $2b+1$. For these identifications stabilize by pullbacks of forms away from the branch set on the quotients. A solution of the stabilized invariant equation descends off the branch points, is bounded there and extends by removability, which proves the asserted cokernel identifications using the equality of indices at a complex-linear operator. The descent on a family of common shifts is compatible with differentiation on the kernel. Evaluating the common-shift and time derivative on a nonzero vector of the $\tau$-fixed line in $V$ gives the lower isomorphism $W_f$. Since $\operatorname{End}_{S_3}(V)=\mathbb R$, evaluating the same equivariant maps on $s$ identifies these $2b+1$ columns with the entire negative standard summand. They have zero sign projection.

For the remaining $b$ complex shifts project the derivative to the sign part. Moving just $r_y$ by a branch-value displacement $\alpha$, the obstruction is the principal-part class of $\pm\alpha v_y/(2z)$ on that disk, modulo regular domain sections. Here is a local derivation. Use the family $w=y+z^2+\alpha$ on the inner disk, trivialize the cover by its target coordinate on the exterior, and interpolate on the collar. On the inner disk choose smooth local solutions $s_\alpha$ for the translated equation with $s_0=s$. A Cauchy right inverse on a small disk corrects the error of the constant family and depends smoothly on the displacement; averaging with the odd projection preserves $s_0$. At fixed punctured target coordinate the local sheet becomes $(z^2-\alpha)^{1/2}$. The derivative of $s_\alpha$ evaluated on this sheet solves the unchanged pulled-back equation away from zero. Its only unbounded term is $-\alpha v_y/(2z)$ in directional-derivative notation: the material derivative is smooth, $s_z-v_y=O(|z|)$, and $s_{\bar z}/\bar z$ is smooth by the equation. Subtract the pole. The bounded remainder solves an inhomogeneous equation with smooth forcing since $D_Z(v_y/z)$ off the pole extends smoothly. Removability followed by regularity gives a regular section at zero. Patching the local solution and the unchanged exterior one with a cutoff leaves only their derivative mismatch on the collar. Modulo the image of regular variations this is the class of $D_Z(\chi_y\alpha v_y/(2z))$ computed off the pole. The overall sign depends on which side of the linearized equation contains the deformation column.

The sign projection on a $\tau$-odd column is the average over $A_3$. The three translated pole disks are disjoint, project isomorphically to the same disk in $T$, and carry translates of the sole original column. In a chart lifted from $r_y$ the averaged residue is one third of the original one. It is nonzero. Thus the projected fixed-flag block, up to invertible changes of the $b$ source planes, is precisely the map from the polar summands to $\operatorname{coker} F^-$. Its invertibility is equivalent to that of $\widehat F^-$ since $\ker F^-=0$. Together with the common columns this proves invertibility of the fixed derivative. The full source, including time, and $\operatorname{Hom}(K,P)$ have real dimension $6b+5$. Their moving parts both have dimension $2b+4$.

## The derivative along the nodal divisor

There is a further description of the two real directions needed for the signed calculation. Write $e_x$ for a nonzero vector in the evaluation image in (1). Assume $e_x\notin\mathbb C v_x$. This holds on generic crossing paths: on the marked cubic the difference of the heights at the two distinct points in the branch fiber and the holomorphic first derivative at the ramifying point give arbitrary simultaneous variations in $N_x^2$. Both tests annihilate pulled-back base sections. The adjoint test of Article IV, §2 therefore applies to the combined marked functional, using independently prescribed values and holomorphic first jet at the two points. The value difference is a nonzero real multiple of $e_x$, and the derivative a nonzero real multiple of $v_x$ in compatible branch coordinates. For nonzero derivative the excluded condition has real codimension two. This is a separate open hypothesis for a prescribed graph, and is unnecessary for the parity theorem.

On the divisor $q=0$ the joined obstruction column is smooth in normalized-component, attaching and time variables, by the matching presentation. Its derivative $J$ is injective. To verify this, the fixed columns were already accounted for. The moving velocities on the normalized cover consist of opposite shifts $\alpha,-\alpha$ at the two $\tau$-exchanged flags over each $y$. Applied to the odd kernel generator they give a $\tau$-invariant column. It descends to the cubic. Label $\tau=(12)$, take the root $(1,-1,0)$ and an even vector $(1,1,-2)$ in $V$. The first derivative of the section with coefficient vector $(a_1,a_2,a_3)$ at an inertia-$(ij)$ flag is proportional to $a_i-a_j$, with common nonzero vector for that flag. This follows by differentiating the conjugate heights, two of which are interchanged while the third is even. At the two exchanged flags, of inertia $(13)$ and $(23)$, the ratios for the root and the even vector are $1/3,-1/3$. The opposite velocities therefore give exactly one third of the polar column of a common shift applied to the even vector. At the remaining flag the even vector has zero derivative and hence no pole column. By the local variation calculation above these identities of principal parts are identities in the cokernel. On the cubic the common shift is its ordinary branch column in $W_f$. Consequently the moving branch directions have image of dimension $2b$ in the positive eigenspace of $\operatorname{coker}D_Z$, with one-dimensional quotient identified with $\operatorname{coker}D_f/W_f(T_f\mathcal H_f)$ after choosing the even generator. Here $\mathcal H_f$ denotes the fixed-target cubic Hurwitz chart.

Finally varying the attaching coordinate $p$ moves the mark on the sextic with derivative $v_x p$ and keeps the normalized maps fixed. The zero-height component contributes zero to the evaluation derivative. Thus the attaching column is injective in the matching quotient $N_x/\mathbb R e_x$ by the additional hypothesis, and its image there is the projected complex line $\mathbb C v_x$. This proves injectivity of $J$ and gives the exact sequence of real vector spaces
$$
 0\longrightarrow N_x/(\mathbb R e_x+\mathbb C v_x)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{coker}J
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{coker}D_f/W_f(T_f\mathcal H_f)
 \longrightarrow0.                                                        \tag{2}
$$
In (2) we evaluate the column on the chosen root and use the even generator for the cubic identification. Both outer spaces are real lines. Thus the nodal divisor has an injective derivative with two missing equations. The first line measures matching after moving the attaching mark and varying the normalized height. The quotient line measures the obstruction on the cubic left by its branch motions. Determining the signed degree still requires the smoothing parameter in both equations, with control of the errors after eliminating the nodal directions.

## Local degree and the sum over one branch value

Fix a uniformizing ball in the finite-map chart about each central map, with disjoint images for inequivalent data. The kernel is a line at its center. Stabilization by representatives of $P$ gives an equivariant line bundle and its obstruction column over a smaller ball times a time interval. The line can be equivariantly trivialized with the action on $K$. The column is continuous across the node and smooth on the fixed stratum, where it is the normalized smooth-cover column above. For the continuity one can use thick stabilizing forms and the pullback-family construction of [Bai–Swaminathan, Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4#A2), or the local polynomial-frame construction of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. The latter has a single cubic nodal factor here, along with smooth quadratic and étale factors; each can be trivialized separately on its source neighborhood. Equivariant averaging of a finite supplement and selection of a minimal invariant complement give the single column. No derivative transverse to $q=0$ is required.

At time zero the boundaries of the balls are free of kernels by the central classification. Choose the comparisons sufficiently short that their lateral boundaries remain kernel-free. Choose both ends injective at the degree and genus in question. Such ends can be taken arbitrarily close on opposite sides on generic paths, or by arbitrarily small path perturbations preserving the central data and its first derivative as in Article IV, Appendix E. The complement of these neighborhoods is kernel-free on a sufficiently short comparison by compactness at the given degree and genus. The resulting closed cylinder in each uniformizing chart has nonzero column on its whole boundary. Its fixed zero is unique and transverse by the preceding derivative and the inverse function theorem, after shrinking the ball and interval.

The mod-two degree on this boundary is one. Indeed the source and target of the column are isomorphic as real involution representations. Near the unique fixed zero one may extend its fixed derivative by any isomorphism of the moving parts, equivariantly deforming the column in the interior while preserving its fixed restriction there. It can then be perturbed transversely off the fixed set, where additional zeros occur in free pairs. Equivalently this is the mod-two fixed-point formula for the local degree. On the boundary the endpoint-type cokernel bundle is the quotient of $P$ by the nonzero column line. Upstairs it is the pullback of the tangent bundle to the even sphere $S^{6b+4}$ under the normalized column. Its Euler number is twice the signed Brouwer degree, up to the determinant orientation. Integration over the chart quotient divides by two. The localized change is consequently odd. This calculation is the link argument of Article IV, Appendix B with its fixed derivative enlarged by the discriminant pole columns. It also proves invariance of the parity under shrinking the comparison with injective boundary.

At $x$ the number of join charts is the number of based index-$m$ subgroups of $\pi_1(C,x)$, meaning actual subgroups with no conjugacy quotient. For each isomorphism class of unpointed connected cover $u$, there are $m/|\operatorname{Aut}_C(u)|$ inequivalent choices of a point. Thus modulo two the subtotal is the parity of the index-$m$ subgroup count. It is odd exactly for a square or twice a square by [Müller and Schlage-Puchta, Theorem 1](https://www.mathematik.uni-rostock.de/storages/uni-rostock/Alle_MNF/Mathematik/Struktur/Lehrstuehle/Algebra/papers/onerepar.pdf). Multiplying by $b=2l$ gives even parity for the full change. Since $3\nmid d$, every divisor of $d$ is also indivisible by three. The lower-genus vanishing and proper-divisor bound of Calculation H1 identify the full connected change here with the primitive change at super-rigid ends. The per-branch subtotals concern localized Euler pairings; the exponent comparison applies after summing. The chart proof works simultaneously at all finitely many flags for each specified $d$. No common interval over the infinitely many degrees is asserted.

## Genus two on the spin comparison path

Take the repeated and distinct non-effective even-spin endpoints of Article IV, §3.3 on the same genus-two base. That comparison gives a signed count of $32$ ordinary cubic crossings with exactly two simple branch values. Impose the additional residual regularity above on the same path with fixed endpoints. Each such cubic produces two inequivalent degree-seven genus-twelve central maps, one over each value. Hence there are at least **64 distinct pairs of a nodal map and a parameter** with odd localized Euler change in arbitrarily small comparison neighborhoods. The cubic can be recovered from the sextic normalization as the isomorphism class of its reflection quotient, so counting distinct cubics introduces no multiplicity here. This counts local contributions and allows cancellation in the total degree-seven invariant. In every degree $6+m$ with $3\nmid m$, the corresponding statement per branch holds at genus $H_{6+m}+4$. In particular the two branch values of each cubic have odd subtotals for $m=j^2$ or $2j^2$ with $3\nmid j$.
