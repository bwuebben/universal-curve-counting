# H3. The signed smoothing degree at a ramified sextic join

Use the transverse ramified cubic $f:Y\to C$, its Galois closure $Z$, the base-invertible operator $D=D_0$, and the genus $k=H_d+3l+1$ of [Calculation H2](parity-at-a-ramified-sextic-join.md). Here $g=g(C)\ge2$, $b=2l>0$ is the number of simple branch values, $H_d=1+d(g-1)$ and $d=6+m$, $m\ge1$, $3\nmid d$. We assume the central classification and discriminant regularity of that note. Time increases across the crossing. Write $\epsilon(F)$ for the absolute determinant sign of an invertible index-zero real Cauchy–Riemann operator $F$.

**Theorem.** On generic paths each of the pointed joins at genus $k$ is isolated in the full finite-map chart times the path and contributes one signed unit. For every lower branch value $x$ the sign $a_x$ for the zero-height identity component is given by the oriented determinant (3) below. For another pointed unramified zero-height component $u:U\to C$ the sign is $a_x\epsilon(u^*D)\epsilon(D)$. In particular the full connected change, and the full change of the elementary exponent at super-rigid ends, are
$$
 \Delta L_{d,k}=\Delta n_{d,k}
 =\epsilon(D)\left(\sum_x a_x\right)
       \sum_{[u:U\to C]\ {\rm connected,\ unramified},\ \deg u=m}
                \frac{m}{|\operatorname{Aut}_C u|}\epsilon(u^*D).          \tag{1}
$$
Each assertion is coefficientwise. The sign for a prescribed cubic uses the jet and adjoint conditions below; it depends on the local operator. For $g=2$, $b=2$, $d=7$, the possibilities in (1) are $-2,0,2$.

## The two real linear forms

At a chosen flag use $w=z^2$ on $Z$ and choose the odd root section $s$ and $v=\partial_zs(0)$ of Calculation H2. Its conjugate even line has evaluation $\mathbb R e$ at this point. We use a frame holomorphic for the complex-linear part of $D$ and write $D=\bar\partial+B(w)\,d\bar w$ with $B$ antilinear. Assume $e\notin\mathbb C v$. The quotient cubic $Y=Z/\langle\tau\rangle$ has one unramified point $y$ over the chosen flag, with parameter $w$. A real cokernel functional on $D_f$ annihilating all lower branch-motion columns is unique up to a real factor. Represent a nonzero such functional by a smooth adjoint $\psi$, pairing a coefficient $a\,d\bar w$ at $y$ by $\operatorname{Re}\psi(w)(a)\,dA(w)$ with $\psi(w)$ a complex-linear covector in this frame. Fix the normalization of the projection from $\operatorname{coker}D_Z$ by averaging over $\tau$ before descent; on the two local sheets it takes the half-sum. Denote the resulting real functional on the joined cokernel $P$ by $\Lambda$. It kills both the matching subspace and the image of the nodal derivative $J$ from [Calculation H2](parity-at-a-ramified-sextic-join.md).

The two forms on the complex smoothing plane are
$$
 A(q)=[\bar q\,B(0)v]\ \in\ N_x/(\mathbb R e+\mathbb C v),\qquad
 H(q)=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{Re}\psi(0)(qv)\ \in\mathbb R.           \tag{2}
$$
Assume that $(A,H)$ is invertible. This is a condition at a smooth cubic, involving its adjoint and the branch jet. Its genericity is proved below. Let $\iota:N_x/\mathbb R e\to P$ be the normalization connecting map, with the sign convention in which a cutoff section of value $a$ on $Z$ and zero on $U$ represents $\iota(a)$ by its ordinary forcing on the normalization. Choose any $n\in P$ with $\Lambda(n)=1$. In coordinates $h$ on the nodal divisor including time and $q$ for the complex smoothing, the signed degree is the sign of the real linear isomorphism
$$
      (h,q)\ \longmapsto\ Jh+2\iota(\bar q B(0)v)+nH(q).                 \tag{3}
$$
Use the complex orientation of the chart followed by increasing time and the absolute Fredholm orientation of the column evaluated on $s$. The obstruction convention is $\mathcal A u=j(c)$ with positive inclusion of the thick supplement $j$; $c$ is the column. Changing $n$ adds a shear in the two-dimensional target quotient. Rescaling $\psi$ changes $n$ inversely modulo its kernel. The degree proof below identifies the formula with the oriented Euler change, in particular making it independent of the frame, the chosen root generator and analytic trivializations. Computation of (3) requires smooth normalization data only.

## Projection of the smoothing equation

Choose the finite-map chart, thick forms and real normalization of the central kernel as in Calculation H2. Use its equivariantly identified polynomial presentation on the fixed base curve: on the joining disk the algebra has basis $1,z,z^2$ and relation $z(z+p)^2-wz+q=0$. The attaching parameter $p$ is one component of $h$. Translate the branch center on a smaller disk. At disjoint quadratic factors make the corresponding translations separately, using their source idempotents; interpolate smoothly on unramified collars. Fix the supplement and the normalizing functional on unchanged thick disks. At the central join the height on the cubic algebra is $z u_1(w)$, where $u_1(0)=v$, $u_1(w)-v=O(|w|)$ and its weak first derivatives are bounded. This follows from oddness and the smooth equation on $Z$, which gives $s=vz+O(|z|^3)$ with first-derivative remainder $O(|z|^2)$. Ordinary branch factors admit interpolation in $W^{1,t}$, $2<t<4$, by decomposing their heights into the even average and the odd difference divided by the local square root. The even average descends by removability; the odd part has the same local estimate just used. Thus the single-column stabilization represents the actual normalized operator near the join by the index and evaluation comparison of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B.

Write $Q=q\log(1/|q|)$, extended by zero. The full column $F(h,q)$, with values identified with the central supplement $P$, satisfies
$$
 F(h,q)=Jh+2\iota(\bar Q B(0)v)+o(|h|+|Q|).                              \tag{4}
$$
Indeed the only logarithmic point mass in the conjugation matrix on the cubic disk is $-2\pi\bar Q\,z^2\delta_0$ on the coefficient of $\bar z$, from integration of the quadratic interpolant at the three moving roots. The separate smooth factors add their ordinary nodal derivatives. To see the sign of its matching class, for any $a$ take a local real frame of $D$-solutions $S$ with $S(0)=1$. The primitive $\chi z^2 S(w)a/w$ evaluates to $\chi Sa$ on the sextic and zero on the other component. Its distributional image has ordinary class $\iota(a)$ and point part $\pi a z^2\delta_0$. Their sum pairs to zero with central adjoints. This gives (4), including its sign, also after quotient by $\mathbb R e$. The matrix estimates giving the little-oh in the full supplement are those on the single cubic disk in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B; the center and time are smooth parameters in the translated frames.

A sharper estimate holds for the distinguished row. For each fixed $C_0$, on $|h|\le C_0|Q|$,
$$
                         \Lambda F(h,q)=H(q)+o(|q|).                      \tag{5}
$$
We give the estimates since an error $o(|Q|)$ alone does not determine the degree. A polynomial forcing $a_0+a_1z+a_2z^2$ on the central cubic disk has half-trace $a_0+w a_2$ on $Y$ and is tested against $\psi(w)$. In particular the weight on the most singular coefficient vanishes to first order. Work first on the frozen central generator. Put $\varepsilon=|q|^{1/3}$ and $R=|w|^{1/2}$. For $|w|\le A_0\varepsilon^2$, with a fixed sufficiently large $A_0$, the roots are $O(\varepsilon)$ and have diameter comparable with $\varepsilon$: their mean is $O(p)=o(\varepsilon)$ and their product is $-q$. The constant coefficient of the interpolant of $\bar z$ is $O(\varepsilon)$ and its quadratic coefficient is $O(1/\varepsilon)$ by the divided-difference bound. Their weighted sum is $O(\varepsilon)$, hence has integral $O(\varepsilon^5)=o(|q|)$. The central half-trace on this generator vanishes there for its conjugated $z$ term. Smooth coefficients are bounded.

Outside this disk let $r^2=w$. In $z/r$ the three interpolation roots are smooth near $0,1,-1$ in $p/r,q/r^3$. For the weighted test the prefactors of both coefficients scale in magnitude as $R$. Taylor's quadratic remainder is bounded by
$$
 C R (|p|/R+|q|/R^3)^2.
$$
Integration against $O(R^3\,dR)$ gives $O(|p|^2+|pq|+|q|^2/\varepsilon)=o(|q|)$. The linear $p$ term is bounded by $C|p|$ and the linear $q$ term by $C|q|/|w|$. Restoring their integrals on the excised disk costs $o(|q|)$. These estimates also allow multiplication by the bounded varying smooth coefficients, $u_1$ and the smooth test. The translated parameter and time changes give ordinary first-order terms plus $o(|q|)$ here: their smooth multipliers have quadratic Taylor error and their mixed products with the matrix difference are bounded by $O(|h|(|h|+|Q|))$ in the integrated pairing. The collars and remaining smooth factors give their usual first derivatives with quadratic remainder in these trivializations. In a fixed frame at an untranslated ordinary quadratic branch, the angular multiplier has derivative $O(1/|w|)$ and second derivative $O(1/|w|^2)$ under translation. Splitting at the translation radius gives $L^1$ Taylor error $O(|h|^2(1+|\log|h||))$, also sufficient.

For the actual stabilized generator one needs a rate of convergence. On the inner disk the worst matrix entry is $O(1/\varepsilon)$ and the area is $O(\varepsilon^4)$. On its exterior the difference from the central matrix has worst bound $C R^{-1}(|p|/R+|q|/R^3)$ by interpolation. For fixed $2<t<4$, its $L^t$ norm on the entire joining disk is therefore bounded by
$$
 C\bigl(\varepsilon^{4/t-1}+|p|\varepsilon^{4/t-2}\bigr).
$$
Smooth changes in $h$ and on collars add $O(|h|+|q|)$ to the operator norm $W^{1,t}\to L^t$; if ordinary centers are untranslated their multiplier changes add $O(|h|^{2/t})$. The uniform normalized stabilized inverse and the embedding into $C^0$ now give $O(|q|^\delta)$ convergence in $W^{1,t}$ and $C^0$ on this cone for some $\delta>0$. Central adjoints annihilate the central operator applied to the generator difference. Pairing that difference with the multiplier variation, whose full $L^1$ norm is $O(|h|+|Q|)$, or with the smooth differential-part variation on collars gives $o(|q|)$. All estimates are uniform in the argument of $q$. Finite thick representatives and the normalization functional can be kept fixed on their supports, so the pairing gives exactly the corresponding row of the reduced column.

It remains to identify the integrable first derivative just obtained. The $h$-linear terms pair to zero because $\Lambda J=0$. Differentiate the frozen polynomial at fixed target point in the $q$ direction. The two sextic roots move by $-q/(2w)$ and the zero-component root by $q/w$. The half-sum of the sextic sheet variations is $-q u_1(w)/(2w)$ near the chosen flag. Away from it, identify by the projection on étale collars. Differentiating an equation whose central forcing is zero gives the pullback normal operator on the sheet variation; derivatives of a comparison of forcing frames multiply zero. After $\tau$-averaging this is $D_f$ applied to a globally defined variation on the punctured cubic, with just the indicated simple pole at $y$. On the other branch disks the cover and its chosen section have no $q$ variation; the smooth collar contributions are part of this same global section. Integrating against the adjoint on the cubic with a small disk removed gives minus $\pi$ times the real pairing with its pole coefficient. Indeed $\partial_{\bar w}(1/w)=\pi\delta_0$ for Euclidean area and the bounded term in $u_1(w)/w-v/w$ has vanishing boundary pairing. The zero-component variation has zero test. This proves (5). In particular only data on the cubic appear in $H$.

## Signed degree and unramified zero components

Consider the straight-line homotopy from $F$ to the right side of (3) with $\bar q$ replaced only in the matching term by $\bar Q$. Any sequence of noncentral zeros converging to the origin along this homotopy satisfies $|h|=O(|Q|)$ by (4) and injectivity of $J$. If $q=0$ the nodal derivative excludes a noncentral zero. Otherwise project first to $P/\operatorname{im}J$, divide by $|Q|$, and use (4); a limiting unit direction of $q$ must be in $\ker A$. Applying $\Lambda$ and dividing by $|q|$ in (5) gives the same direction in $\ker H$. This contradicts (2). Thus the full chart has an isolated zero and its degree is that of this model. Replacing the positive logarithmic factor by one preserves the boundary degree since (2) excludes noncentral model zeros at every positive factor. The Euler number on the link is twice this signed degree and the inertia group has order two, as in Calculation H2. This proves (3).

For different pointed $u$ the normalized obstruction space and both displayed projections have the same description. In the nodal matching presentation the zero component is invertible and its normalized solution is identically zero as the sextic and the mark vary. Different splittings of the matching sequence or choices of thick forms only change the eliminated block by a shear. More explicitly, $J$ projected to the normalized cokernel is the common sextic derivative on branch and time variables; it has image of codimension one and is injective there on those variables. The two attaching columns have zero normalized projection and give $vp$ in matching. Thus entries from the branch and time columns into matching do not affect the determinant, by the block determinant with (2). The deformation spaces for the joins have the same complex-oriented branch, attaching and smoothing parameters. The normalization determinant is $\det D_Z\otimes\det D_U\otimes(\det_{\mathbb R}N_x)^*$ with the complex comparison orientation. The sole change of absolute sign from the identity zero component is $\epsilon(u^*D)\epsilon(D)$. Summation over $m/|\operatorname{Aut}u|$ pointed orbits and then over the $b$ branch values gives (1). Compactness and the lower-genus and proper-divisor bounds are exactly those in [Calculation H1](kernels-in-all-degrees-at-a-cubic-wall.md). In particular the isolated charts exhaust the first possible change.

## Regularity of the two forms

The nonzero conditions $e\notin\mathbb Cv$ and $B(0)v\notin\mathbb Cv$ hold at every marked ramified cubic on a generic path. For the first, use Calculation H2. For the second, augment the joint marked value-difference and derivative test on the cubic by the value of the antilinear coefficient at the base point. Disk-supported variations off the marks kill the annihilating adjoint off the marks. Pure holomorphic jets with independently prescribed marked values and derivatives kill the marked multipliers, since both tests annihilate pulled-back base sections. Elliptic regularity then kills the adjoint across the marks, and free coefficient variation at $x$ kills its multiplier. This is the marked test of Article IV, §2 with the coefficient evaluation of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. For $v\ne0$ the excluded projection of $Bv$ has real codimension two. The real hyperplane $\mathbb R e+\mathbb C v$ contains just one complex line, $\mathbb C v$. Hence $A$ has real rank one. Choose $\beta\ne0$ in its real kernel.

We prove $H(\beta)\ne0$ after a generic small disk perturbation preserving the prescribed graph and its branch jets. On the cubic, adjoin to the ordinary operator the $b$ complex simple-pole planes at the ramifying flags representing its lower branch-motion columns. The resulting smooth-section-plus-poles operator has kernel exactly the prescribed line and cokernel $\mathbb R\psi$ by $W_f$. Adjoin also one real pole column on the unramified sheet $y$ with residue $\beta v$. In a complex-linear holomorphic frame use the cutoff local representative
$$
      R/w-B(0)R\log|w|^2,\qquad R=\beta v.
$$
The off-pole image is $O(1+|\log|w||)$ and lies in every finite $L^t$; the logarithm cancels the $1/\bar w$ forcing. Pairing its image with $\psi$ gives $-\pi\operatorname{Re}\psi(0)(R)$ by the same boundary integral. Per choice of frames this defines a smooth one-real-column family (a change with the same residue changes the correction by a regular Sobolev section, or one may keep the local choices fixed). Quotient the regular domain by the known kernel line. The resulting operator has index zero. Its invertibility is equivalent to $H(\beta)\ne0$, and its kernel and cokernel when defective have dimension one.

Use the disk of complex independence of two cubic sheet heights $s_1,s_2$ to vary the operator by real tensors killing both. In particular all poles, residues, and $\beta$ are fixed. On the punctured Galois closure a prospective extra polar solution has three conjugate sections $h_1,h_2,h_3$. Its new residue must be nonzero. At each of the three flags over $x$, precisely one conjugate has order $z^{-2}$ with nonzero coefficient; its logarithm has lower order. The others have at most simple poles. Thus the three conjugates and the two bounded standard sections have linearly independent real solution germs on the disk by unique continuation. At a Petri jet the following adjoint test applies. An annihilator of the disk variations gives $\sum h_i\otimes\psi_i=\sum_{j=1}^2 s_j\otimes\alpha_j$, where the $\psi_i$ are the three local adjoint components. Pairing this equality with $[D,\Phi]$ for compactly supported complex-linear $\Phi$ shows the $\alpha_j$ solve the adjoint equation, since $\Phi s_1,\Phi s_2$ are arbitrary independent test sections. The Petri jet and independence of the five germs force the adjoint jets to vanish. Unique continuation gives zero adjoint. The density of a Petri jet under these two prescribed sections, by a local complex gauge and cutoff, is proved in [Calculation H1](kernels-in-all-degrees-at-a-cubic-wall.md) using [Wendl, Corollary 5.10](https://www2.mathematik.hu-berlin.de/~wendl/pub/superrigidity14_final.pdf).

Approximate first by a constrained operator with this jet. At any remaining defect the test gives a perturbation with nonzero kernel-to-cokernel derivative for the index-zero quotient. A small multiple removes its simple kernel. This proves open density with fixed graph and fixed $A$. The finitely many marked conditions can be imposed simultaneously. For unrestricted generic paths, each normalized transverse crossing continues uniquely on a marked Hurwitz–path chart. The regularity conditions on these finite tests are open, and density on the chart follows by a time cutoff of the graph-preserving perturbations. A countable cover of the crossing incidence by such continuation charts gives the residual path statement as in Article IV, Appendix E. Intersect with the central classification and discriminant regularity of Calculation H2. This proves the stated generic signed result.
