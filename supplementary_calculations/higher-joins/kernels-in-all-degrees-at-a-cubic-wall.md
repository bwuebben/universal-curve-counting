# H1. Kernels in all covering degrees at a symmetric cubic wall

Fix a complex rank-two bundle $N$ of degree $2g-2$ on a smooth closed curve $C$ of genus $g\ge2$. Let $D_t$ be a fixed-symbol path with invertible base operator and an ordinary transverse $S_3$ cubic crossing $f:Y\to C$ at $t=0$. The branching is simple at distinct values, possibly empty. The kernel on $Y$ is a real line with generator $\xi$, whose graph is embedded. The trace of $\xi$ is zero. Let $Z\to C$ be the compact Galois closure and $V$ the standard real representation of $S_3$. Its three conjugate sections on $Z$ sum to zero and span a copy of $V$ in the kernel. These are the hypotheses and the representation identified in Article IV, §§2 and 4.

**Theorem.** Suppose two lower sheet values form a complex frame on a regular disk. For a residual set of small smooth zeroth-order perturbations supported on that disk and preserving the prescribed graph, the following holds on every smooth connected finite cover $u:X\to C$. Form the joint monodromy of $u$ and $Z$ over their common regular locus. Write $K\le S_3$ for the image of a sheet stabilizer of $X$ in the cubic monodromy. Then $\ker u^*D_0$ is the pullback of the space $V^K$ of lower solutions. Its real dimension is two for $K=1$, one for a transposition subgroup, and zero in the other cases. The description is independent of the chosen sheet up to the natural $S_3$ action.

At every ordinary cubic crossing on a generic path this conclusion holds and evaluation on $V$ is real injective at every regular value of the cubic. Write $b=2l$ for its number of simple branch values and $H_d=1+d(g-1)$. In degrees $d$ indivisible by three the following consequences hold for the connected local changes and, at super-rigid endpoint germs, for the geometric elementary exponents.

- At an unramified crossing the change vanishes in every genus.
- At a ramified crossing the change vanishes in every genus for $d=1,2,4,5$. For larger $d$ indivisible by three it vanishes in every genus $k<H_d+3l+1$. This bound is attained by the central kernel incidence. The Euler weight at equality is undetermined.

Both vanishing statements are coefficientwise local assertions. Intervals and choices of super-rigid ends can be made for each finite list of coefficients as in Article IV, Appendix E.

## Keeping a plane of solutions fixed

Choose a closed subdisk of the regular disk on which two sheet sections $s_1,s_2$ form a complex frame, and work with smooth perturbations supported there. Killing these two vectors as real vectors kills the third, which is $-s_1-s_2$. In particular the solutions are unchanged on the prescribed cubic. Restrict the size of the perturbations to preserve the dimension of its kernel, base invertibility and lower transversality.

The unrestricted infinite-order Petri condition is dense at any specified interior point of a smaller disk. To transfer it to this constrained space, take an unrestricted nearby operator $D'$ with such a jet at the point. For each $i$ solve $D's'_i=0$ locally with $s'_i$ close to $s_i$. The small-disk Cauchy inverse and interior estimates of Calculation G3 apply to both equations. The complex gauge determined by $Gs_i=s'_i$ is close to the identity and invertible on a smaller disk. Conjugating $D'$, taking its difference from $D_0$, and cutting off this difference as in Calculation G3 produces a constrained operator with the same Petri property. [Calculation G3](../transversality/kernels-on-all-finite-covers-with-a-prescribed-double-graph.md) · [Wendl, Corollary 5.10](https://www2.mathematik.hu-berlin.de/~wendl/pub/superrigidity14_final.pdf).

Let $W$ be an irreducible real finite-monodromy system distinct from the prescribed standard system on the punctured curve. Take a common finite normal cover and a nonzero solution of the twisted equation with flat components $w_i$. The sections $w_i,s_1,s_2$ are real linearly independent on the normal cover by irreducibility and inequivalence. Their jets at a regular point are independent by unique continuation. Suppose a twisted adjoint with components $v_i$ annihilates the allowed perturbations in the linearized equation. The pointwise annihilator of real maps killing $s_1,s_2$ gives
$$
                 \sum_i w_i\otimes v_i=s_1\otimes\alpha_1+s_2\otimes\alpha_2.
$$
Both $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ solve the local adjoint equation. To see this, pair the identity with $[D,\Phi]$ for any compactly supported complex-linear endomorphism $\Phi$. Integration against the left side vanishes by the solution and adjoint equations, so $\sum_{j=1}^2\int\langle\alpha_j,D(\Phi s_j)\rangle=0$. Since $s_1,s_2$ form a complex frame, $\Phi s_1$ and $\Phi s_2$ are arbitrary independent compactly supported test sections. The Petri property now forces each adjoint jet on the left to vanish. Adjoint unique continuation gives surjectivity of the constrained universal equation for $W$.

For each compact chart of effective branch data choose the test point in the subdisk away from its branch positions, refining charts when necessary. Projectivizing the nonzero section and moving the $s$ effective punctures gives projection index $2s-1-2\sum_x\operatorname{codim}W^{I_x}\le-1$. Compactness on each block, finite-dimensional Sard and countable intersection give absence of every such $W$ exactly as in Calculation G3. For the excluded isomorphism type, the standard representation is faithful, so equality with the lower system locally fixes its effective branch positions and discrete monodromy. On the common normal cover all copies of the surviving representation descend to $Z$. Each such copy has a one-dimensional invariant space under a sheet transposition; it therefore gives a distinct invariant line of solutions on the prescribed $Y$. Since the entire kernel there is a line, there is exactly one copy. Taking the sheet stabilizer invariants proves the formula for $X$. At nodes one matches the actual evaluated vectors from adjacent positive components; connected contracted blocks carry matching constants.

## Evaluation at the branch values

The marked two-difference evaluation on the ordinary cubic incidence in Article IV, §2 is submersive, including at an arbitrary regular marked base point. In a local labeling its two differences are invertible real linear combinations of two independent conjugate sections in the abstract plane $V$. Real rank one for their values in $N_x$ has real codimension three in the space of pairs of four-vectors. Rank zero has codimension eight. Allowing the regular point to move adds two real dimensions to the zero-dimensional crossing incidence along a path. Transversality to both rank strata therefore gives real rank two at *every* regular value on a generic path. Compact exhaustions away from the branch values suffice. Existence of a disk of complex independence is the separate fixed-mark result of Article IV, §2. Transfer of the constrained exclusions to the continued crossing charts then uses the time cutoff argument of Article IV, Appendix E, since the normalized transverse kernel and cubic cover continue uniquely. If a particular graph is prescribed, the evaluation ranks are hypotheses on that graph.

At a ramification point of $Z$, inertia is generated by a transposition. The evaluation $V\to N_x$ annihilates its negative eigenline. Its rank is exactly one. Indeed the three sheet heights there are $v,v,-2v$, and embeddedness of the graph on $Y$ implies $v\ne0$ by separation of the two points in the cubic fiber. The negative eigenline consists of the multiples of a root such as $(1,-1,0)$ in the sum-zero plane. Each nonzero vector on it has trivial stabilizer as a vector because its three coordinates are distinct. A nonzero vector with transposition stabilizer has two equal coordinates.

It follows that every nonzero component factors through a cubic quotient $Z/\langle\tau\rangle$ or through $Z$ itself. More precisely, its coefficient vector is fixed by the image of its sheet stabilizer, so the component factors through the quotient of $Z$ by the stabilizer of that vector. The resulting degree over $C$ is divisible by three. If the section has a zero then a branch value is necessary and its coefficient is on a negative eigenline there. The component factors through $Z$ and has degree divisible by six. Projectivization does not enlarge the vector stabilizer used in the descent of a section. In the unramified case, real injectivity on every fiber embeds each nonzero orbit multisection, and images of different orbits are disjoint. Matching on a connected domain then confines the whole graph to one such cubic or sextic curve.

## The first possible joins outside degree divisible by three

For a connected stable map with nonzero normal kernel and $3\nmid d$, some positive component has zero height, since the sum of the degrees of nonzero components is divisible by three. By connectedness a nonzero positive component must meet height zero, directly or through a constant contracted block. At an unramified crossing this is impossible. At a ramified crossing it requires at least one component factoring through $Z$, and also a zero-height positive component. This excludes $d<7$.

There are three simple ramification points of $Z$ above every lower branch value, so $g(Z)=H_6+3l$. For a component of degree $6e$ factoring through $Z$, Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus at least $H_{6e}+3el$. Every other positive component of degree $j$ has genus at least $H_j$. If there are $n\ge2$ positive components, adding genera gives $k-H_d\ge 3el+n-1\ge3l+1$. At equality one has $e=1$, $n=2$, no further ramification, no contracted genus, and no cycle in the dual graph. Stability excludes contracted rational vertices in a tree with only two positive vertices. Hence the source is $Z$ joined at a single node to a connected unramified degree-$(d-6)$ cover. Its height on $Z$ lies on the negative eigenline at the joining flag. Conversely these maps have a kernel for every allowed $d$ at this genus, by taking a ramification point of $Z$ and a preimage of its value on the chosen unramified cover.

Absence of a central projective kernel on the compact fixed-degree, fixed-genus stable-map space implies local constancy of the connected coefficient. For the exponent use the geometric endpoint recursion of Article III, §3. Every divisor of a degree indivisible by three is again indivisible by three. In the unramified case induct first on degree, then on genus. In the ramified case a proper divisor at which a change can occur has excess at least $3l+1$, and its $j$-fold multiple starts at excess at least $j(3l+1)$ for $j\ge2$. This proves the asserted primitive vanishing through the indicated open range, as well as in all genera for $d=1,2,4,5$. For two lower branch values over a genus-two curve, the first possible genus in degree seven is twelve. At equality the same proper-divisor bound shows that the primitive change equals the connected change whenever the latter is compared at super-rigid ends. Computing that common value requires the local wall degree of the sextic join.
