# D3. Real spin operators at finite contacts

We compute a finite-dimensional reduction of the actual real spin operator through a nonseparating degeneration of the target. We use the exceptional target spin of [Calculation D1](even-contact-spin-matching.md). Its two gluings include the two distinct even target spins. The expansion of [Calculation D2](contact-two-elliptic-tail.md) is the case of one contact of order two.

## Finite-cover theorem

Let $f:Y\to B$ be a finite map of smooth connected compact complex curves. Let $B$ have genus at least one. Choose two distinct target points and pair their preimages with the same contact orders $m_1,\ldots,m_n$, including the contacts of order one. Write $m=\sum_i m_i$, $M=m-n$. Keep all other ramification, of total degree $2r$, in a fixed complement of the plumbing discs. Fix branch coordinates, smoothing roots, and an isometry of the two spin data on the complement. The target plumbing is $xy=t$. Choose the semistable spin target with parameter $\delta^2=t$, with exceptional spin of degree one on its rational component and square root $A_0^2=K_B$ on $B$. Write $L$ for its pullback to the expanded source. Choose smooth positive Hermitian metrics on these line bundles on the family, with common restriction on $Y$. For a deck quotient take deck-invariant metrics, obtained by averaging over the finite group. The operator on smooth fibers is $D=\bar\partial+c\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the spin square map followed by the cover differential and $c$ is metric conjugation. Orient its determinant by continuation from $\bar\partial$.

Work on a framed disk with $s_i^{m_i}=t$, $s_i=d_i^2$. We may take $t=q^{2N}$, $N=\operatorname{lcm}(m_i)$, and $d_i=\gamma_i q^{N/m_i}$ for fixed compatible roots. The normalized source acquires a two-pointed rational bridge at each pair. Finite-dimensional reduction by stabilization of the central cokernel gives a real map
$$
 T_q(c)=b_q\big((d_i^{\nu_{ij}}c_{ij})_{i,j}\big),\qquad
 \nu_{ij}=\min(j,m_i-j),\qquad 1\le j<m_i.                    \tag{1}
$$
Here $b_q:\mathbb C^M\to V$ is a continuous family of real-linear injections for small $q$, including zero. The real space $V$ represents the cokernel on the normalization, of rank $2M+2r$. The map $b_0$ is, up to the common stabilization isomorphism, real projection of a specified isotropic subspace of the ramification residue space. We calculate it below. In particular, on an étale smoothing ($r=0$) the absolute real determinant of this finite block is a continuous positive unit times
$$
                    \prod_i |d_i|^{2\lfloor m_i^2/4\rfloor}.
                                                               \tag{2}
$$
This exponent uses the displayed coordinates, the stated trivializations of the finite-dimensional reduction, and the balanced parameters $d_i$. It describes the vanishing of the determinant of that map, as distinct from singular values in a degenerating $L^2$ metric.

For $r=0$ the relative sign between the two target-spin gluings on the same smoothing is
$$
                        (-1)^{\#\{i:m_i\ {\rm even}\}}
                        =(-1)^{m-n}.                            \tag{3}
$$
The two target spins have equal parity, hence their mixed sign on the degree-one core is positive. Formula (3) describes the determinant contribution in this profile, on either labeling of the roots. The absolute étale sign already follows from Article III, Proposition 14.2. The new information is the real map through the central fiber, its orders, and the specific residual directions on every bridge. Positive residual ramification gives the rectangular map (1), with a remaining cokernel of real rank $2r$. Its determinant orientations for the two spin choices also differ by (3), using their common complex orthogonal residue identification off the neck. This compares oriented spaces even when their Euler classes vanish.

## 1. The central Fredholm family

The $i$-th neck has $uv=d_i^2$, $x=u^{m_i}, y=v^{m_i}$. Insert the bridge with coordinate $Z=u/d_i$, so $v=d_i/Z$. The two node charts have $u\xi=d_i$, $v\zeta=d_i$ with $\xi=1/Z$, $\zeta=Z$. The map to the target bridge in coordinate $x/\delta$ is a unit times $Z^{m_i}$. On this bridge the target-pulled spin has degree $m_i$. Its branch frames satisfy
$$
        \ell_y=\kappa_i Z^{-m_i}\ell_x,\qquad
        \kappa_i=\varepsilon i\delta/d_i^{m_i},\qquad
        \kappa_i^2=-1 ,
$$
where $\varepsilon=\pm1$ labels the target gluings. The frames square to $dx,dy$ on the smooth target. On $Y$ the operator is the ordinary spin operator for $f^*A_0$ and $df$. It is injective by the spin norm identity. Its complexified cokernel is the orthogonal residue space $E$ at the ramification points. The actual real cokernel is a positive real form of $E$, with the universal normalization of the residue pairing in Article V, §§4–6. On a rational bridge the operator is $\bar\partial_{\mathcal O(m_i)}$. Holomorphic sections evaluate onto the two endpoint fibers. Normalization therefore gives the central kernel as the sum of the endpoint-vanishing bridge sections, with basis $Z^{-j}\ell_x$, $1\le j<m_i$. Its cokernel is the real cokernel on $Y$.

We use the norm-continuous nodal Dolbeault realization for prestable curves with $p>2$. Its normalized central sections have matching values at the nodes and the charts control the sup norm uniformly. In the half-node coordinate $z$ with $zw=d_i$ and $|z|\ge|w|$, a bounded smooth ambient one-form $F\,dz+G\,dw$ has bounded relative coefficient $F-Gd_i/z^2$. In the charts for forms the collar of radius $a$ has measure $O(a)$ in $d|z|\,d\arg z$, uniformly in $d_i$. Thus ambient forms restrict continuously in the family $L^p$ norm by convergence off shrinking collars and an $O(a^{1/p})$ bound on them. Multiplication by an ambient coefficient of this kind, followed by metric conjugation on the line, is norm-continuous from the section spaces by their uniform sup-norm control. This also applies after the ramified base changes $d_i=d_i(q)$. The spin mass coefficient comes from precisely such forms: for instance its holomorphic factor in the left frame is $m_i u^{m_i-1}du$. It restricts to zero on the central bridge. Therefore the unmodified $D$ and its complex-to-real mass homotopy are continuous Fredholm families. These assertions concern continuous finite-dimensional stabilization. No differentiability of a Banach trivialization at the node is needed. [Siebert, §§4.1–4.4, Corollaries 4.7 and 4.11, Proposition 4.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/dg-ga/9608005v2)

Choose a real space $V$ of smooth forms supported in the fixed complement projecting isomorphically onto the central cokernel. Such forms span it by unique continuation for the adjoint operator on $Y$. Use the same space for both spin gluings. On each bridge with $m_i>1$ evaluate at $m_i-1$ distinct fixed points of $\mathbb C^*$ in the left frame. The evaluation matrix on the listed basis is invertible by polynomial interpolation. Its inverse defines a map $e_q$ to $\mathbb C^M$ in a neighborhood of zero. The augmented operator
$$
       G_q(h,v)=(D_qh+v, e_q(h))
$$
is an isomorphism at $q=0$ by the kernel and cokernel calculation, hence on a sufficiently small disk. Evaluation at these smooth sections is norm-continuous, using the charts on fixed bridge interiors. We can take the marked points at constant $Z$ for every $q$. This auxiliary evaluation constructs a reduction on the framed chart. The determinant signs use the same complex orientation on each interpolation space.

## 2. Computing the actual reduced map

Choose a trial section $k_{ij,q}$ equal to $Z^{-j}\ell_x$ throughout the middle of the bridge and cut off to zero in fixed annuli on the two normalized branches. Thus near the left node its coefficient is $\xi^j$, and near the right node it is $\kappa_i^{-1}\zeta^{m_i-j}$. On the two fixed cutoff annuli these coefficients are $d_i^j/u^j$ and $d_i^{m_i-j}/(\kappa_i v^{m_i-j})$. It is a smooth section through the central fiber, and the cutoffs can be chosen equal to one at the auxiliary evaluations. For arbitrary $\alpha\in\mathbb C$ consider the inhomogeneous term on nonzero fibers
$$
                F_{ij,q}(\alpha)=D_q k_{ij,q}(\alpha/d_i^{\nu_{ij}}),
$$
where the argument multiplies the trial section *before* applying the real operator. The forcing has a continuous limit, although the divided section itself need not have one. The Dolbeault term is the derivative of a fixed cutoff times the stated polar coefficient on each annulus, divided by $d_i^{\nu_{ij}}$. For the mass term the two holomorphic expressions before applying $c$ and the cutoff are
$$
 m_i\alpha\,d_i^{j-\nu_{ij}}u^{m_i-1-j}\,du/\ell_x,\qquad
 m_i\alpha\,\kappa_i^{-1}d_i^{m_i-j-\nu_{ij}}v^{j-1}\,dv/\ell_y.
$$
All displayed powers are nonnegative. The expressions agree in their respective frames on the overlap. On the central bridge their relative one-forms vanish. The ambient-form estimate in §1 proves the required norm limit on the whole core.

Set $b_q(\alpha)=\operatorname{pr}_V G_q^{-1}(-\sum F_{ij,q}(\alpha_{ij}),0)$, using the same definition with the limiting forms at zero. For an anchor vector $c$ use the trial $\sum c_{ij} k_{ij,q}$ and solve for the correction of zero anchor with $G_q^{-1}$. The resulting stabilized section has anchor $c$. Its stabilizing coefficient is exactly (1). This proves the factorization over the punctured disk and gives $T_0=0$. In particular it gives the leading term of an actual real Fredholm reduction without an exchange of gluing and spectral limits.

The class of each limiting force in the cokernel on $Y$ has a direct description. Write $a$ for its meromorphic polar section with the branch coefficients given below. If $\eta$ denotes the cutoff, the pair
$$
             (\bar\partial(\eta a),\ \sigma\eta a)
$$
is a smooth closed cochain of the Dolbeault spin complex on $Y$. In the first entry the derivative is taken off the poles and extended by zero near them. No distributional point mass is taken. Its residue class is the prescribed polar part. Under projection to the fixed real harmonic space the class maps, up to the common factor two, to the cokernel class of $\bar\partial(\eta a)+c(\sigma\eta a)$. This follows from the chain-level involution $(\beta,v)\mapsto(c(v),c^{-1}(\beta))$ and the description of the image of a coboundary in Article V, §§4–6. This is exactly the class represented by the limiting forcing. In passing to $b_0$ there is also a common minus sign from the augmentation.

Writing $k=m_i$, the surviving polar vectors, in the two branch frames in the order $(u,v)$, are
$$
 \begin{cases}
   (u^{-j},0), &2j<k,\\
   (u^{-j},\kappa_i^{-1}v^{-j}),&2j=k,\\
   (0,\kappa_i^{-1}v^{-(k-j)}),&2j>k.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{4}
$$
At one branch the form on pole orders $j,j'$ takes value $k$ when $j+j'=k$ and zero otherwise, by the residue of $k u^{k-1-j-j'}du$. Thus (4) spans a maximal isotropic in the two branch residue spaces of this contact. The middle vector is null because $1+\kappa_i^{-2}=0$. Distinct contacts and the other ramification points are mutually orthogonal. The direct sum $U_\varepsilon$ of (4) has dimension $M$ and is isotropic in $E$. Projection from it onto the positive real form is real-injective because the imaginary multiple of a positive real form contains no nonzero null vector. This proves injectivity of $b_0$ and hence of $b_q$ near zero. For $r=0$ it is an isomorphism. The sum of the orders $\nu_{ij}$ at a part $k$ equals $\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor$, proving (2).

In the étale case $U_+$ and $U_-$ are maximal isotropics. Their intersection has codimension one for each even contact and zero for each odd contact. In a hyperbolic basis each exchanged null coordinate changes a complex orientation by conjugation in projection to the common positive real form. Right-branch factors $\kappa_i^{-1}$ and changes of complex basis preserve real orientation. The common complement on $Y$ is oriented identically for the two central determinant comparisons: the bridge Dolbeault determinants, endpoint evaluations and all anchor identifications at mass zero are complex, and varying the mass on $Y$ contributes the same determinant orientation to each. Equivalently, compare with the absolute smooth sign in Article III, Proposition 14.2. We obtain (3). Relabeling branches or changing an individual smoothing-root unit changes the basis or the common labels and preserves the sign ratio. No stack degree or count of smoothing roots enters (1)–(3).

For $r>0$ the orientations can be compared on the actual residual cokernels. Fix a maximal isotropic $U_R$ in the common off-neck residue space. In disjoint discs at its support, represent a vector by meromorphic polar sections $a$ and form the smooth right-hand side $F_R=\bar\partial(\eta a)+c(\sigma\eta a)$ as above, with a sum over the discs. These forms vary continuously through the degeneration because their support is in the fixed complement. On a smooth fiber their cokernel classes are twice the real projections of $U_R$ into the positive real form of that fiber's off-neck residue space. Thus they give a real isomorphism there and specify a common orientation reference via the complex orientation of $U_R$. This reference is defined separately for the two positive real forms using the same $U_R$.

Transport these forms to $V$ by $v_R(q)=\operatorname{pr}_V G_q^{-1}(F_R,0)$. Modulo the image of $T_q$ this is precisely their class in the true cokernel, since the augmented equation for the first component is $D_q h+v=F_R$. At zero the two blocks $(b_0,v_R(0))$ together project the maximal isotropic $U_\varepsilon\oplus U_R$ to the real cokernel on $Y$, with the same stabilizing sign convention in the first block for both spin choices. This square map is invertible, hence it stays invertible near zero. The determinant exact sequence for the augmented operator compares the orientation induced on the quotient by the first block with the orientations of this square map and its domain. The factors $d_i^{\nu_{ij}}$ are orientation preserving for $q\ne0$. The central determinant identifications are common for the two gluings by complex normalization at mass zero followed by the same mass path on $Y$. Thus the orientation ratio on the smooth residual cokernels, in the common reference just specified, is the relative projection sign of $U_+\oplus U_R$ and $U_-\oplus U_R$. Their intersection again has codimension the number of even contacts. This proves (3) for arbitrary off-neck ramification. The ratio is independent of the choice of $U_R$.

## 3. A regular real extension on the expanded family

Put $l_i=\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor$ and $b_i=m_i-2l_i$. On the two end charts use $n_x=\ell_x/u^{l_i}$ and $n_y=\ell_y/v^{l_i}$ as new frames, glued to the original line on the complement. On the bridge use the transition $n_y=\kappa_i Z^{-b_i}n_x$. Indeed $u/v=Z^2$. This defines a line $H$ of degree $b_i$ on each bridge. The inclusion $I:L\to H$ has coefficients $u^{l_i}$ and $v^{l_i}$ on the respective ends and is an isomorphism on smooth fibers. This description by frames works on the base-changed node charts even when their total space is singular. In the one-bridge balanced model $H=L(l_iT_0)$.

Transport the unmodified operator on $L$ by $I$ on smooth fibers. In the $n_x$ frame its antilinear coefficient is
$$
         m_i h_L^{-1}u^{l_i}\bar u^{m_i-1-l_i}\,d\bar u ,
$$
where $h_L$ is the squared norm of $\ell_x$. This follows by multiplying $m_i h_L^{-1}\bar u^{m_i-1}d\bar u$ by $u^{l_i}/\bar u^{l_i}$. The expression extends as an ordinary smooth ambient form and vanishes on the central bridge after restriction, including at contact order one where $du$ itself restricts to zero on that component. The right chart gives the same construction. Section 1 gives a continuous real Fredholm extension $D_H$, with $D_H=I D_L I^{-1}$ on smooth fibers.

On $Y$ away from the ends the spin square on $H$ gives a differential $\sigma_H w^2$ for a kernel section $w$. In the $n_x$ frame $\sigma_H=m_i u^{m_i-1-2l_i}du$. It has a logarithmic pole at even contacts and is regular at odd contacts. On each even bridge a kernel section is constant in the trivial line. The two limiting residues of $\sigma_H w^2$ there sum to zero by the frame transition and $\kappa_i^2=-1$. The odd contacts contribute no residues. On the punctured normalization the equation implies that $\bar\partial(\sigma_H w^2)$ is a fixed nonzero scalar times the nonnegative spin energy density. In a local holomorphic frame the latter is the squared norm of $\sigma_H w$ times $|u|^{2l_i}h_L^{-1}$ with the smooth coordinate area measure. The density is integrable, including at each logarithmic pole since then $l_i\ge1$. Elliptic regularity for the extended ordinary coefficient gives smooth values of $w$ on the normalization. Integration over $Y$ with the branch discs removed gives zero by the residue cancellation. Hence $w$ vanishes on $Y$. On a bridge it is now a section of $\mathcal O(b_i)$ vanishing at both endpoints, hence zero for $b_i=0,1$. This proves injectivity of $D_H$ on the central core. The real core index is $-2r$, the same as on the smooth fibers. For an étale smoothing $D_H$ is invertible on the whole disk; in general its real cokernel has constant rank $2r$.

For a persistent contracted one-pointed tail, normalize the attaching node. The constant tail sections evaluate isomorphically to its matching fiber. When the core is invertible the actual tailed cokernel is therefore $H^1(F,\mathcal O_F)\otimes H|_{\rm mark}$ as a real vector space, oriented by its complex orientation times the core determinant sign. This is also an isomorphism of families: after eliminating the core by its inverse, a tail primitive with a given right-hand side can have its constant adjusted to match any prescribed core value, and the quotient is its arithmetic $H^1$. This works through nodal tails by ordinary Dolbeault normalization or cohomology and base change. To compactify the moving section over a core node, take the pointed semistabilization, which inserts a two-ended rational curve carrying the mark. Pull back the core line and operator coefficients by its contraction. The pullback coefficient on that rational curve vanishes and holomorphic constants enforce exactly the previous endpoint match. The ambient-form estimate also applies after this modification, by writing a pulled-back ambient coefficient in each new plumbing chart. Consequently the description is continuous on the proper family of attaching points, including the bridge ends. It concerns the chosen semistabilization over the specified spin target. Further expansions in a relative stable-map compactification require their own comparisons.

## 4. Where the two Euler extensions differ

Specialize to the connected degree-two étale smoothing of Calculation D2, with smooth target genus $g$, its single order-two contact and a persistent contracted elliptic tail. On the smooth balanced attachment surface $\mathcal S$ let $T_0$ be the reduced rational bridge, with normal line $N$ of degree $-2$. Consider the product with $\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}$ and, for stack degree, take the quotient by the deck involution of the family. Write $j$ for inclusion of the bridge product, $h_i=c_1(H_i)$ for the two evaluated source extensions and $\lambda$ for the Hodge class. On the whole space the target evaluation lines have Chern classes $h_i-[T_0]$ by the two inclusions $L_i=H_i(-T_0)$. The source choice of §3 gives an actual oriented cokernel bundle. For the target choice the formula below uses the Hodge factor from separating the tail, with the smooth signed orientation $\sigma$; the target-pulled core itself has the kernel jump in (1).

In rational Chow or cohomology the difference of these two signed Hodge Euler extensions, target minus source, is
$$
 \sigma\big[(h_1-\lambda-[T_0])(h_2-\lambda-[T_0])
              -(h_1-\lambda)(h_2-\lambda)\big]
      =\sigma\,j_*\big(2\lambda+c_1(N)\big).                  \tag{5}
$$
Indeed $h_i|_{T_0}=0$ rationally, and the self-intersection formula gives $[T_0]^2=j_*c_1(N)$. This specifies a supported representative via the actual evaluation lines and Cartier divisor. For example, restriction to the bridge followed by integration gives $-\sigma/12$: multiply by $c_1(N)$ of degree $-2$, use $\int\lambda=1/24$, and divide once by the deck group order two. On the main normalization the difference is $+\sigma/12$. The same calculation on the marking blowup of Calculation D2 uses its $(-1)$-curve with multiplicity two in the fiber. The mixed pair has $\sigma=-1$ and the absolute smooth total per root is $+(g-1)/12$. These component integrals and their common total now have a regular real-operator model on the source side and an explicit Fredholm reduction on the target side.

## 5. A compact attachment calculation in every even degree

For a single full contact of even order $m$ there are examples in every target genus $g\ge 2$. On the target normalization $B$ choose a line whose $m$-th power is $\mathcal O_B(p+(m-1)q')$ at the two prescribed points. Such a line exists by divisibility of $\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)$. Take the normalization of the cyclic root cover of its distinguished section. Full ramification at $p$ forces connectedness. The characters of the deck generator on the local source coordinates at the two ends are inverses, so the cyclic action preserves the plumbing parameter and extends to each smoothed cover. These deck transformations exhaust the degree-$m$ cover automorphisms. The smoothing is étale and (3) gives its mixed sign $-1$ for the exceptional target-spin pair. Choosing an even $A_0$ realizes two even target spins.

Over the compact attachment family with a pointed elliptic tail, the real source extension of §3 places degree $m(g-1)$ per evaluation line on the normalization and zero on the reduced bridge. In the target-pulled Hodge factor the corresponding degrees are $m(g-2)$ and $m$. Indeed the spin line on the target bridge has degree one and the bridge map has degree $m$. On the deck quotient one integrates $+\lambda$ times the sum of these degrees and divides by $m$. Therefore the smooth signed tail total per prescribed smoothing root is $+(g-1)/12$ for every even $m$. The target Hodge factor assigns $+1/12$ of this to the bridge and the source cokernel assigns zero there. This recovers the degree-two test and explains why the difference on the bridge is independent of covering degree in this cyclic family. The smooth total also follows from the spin-residue calculation in Article V, §§4–6.

The finite-cover theorem computes the predicted sign and the disappearing bridge modes for every partition on the stated chart. Section 3 provides a regular real extension for a separating tail on its pointed semistabilization. Passing to a degeneration formula for the original invariants still requires descent and compatibility across relative charts, the other stable-map strata and the relative virtual cycle. Neither root counts nor virtual multiplicities are supplied by the orders in (2).
