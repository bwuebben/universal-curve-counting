# D5. Collision divisors in the relative first-excess formula

Let a smooth genus-$g$ target degenerate to an irreducible curve with one ordinary node, $g\ge2$. Its normalization is $(B,p,q)$. In degree $m\ge2$ and source genus $2+m(g-1)$, the normal Euler integral for a pair of spin lines splits into a finite-map term and an elliptic-tail term. We compute the specialization of the signed collision divisors representing the finite-map term. For double covers the coefficient on each nodal divisor also has a real-operator interpretation on a neighborhood of the compact divisor. The smooth-target divisor theorem and its numerical evaluations are in Article V, §§4–6.

## The cut formula

Choose a projective one-parameter smoothing over $R=\mathbb C[[t]]$ transverse to the nodal boundary. Use ordered target branches $xy=t$ and ordinary expanded maps. The two exceptional spin gluings of [Calculation D1](even-contact-spin-matching.md) agree off the neck and differ by the class dual to its vanishing circle. For this pair set $\chi(\mu)=(-1)^{m-\ell(\mu)}$ on a seam profile $\mu=(k_1,\ldots,k_n)$. For a repeated spin set $\chi=1$. Write $C_\mu=\prod_i k_i$ and $\kappa=c_1(K_B(p+q))$.

Cut the target node. Include in the cut data a matching of the fibers over $p,q$ with the same prescribed profile $\mu$. Take the quotient by all automorphisms preserving the matching. Keep all cut sources for which the paired source is connected. In the cut relative stack let $J$ range over the components of the following two reduced collision curves, completed by closure, with their matching data.

* On the nodal curve $N$ of the Hurwitz surface, identify an unordered pair of distinct unramified sheets over one ordinary target value. The normalized cover is unramified away from the two relative fibers. It can be disconnected.
* On the triple curve $T$ of that surface, one point over an ordinary target value has ramification index three. All other ramification is prescribed at the two relative fibers.

Write $e_J:J\to B$ for the contracted image of the collision and $h_J=1$ on $N$, $h_J=3$ on $T$. Let $\Phi$ denote the gluing map, including the inclusion of the indicated collision cycle. Then the specialization of the smooth signed-divisor representative of the finite-map Euler product is the following rational zero-cycle class:
$$
 -\frac18\sum_{\mu} C_\mu\chi(\mu)
       \sum_{J\text{ with profile }\mu}\frac{1}{h_J}\,
         \Phi_*\big(e_J^*\kappa\cap[J]\big).                       \tag{1}
$$
One may push from the normalizations of the components. At a point where several branches of the same type meet, the normal line used in (1) belongs to the *total* reduced collision divisor of that type. The formula is for the ordinary target parameter. On a splitting spin or root disk use its actual degree over the chosen parameter as in [Calculation D4](virtual-gluing-for-one-elliptic-tail.md). There is no further factor for permutations of indices on an already matched cover.

In particular the proper cut geometry determines separately the nodal and triple contributions. In the first ramified source genus these exhaust the smooth finite-map integral. Together with the one-tail term of §5 this gives a numerical degeneration formula for this entire genus in every degree. At the level of rational Chow groups (1) specializes a specified algebraic representative. The real smooth Euler class is identified with it in rational cohomology. We do not assume a Chow-valued real Euler class or an all-profile compact real obstruction bundle.

## 1. Collision curves on a smooth target

On a smooth target $C$, the proper finite-map locus is a smooth surface of the expected dimension. Its nodal and triple curves and the curves where two distinct simple ramification points have equal image are smooth and pairwise disjoint. Article V, §§4–6 identifies the spin Euler class with $-1/4$ times the sum of the nodal and triple divisor classes, weighted by the respective single-spin signs. The two-simple-point collision carries zero coefficient. Let $D$ run through the nodal and triple curves and $\nu_D$ be the corresponding normal line on the surface. A rational algebraic representative for the product of the two smooth Euler classes is
$$
  Z_\eta=\frac1{16}\sum_D s_a(D)s_b(D)\,i_{D*}
                           \big(c_1(\nu_D)\cap[D]\big).           \tag{2}
$$
Take the formula on the generic fiber of the algebraic family after a finite extension splitting the component and spin labels. It determines a rational class on the generic fiber before splitting by transfer of the invariant weighted sum. Its cycle class on smooth complex fibers is the actual Euler product by the cited theorem. The normal-line computation below also constructs (2) by evaluating the target cotangent on the collision curve.

For a nodal map the signs come from the étale cover obtained by normalizing the ordinary node. The product is the sign of its permutation around the vanishing circle for the mixed pair. This includes disconnected normalizations by direct sum. For a triple ramification point $r$ the signs are the spin parities of $f^*a(r)$ and $f^*b(r)$. The orthogonal direct image $f_*\mathcal O(r)$ has determinant character equal to the sign of sheet monodromy off the branch value, and the three-cycle there is even. The mod-two index gives the same product sign around the vanishing circle. The exceptional spin pair has equal parity, so the base parity contributes $+1$. This proves the factor $\chi$ for both types. At a triple point on an inserted target line the permutations about its two ends may have different cycle types. Their signs agree by the product relation with the three-cycle at its interior branch value. For the determinant signs and their expression in sheet monodromy, see Article III, Proposition 14.2, and Article V, Lemma 6.2.

## 2. Keeping the collision on the expanded target

First mark the collision value on the target. Normalize the ordinary source node on $N$ and mark the unordered pair over that value. On $T$ retain the triple ramification point. The resulting covers are admissible covers with fixed branch data and one additional marked target value. In the nodal case two smooth sheets of this auxiliary cover will be identified. Properness of admissible covers gives limits after finite base change, with finite choices for a fixed marked stable target. Disconnected auxiliary covers are handled by taking the corresponding products and finite quotients. Conversely the distinguished nodes can be smoothed by the usual contact equations and the cover away from them follows by étaleness off the branch sections. At the triple section the prescribed map is the constant local model $z\mapsto z^3$. This gives smoothings of each of these marked covers. [Cavalieri–Markwig–Ranganathan, §§2.2, 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.4626).

As the sole marked value approaches the seam the stable marked target has one inserted rational line carrying it at $W=1$, with ends $0,\infty$. In the nodal case its normalized inverse image is a union of rational power covers. Identify the two chosen preimages of 1, on the same or on different components. In the triple case its inverse image is a cover branched only at $0,1,\infty$, with one three-cycle over 1. The main cover is allowed different profiles at the two sides of the line. Forgetting auxiliary marks gives stable expanded maps because the distinguished collision fixes the target dilation. A level containing only two-ended power covers is unstable and contracts. There are finitely many choices with the value fixed at the seam. There is a unique ordinary node or a unique triple point away from the distinguished nodes, and its image recovers the forgotten mark. Thus these constructions describe all limits of both collision loci in the expanded-map stack. Their supports remain disjoint. The closure of the two-simple-point collision with a common value can be treated by retaining its two distinct relative ramification marks in this same construction. It keeps two simple points over the smooth marking on the inserted level.

Here are the actual transverse deformations. Use labeled étale charts for the following local computations. At a seam of the expanded target with contact orders $u_i$ the distinguished source and target smoothing parameters satisfy $a_i^{u_i}=c_1$. On a second seam use $b_j^{v_j}=c_2$ with $t=c_1c_2$. Let $P$ denote their complete local ring with these equations, taking only the one seam for a cut endpoint. The complete local ring of the stable map at the collision on the inserted line is $P[[d]]$. The collision is $d=0$. For an ordinary collision away from the seams the same description has a further coordinate for its moving value and uses the distinguished nodes that are actually present there.

Indeed, after all distinguished nodes are held fixed, deformations of each normalized cover with all branching retained at marked sections have no relative tangent or obstruction over its marked target: the logarithmic differential on the source to the pullback of the logarithmic target tangent line is an isomorphism. Mark all preimages of each prescribed branch value in making this computation; the unramified ones follow uniquely. On a two-ended power component source scalings account for its scalar. The remaining local deformations in which the collision changes are those of an unramified pair joined at an ordinary node, or of one index-three point. At the former the local map is $w=u+v$, $uv=0$. Its finite flat algebra over $\mathbb C[[w]]$ has equation $u^2-wu=0$ and relative cotangent differential $2u-w$. At the latter it is $w=z^3$ with differential $3z^2$. The nonzerodivisor in either presentation gives a length-two local deformation space and no local obstruction. The local-to-global sequence (or these charts glued to the log-unramified complement) gives the center motion and the transverse deformation. The target dilation removes the center motion on the rubber. Models around 1 are
$$
                W=1+u+v,\quad uv=d,
             \qquad W=1+z^3+dz.                                   \tag{3}
$$
They glue to the given cover on punctured discs over formal bases. In the first case the two étale sheets there lift uniquely, and in the second one solves $z'^3+dz'=z^3$ by formal étaleness with $z$ invertible. The distinguished discs are disjoint from these charts and use the pure power plumbings. This gives an explicit family over $P[[d]]$.

For completeness, in a hull $A$ choose distinguished parameters satisfying the power equations, absorbing unit factors by roots over the complete complex ring. Predeformability imposes these equations over Artin bases. The previous tangent calculation leaves at most one further parameter after their differentials vanish. At an ordinary node use the ordinary smoothing parameter. At a triple point take the discriminant of the length-two critical algebra over the base near the marked point. It is finite free by Weierstrass preparation on the source differential. In (3) that algebra is defined by $3z^2+d=0$ and its trace discriminant is $-4d/3$. This is a primitive equation. The discriminant of the two *target values* has order three. These facts give a surjection $P[[d]]\to A$ by cotangent generation and completeness. The explicit family gives the reverse map. Their composite induces an isomorphism on cotangents (intrinsic, labeled distinguished node parameters up to units, and the nonzero transverse parameter in (3)). A surjective endomorphism of a Noetherian ring is injective, proving the hull assertion. The pure-contact equations and an analogous hull argument appear in [Calculation D4](virtual-gluing-for-one-elliptic-tail.md) and [Li, Definition 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0009097#page=19).

Each cut hull has pure dimension two, and is reduced and smooth on its dense interior. Near these curves the ordinary tangential virtual class agrees in top dimension with the fundamental cycle. Indeed the relative virtual dimension is two by Riemann–Hurwitz with the prescribed contact conditions, also for disconnected cut sources using the sum of their Euler characteristics. Away from the contact boundary the displayed local deformations have that dimension and the obstruction theory has the expected rank. The contact boundary in these charts has lower dimension. The same argument in dimension three applies to the total target smoothing. A Cartier intersection on the collision curves can therefore use their actual smoothing parameter in the ordinary space.

## 3. The two transverse lines and specialization

On the moving collision let $L$ be the tangent line of the expanded target at its smooth image point. Relative to a cut, its dual is $e_J^*K_B(p+q)$. To see the identification, trivialize the logarithmic differential by $dx/x$ at $p$. With $x=c_1W$ it restricts along target fibers to $dW/W$ at the moving point, including $W=1$ at $c_1=0$, as a relative logarithmic differential. The other end gives the negative logarithmic frame, and ordinary differentials give the frames off the seams. Thus one obtains the pullback of the target log canonical line by contraction. On the total degeneration the same construction uses $\omega_{\mathcal W/R}$ of the unexpanded target, with opposite residues on the two branches. This also gives an operational extension on the marked cover families.

On the nodal collision the smoothing normal is the tensor product of the two source tangents. Both differentials are invertible at the ordinary node branches, so the normal is $L^2$. Exchanging the two branches fixes their tensor under the natural permutation. On the triple collision, let $S$ be the source tangent at the triple point. The third jet gives $S^3\simeq L$. The derivative variation in (3) identifies the normal with $\operatorname{Hom}(S,L)$: the variation of the critical value is the constant term, and moving the source point contributes zero to its first derivative at order three. Thus its cube is $L^2$. These are identifications of lines on the collision with their stabilizer actions. They hold after labeling and descend, or may be used after finite transfer. In particular the normal to the total Cartier divisor of type $N$ or $T$ has
$$
            c_1(\nu)|_J=-\frac{2}{h_J}\,e_J^*\kappa.              \tag{4}
$$
The calculation applies on the normalization of $J$ if several branches meet. A self-intersection on the smooth fiber can be written as intersection of an individual curve with the total Cartier divisor of its type, since the distinct smooth-fiber curves are disjoint. This avoids any need for individual branches to be Cartier on a singular cut chart.

To specialize (2), carry the collision value and the data of §2 on the proper marked-cover closures, or their normalizations, and push forward. The generic class there uses the operational class $-2c_1(\omega_{\mathcal W/R})/h_J$ evaluated at the collision. It extends by the preceding calculation. At the generic point of a central cut collision curve the value is away from $p,q$ and the ring transverse to the central fiber of the collision family has $s_i^{k_i}=t$. Its Cartier fiber at zero has length $C_\mu$ by the tensor-product monomial basis. At a rubber limiting point the two Cartier sides can have different contact products. Their intersection is zero-dimensional in the central collision locus and gives no additional one-cycle. This describes the cycle before capping with the operational class, including possible root permutations. A matching is retained up to its automorphisms. For all matchings the permutations of equal parts are included by the degree of the matching cover. If numbering the pairs, divide the pushforward of a connected labeling cover of $J$ by its actual generic stack degree over $J$. For a moving branched cover this involves monodromy around the collision-value curve as well as automorphisms of an individual cover. The full cover of simultaneous numberings has degree $\prod_k m_k!$, where $m_k$ is the number of matched pairs of order $k$. For a fixed normalized cover and a varying attachment one recovers the case treated in [Calculation D4](virtual-gluing-for-one-elliptic-tail.md) and the ordinary nonseparating gluing formula of [Bryan–Pandharipande, Appendix A, (16)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0306316#page=26).

On a generic point of each cut component the product of spin signs is $\chi(\mu)$ for **every** root in its fiber, by §1. This justifies attaching that weight to the whole central multiplicity even when the individual signs or root labels do not descend. Refined Cartier specialization commutes with proper pushforward and Chern operations. Applied to (2), (4) and the preceding one-cycle, it gives (1) in rational Chow. An auxiliary finite splitting base can be removed by pushforward and division by its degree, tracking ramification in the Cartier fiber. Equivalently take the complete ordinary root algebra before splitting; the invariant generic weighted cycle defined by transfer has the stated lengths. This comparison uses the geometric divisor representative chosen in (2). The ordinary virtual degeneration formula alone does not supply its signed insertion.

## 4. Two local tests

In degree two there is one unordered pair over each collision value. For the normalized map with seam profile $(1,1)$, the rubber consists initially of two copies of the target line meeting at the pinched pair. For profile $(2)$ the normalized map is $W=Z^2$ and identifies $Z=1,-1$. A model across the transverse smoothing is
$$
      y^2=(W-1)^2-dW \quad\hbox{or}\quad
      y^2=W\big((W-1)^2-dW\big)
$$
respectively, with the usual projective completions. The latter has a genus-one rubber cover for nonzero small $d$. The total framed hulls are $\mathbb C[[a,b,d]]$ with $t=ab$ and $t=(ab)^2$. The second limiting map has *two* involutions, one on the main cover and one on the rubber. Their diagonal remains on a generic gluing, while the other acts by $(a,b,d)\mapsto(-a,-b,d)$. In the first profile the involution interchanging the two rubber sheets must agree on the endpoints with the deck involution on the main cover. These computations include the disconnected étale normalization at profile $(1,1)$.

There is a real realization of the coefficient on a tubular neighborhood of each compact double-cover nodal divisor on the paired cut with its spin roots retained. Use the twisted target plumbing $[UV=\delta\,/\mu_2]$, $x=U^2$, $y=V^2$, $t=\delta^2$, where the group simultaneously negates $U,V$ and the spin frame. The line has square the relative dualizing line. The metric in Calculation D4 gives the ambient antilinear coefficient $2(U\,d\bar U-V\,d\bar V)$. At contact two the source seam is ordinary; at each simple contact use an order-two balanced source seam. On rubber interiors write $c_1=A^2,c_2=B'^2$, $\delta=AB'$ and lift by $(U,V)=(A\sqrt W,B'/\sqrt W)$. The sign ambiguity is precisely the target action. The square root extends on the appropriate uniformizers at the two ends. At $W=1$ it is an étale torsor through the ordinary-node smoothing.

Normalize the ordinary node. At the profile-(2) limit the zero-mass spin on the power sphere is trivial and its constant sections enforce cancellation of the two logarithmic boundary residues on the main curve. At profile $(1,1)$ each power sphere has two stacky ends with sign character and invariant pushforward $\mathcal O(-1)$. Both end values vanish in the sign frame. The spin energy identity on the main components then gives injectivity. The normalized index is zero by smoothing or the invariant normalization sequence. Averaging on the finite uniformizing charts preserves the nodal Fredholm estimates of Calculation D3, and injectivity persists on a neighborhood of the compact divisor. The nodal cokernel for one spin along the divisor is its common evaluation line, twisted by the branch-exchange character, with orientation $s_\theta$ of the invertible normalization. At $Z=\pm1$ the transport in the frames $\sqrt W=Z$ has sign $-1$. Its square is the evaluation of $K_B(p+q)$. Thus the actual Euler restriction is $s_\theta e^*\kappa/2$. From (4) its unique supported lift on the tubular neighborhood is $-s_\theta/4$ times the Thom class: the Thom-to-ordinary map in degree two is an isomorphism there since the compact connected orbicurve has nonzero normal degree. The computation uses rational transfer on the spin refinements. It compares real operators on this neighborhood; it does not require a real Euler class with this support on the entire cut surface.

For a double cover every smooth nodal divisor, including the one with disconnected normalization, has deck weight $1/2$. Per smooth root (4) gives $-s_as_b(g-1)/8$. There are $2^{2g-2}$ double covers of $B$ with simple branching at $p,q$, from the square roots of $\mathcal O_B(p+q)$, each with one matching and two smoothing roots. There are $2^{2g-2}$ unramified double covers of $B$ including the split one, each with two matchings and one smoothing root per matching. The deck involution preserves either matching. The ordinary pinch connects the split normalization whenever needed. In genus two each group therefore gives eight roots. For the mixed pair the eight contact-(2) terms are each $+1/8$, the eight contact-$(1,1)$ terms each $-1/8$. For a repeated spin all sixteen are $-1/8$ and sum to $-2$.

In degree three the triple point can change the contact multiplicity across the rubber. Take $W=1+Z^3$. Its ends have profiles $(3)$ at infinity and $(1,1,1)$ at zero. A main cover of $B$ with those prescribed fibers and no other branch exists: two noncommuting transpositions on one handle have three-cycle commutator and act transitively. The Riemann existence correspondence supplies the connected cover. Pair the fibers with those on the inserted line. The total framed hull is $\mathbb C[[a,b,d]]$, $t=a^3 b$. The triple collision has $d=0$; its Cartier special fiber is $3[a=0]+[b=0]$. The two sides carry the same positive permutation sign. Thus a single coefficient for both branches would give the wrong central cycle.

## 5. First-excess degree and checks

For the elliptic-tail contribution add the zero-cycle
$$
     -\sum_{\mu} C_\mu\chi(\mu)\,
        \Phi_*\big(\lambda e^*\kappa\cap[\mathcal T_{\mu}]\big).   \tag{5}
$$
Here $\mathcal T_{\mu}$ sums the matched cut marking families of étale smoothings whose core *after gluing* is connected. Calculation D4 proves (5) for smooth connected cut normalization. For a disconnected cut normalization its components are smooth, the seam graph of the smoothed core must be connected, and exactly one component carries the moving attachment. Take its orbit under the matching-preserving deck group. For completeness, the pointed covers without the moving attachment have isomorphic source and target logarithmic tangent terms component by component. The attachment and its tangent obstruction are on the single marked component, with the same isomorphism from the attaching-node smoothing to the elliptic $H^1$ as in Calculation D4. At either relative contact one rigidifies the unique target level by the tail image, retaining all unmarked power bridges on all components. The same simultaneous root ring and proper marking limits result. Thus the tangential top cycle and the evaluation of $-\chi\lambda\kappa$ are unchanged on this orbit. The contracted-tail Hodge line is pulled back from the pointed-tail moduli; integration gives $1/24$. Summing over the orbits of the marked component accounts for all possible points. This proves (5) at the level of the characteristic-class specialization used here. In genus two and degree two the smooth connected tail sector omits the trivial double cover. Its mixed contribution is $(8-7)/12=1/12$ and its equal-spin contribution is $-15/12$. Adding (1) gives the respective full connected degrees $1/12$ and $-13/4$.

The exact enumeration in `code/relative_first_excess_collisions.py` constructs conjugators between the seam permutations on the cut genus-one surface, groups them by the matching of cycles and tests connectivity after gluing and pinching. In degree three the sums of the cut stack degrees for $(1,1,1),(2,1),(3)$ are $50,52,27$ for nodal curves and $18,36,15$ for triple curves. Multiplying each by its contact product gives the nodal masses $50,104,81$ and the triple masses $18,72,45$. Thus the mixed finite-map integral in genus two is $-(50-104+81)/4-(18-72+45)/12=-6$. The program independently compares the nodal and triple masses to the smooth character formula through degree four and checks the degree-two controls through genus seven. These finite checks test the root factors and orientations. Equations (1) and (5) follow from the geometric argument in every degree.

The smooth Hurwitz surface, its branch components and their self-intersections were studied in the complex local theory by Bryan and Pandharipande. Article V, §§4–6 supplies the real spin weights on the smooth target. The nonseparating degeneration formula gives the ordinary tangential contact factors. The calculation here identifies the transverse lines of the two collision types on the compact expanded cut, including a change of seam profile, and evaluates the corresponding signed specialization. The twisted real realization proved here is local to the double-cover collision. A compact real normal theory for arbitrary relative profiles, its higher-excess boundary pairings and a universal comparison through individual walls remain open.
