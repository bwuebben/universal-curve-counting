# D6. The real spin obstruction bundle on paired expanded covers

Let a projective genus-$g$ curve degenerate transversely to an irreducible nodal curve, with $g\ge2$. Fix the two branches of its node and write $(B,p,q)$ for the normalization. We construct the real spin obstruction bundle on a proper refinement of the *paired* expanded-map space. On finite maps its positive real form is computed by ramification residues, even when a critical point lies on an inserted target component and even at an ordinary source node. At the first genus above the étale minimum this identifies the ordinary relative cycles of [Calculation D5](relative-first-excess-collision-divisors.md) with integrals of the actual real Euler classes.

Use either the two exceptional spin gluings with the same square root $A_0$ of $K_B$ or a repeated exceptional spin. We work over the ordered spin base $\delta^2=t$ for the smoothing $xy=t$, on an algebraic one-parameter family with its chosen target spins, using an analytic disk for the operators. Finite base changes specifying these data are allowed. For a matched seam profile $\mu=(k_1,\ldots,k_n)$ of degree $m$, set $C_\mu=\prod_i k_i$, $\kappa=c_1(K_B(p+q))$ and set $\chi(\mu)=(-1)^{m-n}$ for the mixed pair, $1$ for the repeated spin. Source curves after pairing are connected of positive degree.

**Theorem.** For either spin, the pulled-back real normal operator on the refinement is injective, with oriented real cokernel $V_\theta$ of rank $2r=2(h-1-m(g-1))$ in arithmetic source genus $h$. This assertion allows contracted components. On the locus finite over the expanded target, the logarithmic differential defines a finite-flat ramification scheme of length $2r$. Its bundle of spin principal parts with the symmetric residue form admits a positive real form isomorphic to $V_\theta$ as a real bundle, up to homotopy and with determinant orientation supplied by the operator. At an ordinary source node with branch orders $a,b$ the local summand has dimension $a+b$. Distinguished source nodes over target seams give no summands.

When $r=1$, on the normalization of a matched cut finite-map surface with its spin lift the actual single-spin class in rational cohomology is
$$
             e(V_\theta)=-\frac14\sum_{J\subset N\cup T}
                                      s_\theta(J)[J].                 \tag{1}
$$
Here $N,T$ denote the lifted ordinary nodal and triple-ramification divisors, and $[J]$ is the image of the oriented Thom class of a connected component. The signs come from the smooth normalized spin parity on $N$, and from the spin with the triple point added on $T$. The lift includes the finite root and matching data. After combining the two spins and summing with ordinary-target multiplicities, the finite contribution in rational homology is
$$
  -\frac18\sum_\mu C_\mu\chi(\mu)\sum_{J_0}
       \frac1{h_{J_0}}\Phi_*\big(e_{J_0}^*\kappa\cap[J_0]\big),       \tag{2}
$$
where $J_0$ now ranges over the *ordinary matched cut* collision curves; $h_{J_0}=1$ on $N$, $3$ on $T$. For the connected étale smoothing with one contracted elliptic tail the actual contribution is
$$
       -\sum_\mu C_\mu\chi(\mu)\Phi_*
          \big(\lambda e^*\kappa\cap[\mathcal T_\mu]\big).           \tag{3}
$$
All stack degrees retain the matching-preserving automorphisms. Equations (2) and (3) give the full numerical first-excess degeneration for these spins in every degree. Calculation D5 identifies the specialization of the corresponding specified algebraic representative in rational Chow. The claims for real Euler classes here concern cohomology and homology.

## 1. The target spin and its expansions

At the target node use the balanced stack $[UV=\delta\,/\mu_2]$ with the simultaneous sign action and $x=U^2,y=V^2$. Away from this chart use the ordinary target. The prescribed spin $H_\varepsilon$ has character $-1$ at the stacky point and square the relative dualizing line. With smooth-branch frames squaring to $dx,dy$, the local generators are $n_x=\ell_x/U$, $n_y=\ell_y/V=\varepsilon i n_x$. The squares are $dx/x$ and $dy/y$. This recovers the exceptional spin smoothing on the ordinary smooth fibers. The Hermitian metric of [Calculation D4](virtual-gluing-for-one-elliptic-tail.md) makes its smooth real operator extend with ambient antilinear coefficient $2(U\,d\bar U-V\,d\bar V)$. The interpolation with a positive spin metric away from the neck can be fixed once. [Twisted roots of dualizing lines](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607324).

For a chain with $l$ inserted rational target levels the seam parameters have $t=\prod_{j=0}^l c_j$. Use $A_j^2=c_j$, $\prod A_j=\delta$. With the ordinary coordinates $x=(\prod_{i<j}c_i)W_j$, $y=(\prod_{i\ge j}c_i)/W_j$ on the interior of level $j$, its lifted contraction is
$$
    Z_j^2=W_j,\qquad U=(\prod_{i<j}A_i)Z_j,\qquad
                      V=(\prod_{i\ge j}A_i)/Z_j.                    \tag{4}
$$
Changing $Z_j$ to $-Z_j$ is the target action. This is the usual expansion of $UV=\prod A_j$ quotiented by simultaneous sign in the neck. Its coarse expansion has the original parameters $c_j$. At an open join $Z_j=A_j Z_{j+1}$ in compatible frames, so contracting that level gives the shorter formula. One takes the quotient by changes $A_i\mapsto\eta_i A_i$ with $\eta_i=\pm1$, $\prod\eta_i=1$, using $Z_j\mapsto(\prod_{i<j}\eta_i)Z_j$. Rubber coordinate rescalings lift after square roots of units; their two lifts differ by the indicated stack actions. Equivalently these are factorization charts for the spin smoothing parameter into line sections, whose squares are the factorization charts for $t$. Changes of trivialization act by the corresponding torus, and deleting an invertible section gives the usual level contraction. Glue to the fixed complement along the punctured outer charts, on which the stack has trivial relative inertia. This constructs the twisted expanded target together with its map to the original twisted target and a proper quasi-finite change of the ordinary expansion base after $\delta^2=t$: on framed charts it is the finite squaring map with the specified finite quotient, and contracts open joins compatibly. Over a smooth target its degree is one. Only the displayed local construction at the two ordered branches is needed for an irreducible central target. [Twisted expansions and factorization charts](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.2976).

To retain the source family, pull the ordinary stack of expanded maps to these charts and take representable twisted stable maps to the *expanded* twisted curve with the given coarse stable map. A map stable allowing expansion automorphisms is stable as a map to that fixed expanded curve. Over an expansion chart the twisted target is a proper tame stack over the base with finite inertia. The forgetful map to stable maps to its coarse curve is proper and quasi-finite by [Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli, Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.3040#page=18). Base change retains predeformability of the specified coarse maps; the lifted expansion coordinate changes act on this construction by functoriality. Expanded-map stability leaves finite stabilizers after quotient. Retain the horizontal closure of the smooth-target sector, with its normalization when separating branches. The resulting proper stack has its universal twisted source, map to the expanded target and line $H$ pulled back from $H_\varepsilon$ by contraction. Its map to the ordinary generic sector on the spin base has degree one. We use the local horizontal root rings near the cut below. We do not assign the ordinary perfect obstruction theory to the unrestricted stack of twisted lifts.

At a contact of coarse order $k$ the minimal source stabilizer is $2/\gcd(2,k)$. In the ordinary case $k=2b$ the contact lift has local degree $b$ to the target uniformizer and the equation $A_j=\tau s^b$, $\tau=\pm1$, for the coarse source smoothing $s$. At odd $k$ the source has a balanced order-two chart with smoothing $\rho_0$, $s=\rho_0^2$, $A_j=\rho_0^k$ in suitable frames. Indeed away from the seam the sign cover on a rubber component is $\sqrt{W_j}$, and at each end the local function is a $k$th power times a unit. These charts lift after taking roots of the units, impose precisely the required smooth root identifications, and glue via (4). At an ordinary source point or ordinary source node mapping into the interior of a target level, the expanded target is unstacked. The map there has a unique lift to that part of the target. In particular no root of an ordinary critical-point deformation parameter is imposed. The construction keeps more data than a cut profile and is independent of which join is designated as cut once the paired lift is fixed.

## 2. The actual operator

Use the line $H$ on the source and pull back the ambient mass from the unexpanded twisted target. Call the resulting real Cauchy–Riemann operator $D$. On components with image in the target normalization the usual spin energy calculation involves the differential of the spin square with logarithmic poles at the ends. In a sign frame on the left $2U\,d\bar U=|U|^2\overline{2dU/U}$. The density in the Stokes formula is nonnegative and integrable. At an odd contact the section has zero invariant endpoint value. At an even contact the limiting residue of the section square is its squared endpoint value times its contact order, with opposite signs for the two target branches in a common square frame.

On each connected part mapped to the residual gerbe after contraction the mass is zero. The square of a holomorphic kernel section there is a regular function times the fixed square fiber, hence constant. The sum of contact orders entering from the left equals that exiting to the right on each such part: on every nonconstant rubber component the zero and pole divisors of $W_j$ have equal degree, and on summing their degrees the orders at internal joins cancel. Ordinary contracted components attached there have the same squared value and add no orders. On an unexpanded matched pair the orders coincide by predeformability. Thus all the boundary residues cancel. Stokes on the main components now forces a kernel section to vanish where the differential is nonzero, hence on every nonconstant main component. A holomorphic section of the flat line on a contracted component that vanishes at an attaching point has identically zero square. This propagates through the connected positive-degree source. The proof includes contracted components at ordinary target values, with constant pullback line. For a disconnected auxiliary normalization the conclusion applies whenever each paired connected component has positive degree.

The orbifold degree of $H$ is $m(g-1)$. At a balanced node with sign fiber the two ages sum to one and there is zero invariant matching condition. A node with trivial fiber has zero ages and one complex matching condition. Thus normalization and Riemann–Roch give $\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D=2(1-h+m(g-1))$. In families, the pulled-back ambient coefficient is continuous in nodal Fredholm charts. On each balanced plumbing use the ordinary uniformizing chart, choose invariant cutoffs, and average the stabilized inverses and obstruction spaces over the finite group. The usual estimates for the Dolbeault operator and ambient forms then hold on invariant subspaces. On the complement one uses ordinary local charts. This yields a continuous finite-dimensional stabilization, and constant kernel and index give a real cokernel bundle. Orient its dual determinant by the homotopy from the complex Dolbeault operator. All isomorphisms preserving the lift respect this orientation. These are the local Fredholm estimates of [Siebert, §§4.1–4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/dg-ga/9608005) and [Calculation D3](real-spin-gluing-at-finite-contacts.md). Calculation D7 also compares with the operator after stabilizing components contracted by the target. The argument for the bundle does not require the domain to be finite over the expansion.

## 3. Residues on finite expanded maps

Now assume the map $f:X\to W^{\mathrm{exp}}$ is finite over the expansion. Pullback of the unexpanded dualizing line identifies with the expanded target dualizing line under logarithmic pullback. In (4) this sends $dx/x$ to $dW_j/W_j$ along the fiber, including a collapsed level. Write
$$
   \sigma:H\longrightarrow \omega_X H^{-1},\qquad
   R=Z(\sigma),\qquad E=\pi_*\big(H(R)/H\big).
$$
The homomorphism uses the differential to the source dualizing line. At a distinguished node it is multiplication by the nonzero local order in logarithmic frames. At a smooth point the vanishing order is the ordinary ramification order. At an ordinary node its restrictions to both branches are nonzero off the node. Thus $\sigma$ is a fiberwise nonzerodivisor and defines an effective relative Cartier divisor disjoint from the distinguished nodes. The source family is flat, so $R$ is flat over the base by the local criterion for flatness; properness and zero-dimensional fibers give finiteness. Its length is $\deg\omega_X-2\deg H=2r$. Equivalently $H(R)=\omega_X H^{-1}$ by $\sigma$. The bundle $E$ carries the symmetric form
$$
            b(a,a')=\sum_{z\in R}\operatorname{Res}_z(\sigma aa'),     \tag{6}
$$
using the sum on the normalization branches at an ordinary node. Changing either meromorphic lift by a regular spin section changes each local sum by the sum of residues of a regular dualizing differential, which is zero.

For instance, near an ordinary node with branch orders $a,b\ge1$, take the target function to be $u^a+v^b$ in the ring $A=\mathbb C[[u,v]]/(uv)$. In a dualizing frame $du/u=-dv/v$ the multiplier, up to a unit, is $G=au^a-bv^b$. Up to spin and coordinate units (6) on $A/(G)$ is the product followed by the functional $[u^a]/a+[v^b]/b$. In the basis $1,u,\ldots,u^a,v,\ldots,v^{b-1}$ its two blocks are the anti-identities of sizes $a+1$ and $b-1$, scaled by $1/a,1/b$. This proves the claimed dimension and nondegeneracy, including $a=b=1$. At a smooth point the form on the length-$d$ jet algebra extracts the coefficient of degree $d-1$ up to a unit, so is likewise perfect. Contour integrals around the boundaries of neighborhoods of each cluster of points compute their total residue and vary continuously through collisions, including smoothing an ordinary node. The circles there enclose the two branches with their induced boundary orientations. This gives a continuous complex orthogonal bundle throughout the finite locus.

The physical mass vanishes identically on inserted components at the central target. To relate its cokernel uniformly to (6), choose a smooth background Hermitian metric and multiply the metric conjugate of $\sigma$ by a real weight $w$ positive on the smooth locus of every source component, equal locally at each node to $|u|^2+|v|^2$ up to a positive smooth unit. We need only comparability and an ambient one-form representative, both preserved by taking positive sums from overlapping nodal charts. On $uv=s$ the product of this weight with $d\bar u/\bar u$ is $u\,d\bar u-v\,d\bar v$, including both branch restrictions of this ambient form. Invariant partitions of unity on the stable-map orbifold charts give such a weight; choices are joined by convex interpolation. This regularizes dualizing polar lifts even at ordinary nodes. The physical coefficient on the finite source is also a nonnegative weighted conjugate of $\sigma$ relative to the chosen metric, as follows on each level from (4) and the unexpanded coefficient. The convex interpolation is continuous in the same Fredholm charts. For each positive interpolation parameter the mass is positive off its isolated zeros on each component and the logarithmic residues of $\sigma s^2$ cancel by node matching. The energy identity implies injectivity. At parameter zero §2 applies. Thus the oriented cokernel bundle $V_w$ for the positive weight is homotopy equivalent, as a bundle, to the physical $V_\theta$.

Here is a direct comparison including nodal fibers. For a class $a$ of polar parts choose a local holomorphic meromorphic lift in $H(R)$ and multiply by a cutoff $\eta$ equal to one near its poles and supported off the distinguished seams. For $c$ denoting metric conjugation form the real-linear map
$$
      F:E_{\mathbb R}\longrightarrow V_w,qquad
      F(a)=\big[\bar\partial(\eta a)+w c(\sigma\eta a)\big].          \tag{7}
$$
The cutoff derivative is taken off the poles and is zero near them. Holomorphic lifts of local frames of the finite quotient can be taken on small neighborhoods over a base chart. At an ordinary node the second term is regular in the Fredholm topology by the ambient-form identity above. Outside these neighborhoods the cutoff derivative is an ordinary relative form. A change of lift or cutoff changes (7) by $D_w$ of a matching regular section, so this gives a continuous bundle map. Invariance under (4), domain isomorphisms, and the finite changes of frames follows from this description.

If $F(a)=0$, take a matching regular solution to (7) before projection and subtract it from $\eta a$ to give $s$ with $D_w s=0$ off $R$. With $\sigma=g_0 dz$ in a spin frame of squared norm $h_0$, Stokes gives
$$
       2\pi b(a,a)=4\int_X w h_0^{-1}|g_0s|^2\,dx\,dy.              \tag{8}
$$
The positive normalization uses $d(\sigma s^2)=-4i w h_0^{-1}|g_0s|^2dx\,dy$. At an ordinary node $\sigma a$ is a regular dualizing section, possibly with simple poles in branch coordinates. Its product with the subtracted section has opposite summed residues because the latter has matching values. Continuity (or $W^{1,p}$ with $p>2$) makes its remaining boundary integral tend to zero. The quadratic weight makes the energy integrable there. The distinguished nodes give canceling residues as above, with the quotient-chart integral at a stacky node. Equality with zero energy forces zero polar part on every component. Hence the form is real positive definite on $K=\ker F$. It follows that $K\cap iK=0$. The rank already computed implies $\dim_{\mathbb R}K\ge\dim_{\mathbb C}E$, so equality holds, (7) is onto, and $E=K\oplus iK$ as a real bundle. Multiplication by $i$ identifies $K$ with $E_{\mathbb R}/K$. This proves the positive-real-form assertion. In particular every maximal complex isotropic subbundle maps isomorphically as a real bundle to $V_w$. This argument does not assert that (7) for the undeformed mass has a positive definite kernel at a collapsed level.

There is a consequence at arbitrary excess on fixed-profile strata of finite maps to a fixed paired nodal target, with smooth source off the distinguished nodes. If an ordinary ramification index is even, its local orthogonal summand has odd rank. After ordering the critical points and orienting the summands on a finite cover one may choose the positive real form separately on each: the space of positive real forms is contractible. The rational Euler class of the odd-rank factor vanishes, hence so does $e(V_\theta)$ by transfer. If all indices are odd, write $R=2D$. The half-pole space $H(D)/H$ is maximal isotropic and therefore computes the Euler class up to orientation by (7). At a critical point of index $k=2j_0+1$ its jet filtration has factors $H_z\otimes (T_z X)^j$ for $1\le j\le j_0$. The leading jet identifies $(T_zX)^k$ with the target tangent at the marked value; its dual equals the evaluation of the fixed unexpanded target dualizing line, also on rubber by logarithmic pullback. The first Chern classes are $(1/2-j/k)e_z^*c_1(\omega_{W_0})$. If any $k\ge5$, at least two factors pull back the same class from a fixed curve and their product is zero. These are restrictions on strata, including critical points on different levels. Global integrals can still receive boundary contributions.

## 4. Two levels carrying one simple branch point each

In degree two, take the successive seam profiles $(1,1),(2),(1,1)$. In coordinates with $W_L=c_M W_R$ the rubber maps can be taken as
$$
          W_L=1/(1+z_L^2),\qquad W_R=1+z_R^2.
$$
The two ordinary simple critical points are $z_L=z_R=0$ at value $1$ on their respective levels. The middle source smoothing is $a$, with $c_M=a^2$, and $t=c_La^2c_R$. Equation (4) gives the framed parameter $\delta=A_L a A_R$ up to the even clutch sign, with $c_L=A_L^2,c_R=A_R^2$. In log spin frames and polar coordinates $h_L/z_L,h_R/z_R$ the residue square is $-2h_L^2+2h_R^2$. The two null lines are $h_R=\pm h_L$.

For the physical operator the main normalization is étale, with coarse spin the pullback of $A_0$ and zero invariant evaluations at the odd ends. Its index is zero and it is invertible by the energy identity. Each normalized rational rubber has a flat spin of degree zero with two odd stacky endpoints. The invariant coarse sheaf is $\mathcal O(-1)$; both cohomology groups vanish. The normalization sequence now identifies the physical rank-two real cokernel with the one complex matching fiber at the even central seam, with the determinant sign of the normalized main problem. In particular the physical forcing map can kill a full null line here. On each rubber the meromorphic section with prescribed pole is locally $h_j\sqrt{1+z_j^2}\,n/z_j$ in the changing sign frame. It has finite nonzero value for $h_j\ne0$ at the even end. The two sections match precisely on the null graph specified by the spin clutch and then their physical forcing is exact.

The reversed sequence $(2),(1,1),(2)$ has $t=a_L^2c_Ma_R^2$. Each rubber is again acyclic. On the main normalization the spin line is $f^*A_0(P+Q)$ of complex Dolbeault index one. In its two endpoint fibers $H_P\oplus H_Q$ choose either null graph for the two log residues. Imposing this one complex matching condition gives index zero and kills the kernel by Stokes, so the restricted operator is invertible. Without the condition it is surjective with a two-dimensional real kernel. The actual cokernel on the full curve is the quotient of the two outer evaluation fibers by this kernel's evaluation image; the middle odd contacts have zero invariant fibers. Each null graph maps real-isomorphically onto the quotient. The underlying undecorated deck groups are respectively the diagonal involution for the first sequence and two independent involutions for the reversed one. For a lifted object the image of its stabilizer in the coarse deck group preserves the chosen lift. Source and expansion ghost automorphisms can also occur in the kernel. Integration on the refinement uses the actual stack stabilizers.

To compare the two target spins choose the trivialization of their difference off one chosen seam. Smoothing the other joins puts the two critical points in that complement. The orientation comparison of [Calculation D3](real-spin-gluing-at-finite-contacts.md) is the seam permutation sign in a common off-neck isotropic reference. Here the normalized branched double cover is connected, since a disconnected degree-two cover would have two unramified degree-one components. The reference transports to the common corner via (7) with positive weight, since its restriction to a null line never degenerates. On smooth fibers a change of positive weight preserves the reference by the same homotopy. Moving the clutch past a level negates the difference-line trivialization on that level. It negates exactly one residue coordinate and exchanges the two null lines. Conjugation with respect to the positive real form $K$ exchanges these lines, and $v$ and $-\bar v$ have the same class modulo $K$. Hence their identification under (7) is conjugate-linear and reverses the comparison orientation. This yields the three reference signs $+,-,+$ (respectively $-,+,-$), with composite transition $+1$. The physical operator itself is the pullback along one paired contraction (4).

## 5. The compact first-excess pairing

For completeness about support, the closure of the smooth finite component at first excess consists of maps finite over the expanded target. Mark the two simple branch values and apply admissible-cover reduction over the stable target with those marks. Outside the chain at the target node an extra target twig which disappears on forgetting the markings must carry both marks. The same description holds if the two marks collide at an interior point of an inserted line. On a connected covering component of such a one-ended twig of degree $d$, with $j$ contacts at its attached end and $b\le2$ simple branch values counted in its ramification, Riemann–Hurwitz gives $2g'-2=-d-j+b$. A nontrivial branched component is rational. For $b=2$, $(d,j)=(2,2)$ or $(3,1)$, giving respectively an ordinary node or a triple point after stabilization. If the transpositions are disjoint there are two $(2,1)$ components with $b=1$, leaving two distinct simple ramification points with the same value. Unramified twig components have degree one. Additional levels with only two-ended power maps contract with their source powers. Thus the finite surface acquires none of the positive-genus ghost components of the other sector. Marked admissible reduction also treats arcs where collision and specialization to the seam occur at different orders. [Admissible covers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4626).

On the normalized horizontal spin refinement take the reduced matched cut surfaces with their Cartier-fiber multiplicities. More explicitly, retain the cut designation at the central fiber and normalize each of its reduced components; their proper pushforwards sum with the generic multiplicities before forgetting the cut. The hulls along $N,T$ are the pure contact rings times an unrestricted primitive transverse parameter $d$ of [Calculation D5](relative-first-excess-collision-divisors.md). The spin lifts only replace each seam factor by the odd or even power equation of §1, and can take finite quotients. Indeed at all the distinguished nodes these are precisely the root choices on the predeformable horizontal plumbings. Off these nodes the expanded map lifts uniquely, including throughout the collision chart at the smooth value. Conversely those local horizontal families construct every smoothing branch, and proper separatedness of the stack of twisted maps gives its limits after finite base change. One may compute on framed normalizations: a simultaneous-root factor over a single moving seam parameter normalizes to disks given by powers of one parameter; a closed, designated seam contributes its discrete branches and multiplicity. Thus near a collision the normalized cut surface has smooth charts with parameters $(z,d)$, up to finite automorphisms. At an interior collision $z$ moves its value; at an endpoint it is the normalized residual seam parameter. The transverse parameter $d$ is unramified. The inverse-image collision curves are compact disjoint smooth orbicurves there. Their normal line has first Chern class $-2 e_J^*\kappa/h_J$ by the nodal smoothing tensors or the third jet as in Calculation D5. Each $e_J:J\to B$ has positive stack degree, since the usual moving branch value lies in its dense open. We need smooth neighborhoods only along these curves.

On the complement of $N,T$ there are exactly two distinct simple critical points. They may have the same image. After ordering them and choosing unit frames of their nondegenerate quadratic residue lines on a finite cover, the isotropic line spanned by $e_1+i e_2$ is trivial. Equation (7) shows the pullback Euler class vanishes, hence it vanishes rationally on that entire complement by transfer. Along $N$, normalize the ordinary node. The remaining finite problem is everywhere injective of total index zero, since the paired normalization is an étale smoothing, possibly disconnected. Difference evaluation at the ordinary node gives its complex matching line, with orientation the sign $s_\theta$ of the normalized real determinant. Exchange of branches gives only a flat sign. Its square is the evaluation of the target dualizing line and hence $e(V_\theta)|_J=s_\theta e_J^*\kappa/2$.

Along $T$ the simple-pole line $I=H(r_0)/H$ at the unique triple point $r_0$ is isotropic and gives $I_{\mathbb R}\simeq V_w$ via (7). The spin square and the nonzero third differential give $I^6=e_J^*K_B(p+q)$, using $I=H_{r_0}\otimes T_{r_0}X$. Its orientation sign equals $s_\theta=(-1)^{h^0(f^*\theta(r_0))}$ on a smooth reference. Indeed on a smooth-target connected triple Hurwitz curve the actual Euler restriction is $s_\theta c_1(I)$ by Article V, §§4–6; the curve is proper and $I$ has nonzero degree. Thus the sign of the continuous real isomorphism is determined there. The local triple-preserving plumbings of Calculation D5 smooth every framed branch to such a cover. The isomorphism transports this sign to the central divisor, giving $e(V_\theta)|_J=s_\theta e_J^*\kappa/6$. The same transport on $N$ gives the ordinary smooth spin parity of its normalization. Individual signs are taken on spin lifts, on which these comparisons are continuous.

Excision and the Thom isomorphism in the smooth neighborhoods give a coefficient per connected $J$ for any degree-two class vanishing on the complement. Restricting to $J$ gives its coefficient times its normal first Chern class. This has nonzero degree since $g\ge2$. The two restrictions just computed therefore give (1). Multiplication of the two actual classes uses the disjointness of the normalized collision divisors and their normal lines. The product of signs is $\chi(\mu)$ by the smooth spin comparison on each root, including at the triple point where the three-cycle is even. Proper pushforward recovers (2). To check multiplicities, at the generic point of a matched cut component all branch values are away from the unexpanded target node. The ordered spin disk has $2^e$ framed central clutches for $e$ even contacts; each has total root degree $\prod_{k_i\,\mathrm{odd}}k_i\prod_{k_i\,\mathrm{even}}(k_i/2)$. An odd node's possible change of sign is absorbed by its source-node ghost; in fixed branch frames no such nontrivial ghost fixes the lift. Equivalently the coarse simultaneous powers $s_i^{k_i}=\delta^2$ have $C_\mu$ geometric solutions over a nonzero parameter, and the proper horizontal map is generically an isomorphism. The projection formula counts the root orbits with their actual generic stack degrees. Along a lifted collision no transverse ramification changes this weight. Special endpoint stabilizers do not give extra top cycles. The ordinary tangential cycle on this finite first-excess locus is its top fundamental cycle as in Calculation D5.

On the other compact component normalize the one contracted elliptic tail from its étale smoothing core. The core is invertible on the paired spin refinement by §2, of determinant sign whose product is $\chi(\mu)$. The constant tail maps to a smooth point of the expanded target, even when it lies on a rubber component. Thus its line is an ordinary constant pullback on the whole pointed genus-one curve. Its constants account for the matching fiber and its arithmetic $H^1$ gives the single-spin cokernel $\mathbb E^\vee\otimes H_{\rm mark}$. The square of $H_{\rm mark}$ on the cut stack is $e^*K_B(p+q)$; the moduli of pointed tails give $\lambda^2=0$ and $\kappa^2=0$ on $B$. The product of the two signed Euler classes is therefore $-\chi(\mu)\lambda e^*\kappa$. With the tangential cycle and root degree of [Calculation D4](virtual-gluing-for-one-elliptic-tail.md) and [the proof](relative-first-excess-collision-divisors.md) this proves (3), including a disconnected cut normalization with connected paired core.

In genus two and covering degree two this gives the same finite-map degrees $0,-2$ and full degrees $1/12,-13/4$ for the mixed and repeated spins, respectively. Their relative representatives are now computed with the paired compact real operators in every profile. The Stokes and residue arguments also apply degree by degree to finite covers in higher excess. Contracted components in those genera still require a comparison of tangential virtual cycles with their weighted lifts before one can deduce higher-excess gluing. The construction is on paired cuts of this fixed degeneration and does not supply a numerical theory of unpaired ends or a universal wall-crossing identity in the cycle algebra.
