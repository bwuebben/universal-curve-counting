# D7. Degeneration of the paired spin Euler integral in every genus

Fix a projective genus-$g$ curve, $g\ge2$, in the ordered exceptional-spin degeneration of [Calculation D6](real-spin-obstruction-on-paired-expanded-covers.md). The two target spins $H_+,H_-$ extend the same theta characteristic of the normalization and differ by sign in sewing the two ends. We allow a repeated spin as well. Let $L_{m,h}^{a,b}$ be the connected fixed-target integral of the two oriented real spin Euler classes in degree $m>0$ and arithmetic source genus $h$, for $a,b\in\{+,-\}$. Put $n=h-1-m(g-1)\ge0$. We use ordinary, unmarked stable maps on a smooth fiber. The normal reduction and comparison with the curve virtual class are those of Article III, Proposition 15.4 and Appendices D–E, and Article V, §2.

**Theorem.** Resolve the ordered spin degeneration as in §1. Its two smooth components meet at the two gerbes $D_p,D_q$. In every degree and source genus the fixed-target integral is
$$
 L_{m,h}^{a,b}=
 \sum_{\eta}\frac{\prod_{j\in M} k_j}{|M|!}
 \int_{[P_\eta]^{\rm vir}}
 \operatorname{Av}_\eta\!\left(e(V_{a,\eta})e(V_{b,\eta})\right).       \tag{1}
$$
Here $\eta$ runs over the labeled relative splittings with connected glued source, $M$ labels the matched relative markings, and $k_j$ is the coarse contact order. The contact orders sum to $m$ over each of $p,q$. The stack $P_\eta$ is the product of the two relative map spaces restricted to the indicated colors and inverse inertia sectors, with the product virtual class. On the finite gluing correspondence each $V_{a,\eta}$ is the oriented real cokernel of the two relative spin operators coupled by one complex evaluation equation at every **even** contact. Its real rank is $2n$. The normalized transfer $\operatorname{Av}_\eta$ of §3 retains the dependence on the spin identifications in these equations.

For each connected piece of either relative source introduce a vertex, and retain an edge only for an even contact. Color the retained edges by $p,q$. A term of (1) can distinguish the mixed pair from the repeated pair only if this graph contains a cycle with an odd number of $q$-edges. On a splitting with only odd contacts the relative spin Euler classes on the two sides multiply without any matching equation. The paired insertion vanishes whenever the sign Hodge space on an exceptional vertex has positive rank. In degree two the entire difference is the single relative Euler-square integral (7) on the normalization side. For target genus two its series is evaluated in (6).

## 1. A semistable target for the two spins

On the balanced target chart $[UV=\delta\,/\mu_2]$, the group acts by simultaneous sign and the ordinary smoothing parameter is $t=\delta^2$. After $\delta=s^2$ resolve the ordinary $A_1$ singularity equivariantly before taking the quotient. The two charts have $s=Uz$, $V=Uz^2$ and $s=Vw$, $U=Vw^2$, with $zw=1$ on the overlap. The actions on $(U,z)$ and $(V,w)$ are simultaneous sign. The total stack $W$ is smooth. The main central component $X$ is the normalization $B$ with index-two roots at $p,q$. The other component is $Y=[\mathbb P^1/\mu_2]$ with $z\mapsto-z$. The two fixed points give $D=D_p\sqcup D_q$, with $D_p,D_q\simeq B\mu_2$. The branch normals at either crossing are sign lines with product trivialized by $s$. The projective modification is an isomorphism off the original gerbe. The coarse space is projective over the chosen algebraic base after shrinking around the only critical value, by descent of a power of the relatively ample exceptional line together with the original polarization. The pulled-back spin line has faithful action at each nontrivial target stabilizer. Its frame space has a finite map to the frame space of its descended square over the quasi-projective coarse space (use the displayed finite quotient charts). This gives a quotient presentation with quasi-projective atlas. The two spins and the physical operators below are pulled back through the contraction to the original spin target.

Use Abramovich–Fantechi's moduli of transversal maps for a fixed twisting choice. Expand the whole disconnected divisor $D$ simultaneously, with a single expansion base and a common target twist at the designated splitting. This is a degeneration with two smooth components and smooth intersection in their Convention 2.2.1. In the smooth degree-$m$ sector the central multidegree is $(m,m)$. Pullback of an ample line on the original curve fixes the main coarse degree. On $X$ the vertical divisor $Y$ restricts to the two gerbe points of total degree one. Its restriction to $Y$ has degree minus one since $X+Y$ is the central fiber. Ordinary degrees of its descended square and the pulled-back polarization are locally constant in proper flat source families. Select their open and closed degree sectors in the transversal-map family. Degree zero of the vertical line on a smoothing forces equality of the two coarse covering degrees in this sector. Orders balance along each inserted cylinder, giving the two asserted sums. The relative graphs allow contracted source components of arbitrary genus consistent with $h$. [Abramovich–Fantechi, §§2.2, 3.4, 4.6–4.7](https://arxiv.org/html/1103.5132) · [Abramovich–Cadman–Fantechi–Wise, §§2.3–2.4, 5.4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1110.2976).

## 2. The Fredholm problem on the full family and on its cut

Compose each map to an expansion with contraction to the original balanced spin target. On the universal source pull back $H_a$ and the ambient antilinear term of Calculation D6, §1. Near the original crossing its differential factor is $2(U\,d\bar U-V\,d\bar V)$ in the specified frame. It vanishes after contraction on $Y$ and the cylinders. On a smooth fiber the resulting real operator $D_a$ is the smooth spin operator up to positive metric deformation. Use matched sections at nodes, and orient its determinant by continuation along $\bar\partial+u(D_a-\bar\partial)$, $0\le u\le1$. This convention retains the signed Thom unit at index zero.

Here is the vanishing argument on any central map, including components occurring only in the special fiber. On a main component Stokes for the spin square gives the nonnegative energy of [Calculation D6](real-spin-obstruction-on-paired-expanded-covers.md). Boundary residues are opposite signed multiples of the square of the end value, with multiplier the coarse order $k$. Indeed the log derivative of the coarse coordinate has uniformizing order $k$ times the source stabilizer order, and stack integration divides by that order. A nontrivial fiber character forces the end value to vanish. On each connected part contracted to the original gerbe the mass vanishes, so the square of a kernel section is a constant in the same squared line. Orient the target chain from $p$ through $Y$ to $q$. The divisors of zeros and poles on every nonconstant cylinder or exceptional component have equal total coarse degree. The internal matched orders cancel on summing over the part. Ordinary contracted components add no orders. The signed residues therefore sum to zero, including for maps with different partitions at $p,q$. Stokes forces vanishing on the positive-degree main components wherever their differential is nonzero. The holomorphic constant-square property on the remaining contracted pieces and matching across nodes propagate vanishing on the connected source. At an ordinary contracted component away from the gerbe its line is a fixed fiber and the same propagation holds.

The orbifold degree of $H_a$ is $m(g-1)$. At a balanced node with nontrivial sign fiber the two ages sum to one and the invariant evaluation fiber is zero. At a trivial fiber the ages vanish and one complex matching row occurs. Normalization and orbifold Riemann–Roch give real index $-2n$. The family of cokernels $V_a$ therefore has constant real rank $2n$ on the full transversal-map family. More explicitly, on a balanced plumbing one uses Dolbeault stabilization by forms off the nodes on the finite uniformizing charts. The ambient pullback term satisfies the shrinking-neck bound there as in Calculation D6, §2. Restriction by finite-group averaging preserves the inverse estimates and gives invariant stabilizations. After the surjectivity stabilization, the kernel and quotient charts give a continuous bundle, with the same determinant continuation. This argument works directly on the given source with its actual finite group, even when the spin action has a kernel. It uses no identification of smooth invariant functions with smooth functions in a powered coordinate. The ranks of the *separate* relative kernels may jump. [Nodal stabilization, Siebert §§4.1–4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/dg-ga/9608005).

Let $K_\eta$ retain the split expansion, the root labels, and the identifications of the two source marking gerbes. At one root write $f$ for its source stabilizer order before the additional target twist and $c$ for its representable uniformizer contact. In a coarse coordinate squared on the target, $2c=kf$. Representability gives $f=2/\gcd(2,k)$ and $c=k/\gcd(2,k)$. For a common twist $r$ divisible by all $c$, the source stabilizer is $2r/k$. Its distinguished generator maps to the original sign character as $(-1)^k$. Thus only even $k$ have invariant end values. Write $D_{a,X},D_{a,Y}$ for the operators on the two relative sources with independent section domains, retaining matching at their internal nodes. Write $F_j$ for the invariant $X$-end line at an even root, and $\tau_{a,j}$ for the isomorphism from the other end given by the chosen gluing. Take $W^{1,p_0}$ sections with $p_0>2$ so point evaluations exist. The required augmented Fredholm map is
$$
 (u,v)\longmapsto
 \left(D_{a,X}u, D_{a,Y}v,
    \big(u(j)-\tau_{a,j}v(j)\big)_{j:\,2\mid k_j}\right).           \tag{2}
$$
The evaluation-difference map onto $\bigoplus F_j$ is surjective by local invariant cutoffs. Its kernel is the glued section domain, including the automatic zero equalities at odd roots. Inclusion of the two form spaces with zero last row consequently identifies the cokernel of the glued operator with the cokernel of (2), and identifies their kernels. The identification extends over families by finite-dimensional stabilization and the exact sequence for normalization. At the Dolbeault endpoint this sequence is complex-linear. Its continuation gives the claimed orientation, with all matching fibers complex oriented. Thus (2) constructs $V_{a,\eta}$ from the relative operators and identifies it with the restriction of $V_a$.

## 3. Virtual gluing and rational transfer

Let $K\to S$ be the proper transversal-map family. Its relative obstruction theory gives the fiber virtual classes by Gysin restriction. Pass to rational Borel–Moore homology and cap the total virtual class with $e(V_a)e(V_b)$. The virtual complex fiber dimension is $2n$ and the total Euler degree is $4n$ in real grading. Proper push to the smooth base therefore gives a multiple of its fundamental class. Compatibility of the fiber Gysin map with cap by a class on $K$ and with proper push identifies this multiple at a smooth fiber and at zero. There is one comparison of fiber degrees over the base with coordinate $s$. The input from the algebraic theory is the relative virtual class and its splitting, so the argument includes its vertical components and requires only a topological real Euler class. [Abramovich–Fantechi, §§4.6, 5.5–5.8](https://arxiv.org/html/1103.5132).

Writing $\mathrm{st}_\eta$ for forgetting the split, AF Proposition 5.6.1 gives
$$
 [K_0]^{\rm vir}=\sum_\eta\frac{r}{|M|!}
             (\mathrm{st}_\eta)_*[K_\eta]^{\rm vir}.                \tag{3}
$$
For each allowed type the map $\pi_\eta:K_\eta\to P_\eta$ passes first through the gluing of the relative target halves. That target choice gives one $\mu_r$-gerbe over $P_\eta$. Over it the relative evaluations match along the inverse-band diagonal of the twisted splitting gerbe. The original rigidified inertia sector at the root is $B(\mu_2/\mu_f)$, whose diagonal has degree $2/f$. Passing to the twisted matching corresponds to a finite étale cover of degree $\prod c_j$ (AF Proposition 5.9.1). All these diagonals are étale on the indicated compatible sectors because their geometric fibers are finite gerbes. Consequently AF Proposition 5.8.1 and Lemma 5.7.2 give
$$
 [K_\eta]^{\rm vir}=\pi_\eta^*[P_\eta]^{\rm vir},\qquad
 d_\eta=\deg(\pi_\eta)=\frac1r\prod_{j\in M}\frac{2c_j}{f_j}
                         =\frac1r\prod_{j\in M}k_j.               \tag{4}
$$
Define $\operatorname{Av}_\eta(a)=(\pi_\eta)_!a/d_\eta$, taking the ordinary transfer across a finite étale map and $1/r$ times inverse rational pullback across the gerbe. It sends $1$ to $1$. The projection formula for (3)–(4), with the identification (2), proves (1). The factorial uses AF's sum over labeled types. The two colors subdivide their evaluation spaces. Stack integration already includes source automorphism weights. In particular, full contacts at both colors in degrees one, two and three have respective weights $1,4,9$ before the relabeling and relative automorphism factors. The average of the *product* in (1) preserves correlations between the two spin identifications.  · [AF, §§5.6–5.10](https://arxiv.org/html/1103.5132#S5.SS6).

## 4. Which splittings distinguish the two spins?

The ratio $H_-/H_+$ is a square-trivialized line. On each half of the normalized target choose square-compatible identifications of the two spins. They extend by contraction along the trees of cylinders on that half. Their transition ratio across $D$ is $+1$ at $p$ and $-1$ at $q$, up to simultaneously changing the sign on one half. Choose the same metric on the identified restrictions. Pullback to the source multiplies both spin clutches by the same identifications of marking gerbes, hence their ratio stays the indicated sign at every even root.

On a finite labeling cover for the relative vertices let $\gamma_j$ denote these edge signs. If signs $\epsilon_v$ on the vertices satisfy
$$
               \gamma_j=\epsilon_{v_X(j)}\epsilon_{v_Y(j)}
                           \qquad(2\mid k_j),                         \tag{5}
$$
then multiplication by $\epsilon_v$ on each connected piece intertwines the two versions of (2), with multiplication by $\epsilon_{v_X(j)}$ on matching rows. The same holds along the Dolbeault orientation homotopy. At its complex endpoint these identifications preserve the determinant orientation. Hence the oriented Euler classes agree, including in rank zero. Transfer through the vertex-label cover gives equality in rational cohomology on the original splitting. On a spanning forest one can always solve (5) inductively. Extending the solution to the remaining edges is equivalent to positive product around every cycle. This proves the cycle criterion and shows why odd contacts can be omitted even when they connect distinct vertices of the source. With no even roots (2) is the direct sum of the relative operators. Their kernels then vanish, and their separately oriented cokernels on the compatible relative spaces give the product by complex normalization and continuation. The two relative operators are independent of the clutch in this sector. In particular every graph with only odd contacts at either color gives equal mixed and repeated contributions.

There is a further restriction on the exceptional side. On a connected relative $Y$-source the operator is Dolbeault on a line with structure group $\mu_2$, since $Y$ contracts to the original gerbe. A holomorphic section has constant square. It vanishes identically unless the associated double torsor is trivial, in which case a square-normalized nonzero section trivializes the line. This dichotomy is locally constant in the family. The torsor is proper and flat over the base with geometrically reduced nodal fibers, as is its coarse curve. For the latter assertion use exact finite-group invariants in characteristic zero in balanced charts, which commute with base change and are direct summands for flatness. Its Stein factor is finite étale after localizing on the base, so the number of geometric connected components is locally constant. A trivial double torsor on a connected curve has two components and a nontrivial one has one. [Stein factorization for proper flat families with geometrically reduced fibers, Lemma 37.53.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0E0N).

On a sector where a $Y$-vertex has nontrivial torsor, multiply its evaluations $v(j)$ in (2) by $\lambda\in[0,1]$. Any kernel element has zero section at that entire vertex by its Dolbeault equation. Its equations therefore agree with the original kernel equations at every $\lambda$ and the augmented cokernel rank stays constant. At $\lambda=0$ its former rows are just $u(j)=0$ in the $X$-end fibers. They carry no clutch. Orient this homotopy by continuing the determinant, or by scaling the evaluations also along the Dolbeault path, where the zero-mass comparison is complex-linear. These vertices and their incident edges can thus be deleted before testing (5). Apply the argument on the vertex-label cover and descend the Euler equality by transfer. This retains the vanishing conditions from the deleted rows in the operator. Only their edges are deleted from the sign test.

There is also a vanishing for the product itself. For a $Y$-vertex $F$ of genus $j$ let $A=R^1\pi_*H|_F$ be the Dolbeault cokernel there. It is a vector bundle on each of the locally constant torsor sectors, by cohomology and base change. If $o$ is the number of its external odd ends then orbifold Riemann–Roch and balanced internal normalization give
$$
               \operatorname{rk} A=j-1+o/2+h^0(F,H).
$$
Projection of the form component in (2) to $A$ gives a surjection $V_{a,\eta}\to A$ of real vector bundles. Indeed any class in $A$ can be represented by a forcing on $F$ alone, and images of (2) project to zero. The fixed square-compatible identification on the exceptional side gives the same complex quotient for the second spin. Both oriented real Euler classes are consequently divisible by $c_{\rm top}(A)$, orienting their kernels by the exact sequences.

For the double torsor $T_F\to F$, the pushforward of the structure sheaf splits into its trivial and sign lines. Thus $R^1\pi_*\mathcal O_{T_F}=R^1\pi_*\mathcal O_F\oplus A$. The structure-sheaf cohomologies agree with those of the coarse nodal curves by tameness. Apply Mumford's relation $c(E)c(E^\vee)=1$ for the Hodge bundle to those two families. On a labeling cover one can stabilize each positive-genus connected coarse curve with a marked point when necessary: $F$ has a relative marking, whose inverse images supply markings on each connected covering component. Rational components removed by stabilization preserve $H^1(\mathcal O)$ and genus-zero Hodge factors are trivial. Duality and division of total Chern classes give $c(A)c(A^\vee)=1$. If $A$ has positive rank, the component of twice that complex codimension gives $c_{\rm top}(A)^2=0$. Transfer removes the labels. The mixed and repeated products therefore both vanish on that sector. The three possible exceptional vertices surviving this test are a trivial torsor of genus zero, a nontrivial torsor of genus zero with exactly two odd ends, and a nontrivial torsor of genus one with only even ends. A trivial torsor necessarily has only even ends. [Mumford's Hodge relation, Faber–Pandharipande, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9810173).

## 5. A single relative integral in degree two

In degree two the only graph that can contribute to the difference has one connected vertex on each side and two parallel edges of order two, one of each color. Indeed a negative cycle uses both colors and a contact of order two exhausts that color. The exceptional vertex must have trivial torsor by the evaluation homotopy, and genus zero by the Hodge vanishing. The main relative genus is $h-1$ since the graph has first Betti number one.

Use the minimal twisting choice, with $r=1$ for both possible degree-two orders. The exceptional map contracts to a map to $Y$ which lifts to $\mathbb P^1$ by triviality of its torsor. The lift has coarse degree one. More precisely the relative map has no expansion or attached ghosts. Every nonconstant normalized coarse component of a putative expansion maps onto a two-ended rational target and has at least two preimages of its ends. If $v$ is the number of nonconstant components and $l$ the number of internal source nodes over target joins, the sum of these numbers of preimages is $2+2l$. The genus-zero source graph is a tree, so $l\le v-1$. Equality follows and each component has exactly two ends. Each normalized coarse map is then a power map by Riemann–Hurwitz. The join edges span all nonconstant components, so any remaining tree of contracted rational components would have an unstable leaf. Any inserted target level with only these power maps admits a nontrivial common dilation lifting to the covers, contrary to expansion stability. The remaining relative map is the atlas $\mathbb P^1\to Y$. Its automorphism group is the deck group $\mu_2$. The two relative markings are transverse on uniformizers, and the atlas is étale, so the relative deformation complex is acyclic. Equivalently the differential identifies the two logarithmic tangent lines on $\mathbb P^1$. This component has virtual class $[B\mu_2]$. [Abramovich–Fantechi, Appendix C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1103.5132).

For target genus two take distinct even smooth spins in the indicated degeneration. Write $s(t)=2\sin(t/2)$. For every source genus simultaneously the total on this one graph type (summing its genus distributions and gluing choices) is
$$
 \sum_{h\ge3}\big(L_{2,h}^{+,-}-L_{2,h}^{+,+}\big)t^{2h-2}
           =-2s(t)^2s(2t)^2=-8t^4+\frac{10}{3}t^6+O(t^8).         \tag{6}
$$
Indeed the mixed series is $-s(t)^4/2$ by [Calculation A2](../support/all-genus-double-cover-comparison.md). The repeated-spin series is the connected degree-two balanced series: the two partitions each contribute $s(t)^2s(2t)^2$, and passage to the logarithm subtracts $s(t)^4/2$. This also recovers the leading difference for a genus-$g$ target directly: nonzero binary characters classify connected unramified double covers, each with deck order two. The repeated count is $(2^{2g}-1)/2$ and the mixed character sum is $-1/2$, by Article III, Appendix F. The difference $-2^{2g-1}$ is necessarily carried by the full-contact graph. Equation (6) localizes a series obtained independently on a smooth target. Its coefficients are not evaluations of separate relative vertices.

For any $g\ge2$, let $K_X$ be the relative degree-two genus-$(h-1)$ stack on $X$ with one full marking at each color. Denote its two evaluation maps to $B\mu_2$ by $\mathrm{ev}_p,\mathrm{ev}_q$. After fixing the identifications on the exceptional target, the atlas deck transformation acts by sign at both fibers. Its evaluation map is the diagonal $B\mu_2\to (B\mu_2)^2$. Consequently the matched stack
$$
 \widetilde K_X=K_X\mathop{\times}_{(B\mu_2)^2}B\mu_2
$$
is a representable degree-two étale cover of $K_X$, with pullback virtual class. In (3) the two orders of the colors cancel $2!$ and the split multiplicity is one. The relative kernel of the atlas is the line of constant flat sections, with zero cokernel. Its evaluations identify the two end lines on this cover. Eliminate its surjective Dolbeault block in (2) by any invariant right inverse, then eliminate the constant line against the first matching row. Both eliminations have the complex determinant orientation at zero mass and continue along the mass path. Equivalently the cokernels are identified by inclusion using representatives with zero exceptional forcing and zero first row. On the two sheets the remaining main operator is $u\mapsto(D_Xu,u(q)-T u(p))$ with $T$ or $-T$ as the end identification. Write $e_T,e_{-T}$ for its two Euler classes on $\widetilde K_X$. The deck involution exchanges them and preserves the virtual class. On this stack the mixed term is $e_Te_{-T}$ and the repeated term is $e_T^2$. Symmetrizing the latter gives
$$
 L_{2,h}^{+,-}-L_{2,h}^{+,+}
    =-\tfrac12\int_{[\widetilde K_X]^{\rm vir}} (e_T-e_{-T})^2.       \tag{7}
$$
Equivalently it is minus the integral on $K_X$ of the square of the class in the sign local system whose pullback is $e_T-e_{-T}$. Formula (7) includes all stable maps on the main relative factor. For the genus-two degeneration it gives the series (6), hence integrals of the square on the double cover of $16$ and $-20/3$ in source genera three and four. In genus three the main covers are the four square roots on the elliptic normalization branched at $p+q$, each of deck order two. The two clutches give opposite determinant signs at each by [Calculation D1](even-contact-spin-matching.md), so the square has value four on a stack of degree four, independently recovering $16$.

The two-component semistable model gives (1) in rational homology in all genera. Identifying a separate state on each half for general even profiles, computing individual higher-genus wall corrections, and lifting this comparison to universal cycle classes remain open. Calculation D6 treats a different compactification of the one-node cut. No termwise comparison of the two target models is used here.
