# D4. Virtual gluing for an étale core with one contracted elliptic tail

Cut an irreducible one-node target at its node. We compute the ordinary relative virtual class on the component containing an étale smoothing with one contracted elliptic tail. We then pair the two relative ends with the mixed spin orientation of [Calculation D3](real-spin-gluing-at-finite-contacts.md). The computation includes the limit when the tail attaches over the target node. All virtual classes in this calculation are the ordinary *tangential* Gromov–Witten classes of maps to curves. We specify the normal insertion separately.

## Statement and conventions

The smooth target has genus $g\ge2$. Its normalization at the node is a smooth curve $B$ of genus $g-1$ with ordered points $p,q$. Fix a smooth connected cover $f:Y\to B$ of degree $m$, étale off $p,q$. Its two ramification profiles coincide, with positive orders $k_1,\ldots,k_n$. Fix a matching of the two fibers preserving these orders. Write $P_i,Q_i$ for matched preimages and
$$
             C=\prod_{i=1}^n k_i,\qquad N=\operatorname{lcm}(k_1,\ldots,k_n).
$$
Let $G$ be the deck group of $f$ preserving the matching. Its elements can simultaneously permute pairs of equal order. Indices on the pairs are coordinates on a local chart. The degrees in the statement use the stack with the matching retained and with these simultaneous permutations allowed. The disk parameter $t$ smooths the ordinary target node by $xy=t$. For rational Chow groups take $\mathcal W$ projective over $R=\mathbb C[[t]]$, the completion of an algebraic one-parameter smoothing transverse to the nodal boundary, with fraction field $K$. The plumbing computations concern completed local rings. One may also algebraize the formal plumbing of the stable target using its relatively ample dualizing line.

Let $\mathcal T_f$ denote the closure, in the ordinary relative map stack to $(B,p,q)$, of the maps obtained by attaching a stable one-pointed genus-one curve to $Y$ away from the relative points, mapping it constantly. Include the relative marks and matching as above. Let $\Phi$ glue the two relative ends. Then:

1. This proper cut component has its fundamental cycle as its virtual class, including the points where the attachment lies on a rubber component. Its generic description is $[(Y\setminus\{P_i,Q_i\})\times\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}/G]$. For one full contact the compact component itself is $[Y\times\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}/G]$. Multiple contacts can give singular charts and extra boundary stabilizers.
2. Its contribution to the central tangential virtual cycle per matching is $C\,\Phi_*[\mathcal T_f]$. At a single contact $k$ the complete local chart at the rubber attachment has parameters $a,b,z$ with $t=(ab)^k$, up to the finite stabilizer. The two coordinates $a,b$ smooth the two distinguished nodes and $z$ deforms the pointed tail. Each branch has multiplicity $k$. The intersection of the two branches supplies no further surface cycle.
3. For the two exceptional target-spin gluings considered in Calculation D3, put $\sigma=(-1)^{m-n}$. Let $\lambda$ be the first Chern class of the pointed-tail Hodge line and let $e:\mathcal T_f\to B$ be its image point after target contraction. The specialization of the smooth normal-weighted tail term, for the whole ordinary-target smoothing contribution of this matched cover, is represented by the zero-cycle
$$
     -\sigma C\,\Phi_*\big(\lambda\,e^*c_1(K_B(p+q))\cap[\mathcal T_f]\big).
                                                               \tag{1}
$$
Its degree is $-\sigma C m(g-1)/(12|G|)$. This is a contribution before the change of variable to an ordered spin base. For all-even contacts the cut insertion itself is realized by a regular real normal operator with paired endpoint values on a framed spin branch. For arbitrary profiles (1) computes the specialization of the actual smooth Euler term by its rational characteristic class. It does not require descent of individual spin lines at odd contracted bridges.

Bryan and Pandharipande give the ordinary tangential cycle formula for an irreducible central target just before (16) in Appendix A. Write $d=\prod_k m_k!$, where $m_k$ is the number of parts of size $k$. Over the cut stack without a matching there are $d$ matching choices. The coefficient $C$ on the stack of matchings thus gives their aggregate factor $z(\mu)=Cd$. A separate finite cover numbers the pairs of an already specified matching. If individual numbers are imposed globally, a single labeled component with deck subgroup $G_{\rm lab}$ maps to its matched component with degree $[G:G_{\rm lab}]$. Divide its pushed-forward cycle by this degree to recover (1). The entire cover of simultaneous numberings has degree $d$. For example, on a connected unramified double cover with an equivariant matching, numbering the two pairs removes the deck involution and doubles the labeled contribution. [Bryan–Pandharipande, Appendix A, (14)–(16)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0306316#page=26)

## 1. The cut component

At an ordinary attachment the logarithmic differential
$$
         T_Y(-\textstyle\sum_i(P_i+Q_i))\longrightarrow f^*T_B(-p-q)
$$
is an isomorphism. At a point of contact order $k$ it sends $u\partial_u$ to $kx\partial_x$. The normalized relative cover is rigid and has no infinitesimal automorphisms. Indeed the two-term deformation complex of the pointed map with the contact divisors prescribed is the displayed logarithmic tangent map (with the source and target points respectively fixed as relative sections and divisors). Locally a variation preserving order $k$ is a section of $f^*T_B(-p-q)$ after fixing the marked source coordinates; moving those coordinates gives the indicated source term. Gluing this description gives the complex. Adding the free attaching point supplies its one tangent direction. On the constant tail its image can move to match the moving point. The remaining target obstruction is $H^1(F,\mathcal O_F)\otimes T_{f(y)}B$. Smoothing the attaching node sends its tangent tensor to this space by $df_y\otimes\partial_e$, where $\partial_e:T_eF\to H^1(F,\mathcal O_F)$ is the boundary map for $\mathcal O_F(e)$. This is an isomorphism: both the smooth and the irreducible nodal one-pointed elliptic curve have trivial dualizing line. This argument applies at every pointed-tail modulus. It is the tangential principal-part calculation of Article V, §§4–6.

Suppose the attachment approaches $P_j$. Insert a target line at $p$. Every preimage of $p$ now has a two-pointed source line with map $W=Z_i^{k_i}$. The tail attaches on the $j$-th such line at $Z_j=W=1$. The relative marks lie at the outer ends. A further level covered solely by two-pointed power maps would admit a simultaneous scaling and is removed by stability. The coordinate $W$ on the remaining inserted level is fixed by the tail image. Unmarked power maps have the finite rotations $\mu_{k_i}$ for $i\ne j$ over the fixed rubber; the marked bridge has none. The deck transformations of the main cover fixing this attachment label and the automorphisms of the pointed tail must also be retained. The same description applies over $q$. [Li, Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0009097#page=28)

Here is the complete local ring on a deformation chart before the finite automorphism quotient, with $z$ a pointed-tail parameter:
$$
 P=\mathbb C[[c,a_1,\ldots,a_n,z]]/
                       (a_1^{k_1}-c,\ldots,a_n^{k_n}-c).         \tag{2}
$$
In the explicit family one glues $u_i/Z_i=a_i$ over the target glue $x/W=c$, keeping the opposite relative end and the remaining target fixed. The tail varies with $z$ and remains attached at $1$. The pure contact equations hold even over infinitesimal bases. They have local branch factors which are units and whose product comes from the base; taking roots of the units in characteristic zero gives the displayed powers. [Li, Definition 2.3 and §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0009097#page=19) · [Abramovich–Marcus–Wise, §3.2](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.2892.pdf)

We give the verification that (2) is a hull for the *stable map* with the tail. Let $A$ be its complete local deformation ring on an étale chart. Trivialize the deformation lines of its distinguished nodes and the target expansion parameter in the formal neighborhood. The pure contact equations give $c_A,a_{i,A}$ with $a_{i,A}^{k_i}=c_A$. Choices of the local coordinates multiply the node parameters by units. If all these node smoothing differentials vanish, the normalized main map has zero deformation as above. Each normalized rubber map with its two full contacts varies only by its scalar after fixing endpoint coordinates. Source scalings remove these scalars on the unmarked lines. On the marked line the remaining position/scalar is removed by target rubber scaling. In checking the attachment itself, $d(Z_j^{k_j})_1\otimes\partial_e$ is an isomorphism onto the tail obstruction. A first-order smoothing has the corresponding simple pole on the tail. Deformations preserving the node give no such pole, so its smoothing parameter is zero in the kernel under consideration. Matching the tail's constant image eliminates its remaining translation. The only tangent vector left is the deformation of $(F,e)$. This includes its internal node smoothing when $F$ is nodal.

Choose $z_A$ detecting this last tangent. The resulting $P\to A$ is surjective by cotangent generation and completeness. The explicit family gives $A\to P$ after choosing a lift to the chart. The composite on the distinguished smoothing parameters has invertible linear parts (the smoothing tensor line is intrinsic at each labeled node); for $k_i=1$ the identical linear constraint with $c$ is retained. The tail parameter has nonzero derivative on the remaining direction. The composite is therefore surjective, and a surjective endomorphism of a Noetherian ring is injective. This proves (2) without inferring smoothness from a tangent count on a singular base. Away from the contacts just the moving point and $z$ remain. The same argument there gives a smooth surface. For comparison the pure power equations without the tail are the usual local equations for admissible covers. [Cavalieri–Markwig–Ranganathan, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.4626#page=19)

For clarity about properness, take an arc of ordinary attachment positions approaching a contact, after fixing the cover and tail on finite local charts. The limit of the pointed tail exists by properness of $\overline{\mathcal M}_{1,1}$. At $P_j$ take $c$ to be the $k_j$-th power of its local parameter and after finite base change choose the other $a_i$. The family above extends the arc. Uniqueness in the separated relative stack gives the displayed support; all choices of scalars on unmarked bridges give the same central object. This describes the closure of the ordinary open. Conversely (2) has no additional components at such a point: it is finite free over $\mathbb C[[c,z]]$, étale over $c\ne0$, and reduced. Its generic points come from ordinary attachments. The closure is consequently open and closed along all its points, in the stack with the specified discrete data. The product of the tail stack with the normalizing attachment curve carries the natural contraction to the moving point of $Y$ (up to $G$). On a normalized chart one writes $c=r^N$ and $a_i=\xi_i r^{N/k_i}$. At an endpoint the unmarked rotations permute the normalized branches; before the main deck action the additional stabilizer of one normalized branch has order $N/k_j$ and acts on $r$. Away from the endpoints this marking model has generic degree one over $[Y/G]$. Thus boundary stabilizers do not change the degree of a divisor pulled back from the complete moving-point curve. For one full contact (2) is already smooth with parameter $a_j$; the same moving-point construction over all of $Y$ gives the product quotient in the statement. One may construct it algebraically by blowing up $(P,P),(Q,Q)$ in the core times its marking curve and lifting the map to the target expansion: in $(u,a)$ the ideal $(u^k,a^k)$ becomes invertible after blowing up $(u,a)$.

Riemann–Hurwitz gives $2g(Y)-2=m(2g(B)-2)+2(m-n)$. With the contracted tail and the $2n$ relative marks the ordinary relative virtual dimension is two. Every component of (2) has dimension two and is generically smooth. On the ordinary dense open the tangent calculation and the relative obstruction theory give the fundamental class with coefficient one (a smooth expected-dimensional locus has no excess obstruction in a two-term perfect obstruction theory). Its complement has dimension at most one. Localization in the top Chow dimension proves $[\mathcal T_f]^{\rm vir}=[\mathcal T_f]$ on the proper cut component.

## 2. Self-gluing and smoothing roots

Glue the paired relative points by $\Phi$. At an ordinary tail point over the central target the distinguished source nodes have parameters $s_i$ with $s_i^{k_i}=t$. At the unique inserted level carrying the tail the complete total deformation chart replaces (2) by
$$
 \mathbb C[[a_1,\ldots,a_n,b_1,\ldots,b_n,c_1,c_2,z]]/
       (a_i^{k_i}-c_1,\ b_i^{k_i}-c_2)_{1\le i\le n},
 \qquad t=c_1c_2.                                             \tag{3}
$$
Both ends now have the pure contact equations; the plumbings are $u_i/Z_i=a_i$, $Z_i v_i=b_i$ and $W=Z_i^{k_i}$. The proof of the hull is the proof of (2) with both sets of distinguished node parameters. Once their first derivatives vanish, exactly the same normalized maps remain and only the pointed-tail modulus is free. The unmarked bridge rotations act oppositely at the two ends. In the single cyclic contact case the main deck generator acts by $(a,b)\mapsto(\zeta a,\zeta^{-1}b)$ and fixes the central marked bridge. A generic moving point of the smooth cover breaks that deck symmetry.

The total virtual dimension is three and the total chart is generically smooth of that dimension. In (3) the top central cycle is the Cartier divisor of $c_1c_2$. At the generic point of $c_1=0$, with $c_2\ne0$, the length from the $a_i$ is $C$ (the monomials with $0\le e_i<k_i$ form a basis); the other side gives the same length on its branches. The two choices of cut through the expansion are the two branches at a boundary attachment, while a generic unexpanded marked cover has just one cut for its fixed matching. Their intersection has dimension one and gives no additional surface class. This recovers locally the contribution $C\Phi_*[\mathcal T_f]$ in the ordinary nonseparating virtual formula. It also identifies the persistent-tail family in the total chart, which will be used to extend the normal insertion. Stacks are always integrated with their actual stabilizers. A degree of a root map, a generic quotient degree and a stabilizer at one central point are distinct here.

The normalization of $s_i^{k_i}=t$ has $C/N$ framed branches, each with $t=w^N$, $s_i=\gamma_i w^{N/k_i}$. This follows by choosing all $C$ roots and dividing by the free common action of $\mu_N$ on the root tuples. It is the ordinary-target description. On an ordered exceptional spin base $\delta^2=t$, put $D=\operatorname{lcm}(N,2)$. There are $C\,2/D$ branches over the $\delta$ disk, each of degree $D/2$. Indeed a loop about $\delta=0$ acts on the tuple by the square of an ordinary-target loop. For an even single contact $k$, the two branches $\delta=\pm s^{k/2}$ each have degree $k/2$. For $k=2$ the multiplicity over each of these spin branches is one. The total over a fixed nonzero $\delta$ still contains $C$ framed tuples. A quotient by $G$ weights the orbits by their actual stabilizers; summing the orbit weights gives $C/|G|$.

For example, for orders $(2,4)$ the eight root tuples split into two ordinary branches of degree four, or four ordered-spin branches of degree two. At the end where the tail meets the contact of order two, one normalized cut branch has an additional stabilizer of order two. This stabilizer is trivial when the tail meets the order-four contact. The root counts and these stabilizers can be checked directly by enumerating the phases and their rotations. Exact enumeration in `code/relative_elliptic_tail_gluing.py`

## 3. Specializing the mixed normal class

On a smooth target let $X$ be the étale core. Its core normal operator is invertible, and the cokernel after attaching $F$ is the real underlying bundle of $\mathbb E_F^\vee\otimes f^*(\theta_+\oplus\theta_-)$ at the attachment, with sign $\sigma$. Here the two target spins have the exceptional-type matching, so $\sigma$ is as in Calculation D3 for every root tuple. The normal Euler class on the product of the moving point and tail moduli is
$$
       -\sigma\lambda\, c_1(\omega_X).
$$
In fact the squared evaluations of $\theta_+$ and $\theta_-$ both give $f^*K$, the two-torsion difference has zero rational first Chern class, and $df$ identifies $f^*K$ with $\omega_X$. On the generic fiber over $K$, pass to a finite extension splitting the core and ordered spin data. The moving point and the pointed-tail modulus each range over a curve over this field, so the two square terms vanish in rational Chow on their respective factors. The displayed product is defined over $K$ by the dualizing and Hodge lines. Rational pullback to the splitting field is injective since its pushforward composed with pullback multiplies by the extension degree. Thus it computes the descended rational class for the spin pair. This identifies a rational algebraic representative of the actual oriented smooth Euler class. Article V, §§4–6

The family with a persistent tail carries its pointed-tail map and the image point of the constant component in the unexpanded target, after contracting all rubber. These are morphisms on the above charts, invariant under changes of frames and stabilizers. In the explicit local families one takes the gluing section and the pointed tail. They agree on overlaps, including the relative endpoint, by persistence and uniqueness of the separating genus-one factor. Thus $-\sigma\lambda\,\operatorname{ev}_F^*c_1(\omega_{\mathcal W/\Delta})$ extends the smooth representative over this family, where $\mathcal W$ denotes the unexpanded family of targets. On the cut target its dualizing line pulls back to $K_B(p+q)$, including the logarithmic contributions at both ends. The ordinary virtual gluing term and the projection formula now give (1). Equivalently, specialize the smooth normal-weighted cycle by Cartier Gysin using this extension: any difference between two extensions of the same generic Chow cycle is supported over zero and has zero refined pullback by the self-intersection formula for the trivial normal line of the base point. This is an identification of the *specialization* on this component; equality of the two smooth integrals alone would not identify that cycle.

To evaluate, the degree of $e^*K_B(p+q)$ on the complete moving-point curve, before the deck quotient, is $m(2g-2)$. This can also be obtained by intersecting with a divisor of the same degree away from $p,q$, where the marking presentation is ordinary (or by linear equivalence of a difference of such divisors). The tail factor gives $\int\lambda=1/24$. It follows that the cut term per fixed matching is $-\sigma m(g-1)/(12|G|)$ and the ordinary-target gluing multiplies this by $C$. If one retains the cut normalization $Y$, the evaluation line equals $K_Y(\sum_i(P_i+Q_i))$ by logarithmic Riemann–Hurwitz. In particular the calculation includes attachments specializing at odd contacts, even though an individual reduced source spin has degree one on their exceptional bridge.

## 4. A real operator on the unbalanced paired chart

When every $k_i=2l_i$ is even, the source spin and its regular real operator can be constructed on the unexpanded admissible core, including an attaching point at its node. On a framed normalized spin branch choose $n_x=\ell_x/u^{l_i}$ and $n_y=\ell_y/v^{l_i}$ in the two branch frames of Calculation D3. Since $uv=s_i$ and $\delta^2=t=s_i^{k_i}$, their transition $\varepsilon i\delta/s_i^{l_i}$ is constant. This defines $H_\varepsilon$ on the unexpanded nodal source. On $Y$ the common normalized line is
$$
               H_Y=f^*A_0\big(\textstyle\sum_i l_i(P_i+Q_i)\big),
        \qquad H_Y^2=f^*K_B(p+q),
$$
where $A_0^2=K_B$. The square maps through the logarithmic differential to the dualizing line of the paired core.

There is a convenient smooth target metric giving continuous coefficients on the unbalanced family. On the exceptional target-spin charts put $w=\delta/x$ and take $h_x=(1+|w|^2)^{-1}$ for the squared norm of $\ell_x$. Its companion in the other frame is regular, and one can interpolate to a smooth positive metric outside a smaller neck. Transport the actual operator by the line identification on smooth fibers. In the $n_x$ frame its antilinear coefficient on the unexpanded source neck becomes exactly
$$
       k_i\big(u^{l_i}\bar u^{l_i-1}d\bar u
                    -v^{l_i}\bar v^{l_i-1}d\bar v\big).          \tag{4}
$$
Indeed $|w|^2=|v/u|^{2l_i}$ and $d\bar v=-(\bar v/\bar u)d\bar u$ along the fiber. This is an ambient form also at $u=v=0$. The gluing and sup-norm estimates in [Calculation D3](real-spin-gluing-at-finite-contacts.md) give continuity of the resulting real Fredholm operators in the unbalanced charts, after any of the indicated base changes. The central kernel vanishes by integration of the spin identity on $Y$: the matched values in the two branch frames make the logarithmic residues of $\sigma_H w_0^2$ cancel for a kernel section $w_0$. The core index is zero. Any inserted two-ended source lines on which the coefficient is pulled back by contraction have trivial line, zero mass and constant matching. Attaching the pointed elliptic curve now gives the arithmetic Hodge cokernel with evaluation $H_\varepsilon$ at the moving point, including at the paired node. The same proof works when the main cover and rubber scalings are rigidified; take finite invariant metrics on a chart with stabilizers preserving the chosen spin gluing.

On the actual cut component the two Hodge factors have the common evaluation of $H_Y$ and their product has signed Euler class $-\sigma\lambda\,e^*c_1(K_B(p+q))$. One can see the vanishing of the remaining square term by contraction of the marked core to its moving point on $Y$; the evaluation line is pulled back from that curve. This realizes the insertion of (1) by a real normal problem with paired endpoints. On a quotient that permutes spin branches, the rational insertion in (1) is invariant. We make no assertion of an individually equivariant spin line for a deck transformation that changes the branch data.

For a cyclic cover with a single full even contact $k$, the main deck group has order $k$, with inverse characters on the two branches. It preserves the gluing and the descended operator. Such examples exist for every even $k$ by the cyclic construction of [Calculation D3](real-spin-gluing-at-finite-contacts.md). Here $\sigma=-1$. The cut relative normal pairing is $+(g-1)/12$, and the glued ordinary-target contribution is $+k(g-1)/12$ for that matched core. In genus two and degree two these are $+1/12$ and $+1/6$. The resolved spin-target evaluation in Calculation D2 puts $+1/12$ on its exceptional curve per smooth root. The intrinsic unbalanced model uses (4) and the target dualizing insertion. An extra seam contribution added to (1) would count part of the same smooth specialization twice.

This proves a virtual pairing with nonzero contact data on the one-elliptic-tail sector. The finite ramified component of the same excess, strata with several ghosts or nonseparating contracted components, and a gluing theorem for the entire split-spin theory still require separate arguments. The result concerns a numerical local theory of a fixed degeneration and does not construct a universal evaluation on arbitrary analytic parameter families.
