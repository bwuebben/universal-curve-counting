# E5. Polar kernels on double covers with two branch values

Let $C$ be a closed curve of genus $g\ge2$ and $N$ a complex rank-two bundle of degree $2g-2$. At a point $c\in C$ fix a complex line $\ell\subset N_c$. On a smooth double cover $u$ branched over $c$ and one other value, consider odd solutions of the pullback normal equation with at most one simple pole, in direction $\ell$, over $c$. Their signed count in a one-parameter family measures the change of an Euler number on the compact curve of marked covers. Two explicit choices of the normal operator force such solutions to occur in every target genus.

Write $n=2^{2g}$. Use the compact unmarked gerbe $\mathcal H$ and the ramification curve $R$ of [Calculation E4](the-marked-double-cover-surface-at-a-quartic-join.md). Their real dimensions are four and two. The marked curve $R$ includes the trivalent rational component at every point where the two branch values coalesce. Write $S=T_{\rm mark}\otimes\ell$. At a real Cauchy–Riemann operator $D$ with invertible base, ordinary injective upper family, and injective principal-part maps $P:S\to F$ on $R$, put
$$
 F_2(D)=\int_{\mathcal H} e(F),\qquad
 E(D;c,\ell)=\int_R e(F|_R/P(S)).
$$
Here $F$ denotes the full real cokernel, of rank four, pulled back when necessary. The quotient orientation uses the complex orientation of $S$ first. Write $\epsilon(D)$ for the base determinant sign.

**Theorem.** Both injectivity hypotheses hold after arbitrarily small smooth fixed-symbol perturbations preserving a prescribed transverse embedded odd double graph with more than two simple branch values. Here $c$ runs over its branch values and $\ell$ is the corresponding vertical tangent line. The base is assumed invertible and the lower crossing remains transverse.

For any endpoint satisfying the displayed injectivity hypotheses, join a generic complex-linear reference to $D$ by a generic path of real fixed-symbol operators. Let $W_4$ count signed projective odd kernels on smooth double covers with two distinct branch values. Let $W_2(c,\ell)$ count signed projective odd solutions with a simple pole in direction $\ell$ at the ramification over $c$. The signs are the local Fredholm degrees defined below. These are finite counts, and
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 F_2(D)&=\epsilon(D)\left(\frac{n(g-1)(g-2)}{16}+W_4\right),\\
 E(D;c,\ell)&=\epsilon(D)\left(\frac{n(g-1)}4+W_2(c,\ell)\right).
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{1}
$$
The path may cross a base wall. Its interior operators need not carry a lower graph. The two integers depend only on their endpoints. In particular, if $D$ is the repeated positive spin operator and $\ell$ is either isotropic line at $c$, then
$$
  E(D;c,\ell)=-\frac n4,\qquad
  W_2(c,\ell)=-\frac {ng}4.                                       \tag{2}
$$
For two distinct positive spin lines with either isotropic choice one has $E(D;c,\ell)=0$ and $W_2(c,\ell)=-n(g-1)/4$. In genus two every generic path to the repeated spin endpoint has at least eight polar crossings counted without signs, for each fixed $c,\ell$. The events for different flags need not have distinct times.

## The odd Fredholm families

In a coordinate $y$ at the upper mark, choose an invariant disk and an even cutoff $\chi$ equal to one near zero. Use a frame holomorphic for the complex-linear part of $D$. To the odd domain of $D_u=u^*D$ adjoin the complex plane of real dimension two represented by principal parts $\chi v/y$, with $v\in\ell$ in these coordinates. Take the ordinary image off the pole, extended smoothly. Indeed the antilinear coefficient is divisible by $\bar y$ where $u'(0)=0$. On the rational component the pullback operator is just the Dolbeault operator. The local meromorphic lift there may be averaged into its odd part under the deck action, which preserves the principal part. Changes of coordinates, frame and lift add smooth domain sections. Thus this augmentation of the domain is intrinsic; a cutoff merely splits the extension locally. Smooth parameter-dependent choices can be made in smooth families of curves and operators. Continuous stabilization at a collision uses forms supported off the nodes, together with this extra column on a disk about the smooth marking. On that disk the quotient $\overline{u'(y)}/\bar y$ extends continuously in the family on $R$. These assertions also hold at an operator with kernel by adding finite-dimensional supplements first. [Bai–Swaminathan, Appendix B, (B.5)–(B.9)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4#A2).

The invariant part has the oriented determinant of the base operator. To check this, a homogeneous invariant solution descends off the branch values and extends by bounded removability. The same holds with forcing pulled back from smooth base forms away from the special values, by subtracting local solutions there. On a nodal double use the normalization and matching, and on the trivalent component use constant invariant sections. The invariant index is the base index, by deformation to the complex-linear finite pushforward, with the constants on the inserted bridge imposing the same matching. Stabilize the base by forms on regular disks: the stabilized invariant and base kernels coincide, and this identifies their determinant lines. The isomorphism is complex-oriented at a complex operator and continues along all real operators. The polar column is odd. Thus the two odd families have real indices $-4$ and $-2$. At a regular endpoint their cokernels carry the orientations of $F$ and $F/P(S)$ multiplied by $\epsilon(D)$. All exchanged even-dimensional factors preserve orientation.

## Perturbations preserving a lower wall

Let $f:Y\to C$ be the lower double, with chosen odd generator $\xi$. Fix a disk of regular target values on which a sheet of its embedded graph has nonzero height. Perturb $D$ by smooth real zeroth-order tensors supported there and annihilating $\xi$ on this sheet. They also annihilate the opposite height. A small perturbation preserves the lower kernel exactly as a line, its branch/time transversality and any open jet hypotheses imposed on the original crossing.

Consider a nonzero odd upper solution, ordinary or augmented, denoted by $H$ on the punctured source. In the augmented problem it equals a principal part plus a smooth section and solves the homogeneous equation away from the mark. After deleting all branch values and poles, the upper and lower sign characters are different: the lower has more than two branch values and the upper has two or zero. On the connected common cover their lifts transform by distinct characters. On some regular subdisk of the testing disk $H$ and $\xi$ are real-linearly independent. Otherwise $H=a\xi$ there on one sheet, where the scalar $a$ is real and smooth. The equation yields $\bar\partial a=0$ and $a$ is constant. Unique continuation on the common cover contradicts the character distinction. This argument includes any connected étale upper double.

For the universal kernel equation, an annihilator of its differential in the odd target solves the odd adjoint equation away from the special points, by variation of the smooth domain section. On the subdisk the allowed operator perturbations can prescribe any value on $H$ while killing $\xi$. The adjoint pairings on the two upper sheets coincide by oddness. Localized testing forces the adjoint to vanish, first on that disk and then throughout. The dual is represented by an $L^{p/(p-1)}$ form for the $W^{1,p}\to L^p$ equation with $p>2$, so no functional supported only at the marked point remains. Together with the Fredholm range this proves transversality. For smooth stable covers use local smooth trivializations of the sources and of the pole column to apply Sard–Smale on countably many charts. For connected étale doubles there are finitely many characters. Subtracting one real dimension for the choice of a kernel line, the respective projection indices are
$$
     -4+4-1=-1,\qquad -2+2-1=-1,\qquad 0+0-1=-1.
$$
Thus generically all these smooth families have zero kernel. At a nodal finite cover upper injectivity follows from invertibility on its étale normalization, including the base operator in the split case. At a trivalent component the principal part contributes the difference of the two endpoint values, a nonzero scalar multiple of its prescribed residue vector, as in Calculation E4. Compactness and the continuous stabilized families now give openness on the whole surface and ramification curve. One can work in sufficiently high finite differentiability, then approximate within the real subbundle of tensors annihilating the lower section. This gives density in the smooth topology, simultaneously for finitely many lower flags. A time cutoff inserts the perturbation at the original lower crossing. More than two branch values is essential to the ordinary assertion: with two, the unmarked upper family contains the lower cover itself.

## The signed counts

For a generic complex-linear operator on $N$, the base and all its étale sign twists have no kernel. The odd unmarked operators and the augmented ones are also generically injective. On smooth covers their complex indices are $-2$ and $-1$, offset by the respective complex parameter dimensions two and one before removing complex scaling. Complex-linear endomorphism variations supported on a regular target disk act arbitrarily on any nonzero value of one odd solution. The preceding adjoint test therefore applies with complex projectivization, even with fixed $c,\ell$. The nodal tests follow from the regular étale normalizations. This constructs a reference on the given smooth complex bundle.

Along a generic real path with the prescribed regular endpoints, use the odd systems alone. In the smooth loci the same universal projective argument, now with unrestricted real variations supported also in time, has index zero. A transverse zero is a simple kernel since a kernel of real dimension at least two gives a positive-dimensional projective fiber. A solution of the augmented problem with zero residue would be an ordinary kernel with a branch fixed at $c$. That incidence has path index $-4+2+1-1=-2$ and is avoided.

At an unmarked nodal cover with collision point $p$, an odd kernel is a nonzero solution $v$ of $D^\alpha$ on the flat sign twist of $N$ satisfying $v(p)=0$. The trivial character covers the split case. At a trivalent marked limit $p=c$, an odd augmented kernel requires $v(c)\in\ell$ in a sign frame. Indeed an odd meromorphic section on the bridge with at most one simple pole is determined by its opposite endpoint values, which lie in the residue line. A solution vanishing on the normalization is zero by this description. For an operator path, adding $p$ and imposing the former condition gives index $0+1-1+2-4=-2$. Fixing $c$ and imposing the latter gives $0+1-1-2=-2$. For transversality test away from the evaluation point with arbitrary operator variations where the solution is nonzero. This kills the adjoint as before, and arbitrary variations of the smooth section at the evaluation point kill any remaining evaluation functional. Thus generic paths avoid both strata for every character, even when a base or étale operator becomes singular elsewhere. Continuous stabilized reduction and properness of the compact parameter spaces preclude accumulation. This gives finite smooth crossing sets.

For index $-d$ with $d=4,2$, orient the smooth cover parameters complexly and the time by increasing time. At a simple kernel the derivative into $\operatorname{Hom}(\ker,\operatorname{coker})$ is an isomorphism. Orient this space using the odd Fredholm determinant and any generator of the kernel. Reversing the generator changes both its evaluation and the induced cokernel orientation, giving the same sign for even $d$. Equivalently choose the sign so that the Euler integral on the boundary of a small oriented ball is positive for the identity derivative. Indeed the reduced linear map sends a generator to its radial parameter vector, and its quotient on the link is the tangent bundle of $S^d$. The link integral is twice the local sign. Both parameter stacks have stabilizer $\mu_2$ acting by sign on odd sections and trivially on parameters. Stack integration divides the link by two, including at the marked ramification point. Excision gives an increase of one signed unit per crossing in the odd Euler integral. This proves the variation in (1) and the independence of the signed counts from the comparison path. [Bai–Swaminathan, local sphere calculation (6.36)–(6.37)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4#S6.E36).

## The reference integrals

We compute on the ordered cover $C_x\times C_y$ of $\operatorname{Sym}^2 C$. Additional étale choices of the square root have degree $n$, and integration over the root gerbe has weight $1/2$. In rational cohomology the universal root on $C_z\times C_x\times C_y$ has first Chern class $m=(D_x+D_y)/2$, where $D_x=(z=x)$, $D_y=(z=y)$. This notation means pullback to the gerbe of roots over the ordered cover. At a complex reference the odd index is $I=R\pi_*(N\otimes L^{-1})$ with $\pi$ forgetting $z$. Since $\operatorname{ch}(N)\operatorname{td}(C)=2$ on the curve, Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch gives
$$
 \operatorname{rk} I=-2,\qquad c_1(I)=\pi_*m^2,\qquad
 \operatorname{ch}_2(I)=-\tfrac13\pi_*m^3.
$$
Set $k=2-2g$. Let $X,Y$ denote the point classes of the two factors and $\Delta$ their diagonal. Squaring a graph divisor inserts the tangent class of $C$ on the graph. Hence $c_1(I)=(k(X+Y)+2\Delta)/4$, and the integral of $\operatorname{ch}_2(I)$ on the ordered surface is $-k/4$. Indeed both pure cubes vanish and each mixed product $D_x^2D_y,D_xD_y^2$ integrates to $k$. Using $X\Delta=Y\Delta=XY=1$ in degree and $\Delta^2=k$, we obtain
$$
   \int_{C^2}c_2(-I)=k(k+2)/16.
$$
Converting to $\mathcal H$ multiplies by $n/4$ (ordered pairs and the gerbe each supply a divisor of two). This gives $F_2^{\rm hol}=n(g-1)(g-2)/16$.

On $R$ restrict to the ordered slice $(c,y)$, without the factor for unordered pairs. The remaining transfer is $n/2$, hence $\int_R c_1(I)=n(k+2)/8$. Adding the polar line gives index $I+S$, with $\int_R c_1(S)=-n/4$ by the cotangent formula of Calculation E4. The injective complex augmented family has a complex cokernel line and
$$
  \int_R c_1(-I-S)=n(g-1)/4.
$$
This completes (1).

## The repeated spin endpoint

Take $N=\theta\oplus\theta$, $\theta^2=K_C$, with two identical real positive spin operators and the same spin norm. In the chosen square-root identification the two isotropic lines for $v_1^2+v_2^2$ at $c$ are $(v,\pm iv)$. For an augmented kernel on a smooth cover, write $u_1,u_2$ for its two sections and $\sigma$ for the spin multiplication given by the derivative of the upper cover. Integrate the spin norm identity on the complement of a disk around the pole. The cross terms are the real part, up to a fixed normalization, of the boundary integral of $\sigma(u_1^2+u_2^2)$. Locally the derivative vanishes simply, and each section is $v_j/y$ plus a smooth section. The only possible residue is a scalar multiple of $v_1^2+v_2^2$, so the boundary integral tends to zero for either line. Both $\bar\partial u_j$ and $\sigma u_j$ are square integrable on the punctured disk. The sum of their squared norms must then vanish, forcing the sections to vanish wherever $\sigma$ is nonzero and hence everywhere. Ordinary injectivity follows from the same identity without a pole. At a tripod the endpoint-difference computation applies directly. Article V, §§4–6.

Write $V$ for the real oriented spin plane cokernel. The ordinary cokernel of the repeated operator is $V\oplus V$ with its product Fredholm orientation. Rotation $I(a,b)=(-b,a)$ commutes with the equation. With this complex structure $V\oplus V$ is the complexification of $V$, of first Chern class zero. Its complex basis ordered as $(a,Ia,b,Ib)$ differs by one transposition from a product-oriented basis. For the line $(v,-iv)$ the action of $I$ on $S$ is $+i$, so the quotient orientation is opposite its induced complex orientation and its Euler class is $c_1(S)$. For the other line the action on $S$ is $-i$, reversing its orientation, and the quotient has the induced complex orientation and again has Euler class $c_1(S)$. The ordinary base determinant sign is positive because the summands are identical. This proves (2). Article V, §§4–6.

In particular the ordinary spin value is $F_2^{\rm spin}=-n(g-1)/8$, so that $W_4=-ng(g-1)/16$ between the same references. For the bundle model of Calculation E4 the signed number $-2F_2-E$ is $-ng(g-1)/8$ at the complex reference and $+ng/4$ at the repeated spin endpoint. Both calibrations concern actual upper Fredholm operators. Neither calibration presupposes a transverse lower wall at either endpoint. To use (1) for a joined stable map requires a comparison of its smoothing equation with the augmented family.

## Distinct spin lines and the moving branch point

Let $N=a\oplus b$ for two distinct spin lines with square maps to $K_C$. Write $Q$ for their sum-of-squares form and $\ell$ for either isotropic line at $c$. The preceding Stokes argument works for the distinct pair: the only pole term is proportional to $Q(v,v)$ and vanishes. It also gives injectivity if a smooth nonnegative multiplier of the mass is positive on a nonempty regular disk of each positive component. For this last assertion, integrate $d(du\,Q(s,s))$ for a homogeneous solution. After the fixed area normalization its bulk term is the nonnegative squared mass density times the multiplier. This version uses no derivative of the multiplier. The normalization argument treats nodal limits. Choose such a multiplier vanishing near both branch values in every fiber, and interpolate linearly from one. The compact ordinary and polar families keep zero kernel, hence keep their oriented Euler numbers.

There is a description of the resulting odd polar family on the fixed target. Let $N^+$ be the elementary modification allowing a simple pole in direction $\ell$ at $c$. Locally take holomorphic isotropic frames $e_+,e_-$ with $e_+(c)$ spanning $\ell$. The frame $e_+/(w-c),e_-$ of $N^+$ makes $Q$ a nondegenerate form with values in $K_C(c)$. With $p$ the second branch value, form $U=N^+\otimes L^{-1}$ on $C\times R$, where $L^2=\mathcal O(c+p)$ with its universal branch section. Multiplying the form by this section as a map on $U$ gives a symmetric map $\sigma:U\to K_C U^*$ vanishing simply along $w=p$ in both entries, invertible elsewhere.

To compare the cokernels write $d^2=s_{c+p}$ and express the original odd section as $d\,v$. Away from the two values the real equation on $v$ is the Dolbeault equation with mass proportional to the Hermitian conjugate of $\sigma$: the transformed conjugation coefficient is $\bar d/d=\overline{s_{c+p}}/|s_{c+p}|$ in a local frame. The extra positive scalar is absorbed into the metric there. Continue the metric smoothly inside the region where the cutoff vanishes. On the branch disks the equation is holomorphic, and the allowable coefficient in the modified line has a simple pole at $c$ before applying $d$. At the collision use $w-c=A(\zeta-1)^2/\zeta$ and $d=A(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})$. Their ratio is $(\zeta+1)/(\zeta-1)$ and has opposite finite endpoint values on the marked bridge. The ordinary coefficient $d$ vanishes on that bridge, with the two outside branches given by the nodal double. These formulas identify all local holomorphic sections with the prescribed residue and matching, also in the central fiber. In particular the matching difference in the modified axis is free and the complementary axis obeys the original matching. Smooth inhomogeneous forms supported on regular disks and their odd lifts via multiplication by $d$ yield the same normalized solutions. Finite common supplements therefore identify the cokernels continuously. Indeed the change of section is smooth on the thick complement, and on the zero-mass disks the preceding regular functions on the plumbing give the extension for each holomorphic coefficient. Conversely a bounded odd holomorphic section divided by $d$ extends by removability with exactly the one prescribed principal part. The indices agree by complex finite pushforward with this modification, or by the rank and degree of $U$. This proves the comparison for the odd sector including the tripod. It suffices up to orientation here.

On the fixed target we may now increase the cutoff to one while retaining injectivity by the same Stokes identity for the holomorphic symmetric map $\sigma$. The complexification of the resulting real cokernel is the orthogonal residue plane
$$
                    (K_C U^*)|_{w=p}.
$$
For clarity, the two-term sheaf complex $[U\xrightarrow{\sigma}K_C U^*]$ has only this skyscraper cokernel, since $\sigma$ is a scalar simple zero times a nondegenerate form. In the Dolbeault resolution the middle term has pairs $(\beta,v)$ with the bilinear pairing $\int(\beta v'+\beta'v)$. The Hermitian metric gives the involution $(\beta,v)\mapsto(c_H v,c_H^{-1}\beta)$. The image of the first differential plus its image under that involution is the orthogonal complement of the harmonic middle space, by symmetry and integration by parts. Projecting its fixed harmonic space to the first component identifies the real cokernel with a positive real form of the residue plane. Cutoff representatives identify the orthogonal pairing on the skyscraper with the residue pairing for $\sigma^{-1}$. This is the rank-two version of the spin-residue calculation in Article V, §§4–6.

On the étale double $\widetilde C\to C$ associated to $b/a$, the two isotropic lines of $N$ are ordered and each is isomorphic to the pullback of $a$. The deck group exchanges them. The modification increases one degree at the first lift of $c$ and the other degree at the other lift, so the two degrees on $\widetilde C$ remain equal. The coarse cover of $R$ obtained by moving $p$ kills all sign characters and hence maps to $\widetilde C$. Thus the ordered isotropic lines in $(K_C U^*)|_{w=p}$ have equal degrees: dualizing and tensoring by the common canonical and root factors preserves the equality. The residue pairing puts them in perfect duality, so both degrees vanish. In an oriented orthogonal real plane the two isotropic lines of its complexification have Chern classes equal to the Euler class and its negative. It follows that $E(D;c,\ell)=0$ for distinct spins, with either base determinant sign. Equations (1) and Article V, §§4–6 now give $W_2=-n(g-1)/4$ and $W_4=-n(g-1)(g-2)/16$. In genus two there are at least four prescribed-flag polar crossings to the distinct endpoint.

## Arbitrarily many ordinary crossings at a fixed degree and genus

There is a related consequence for ordinary smooth sections, without prescribed poles. Put $L_+L_-=K_C$ with $\deg L_\pm=g-1\pm a$, $a\ge0$. Use the positive spin-type operator for the nondegenerate symmetric pairing between the two axes of $N=L_+\oplus L_-$. Write $D_a$ for this operator. Over a curve these bundles are all isomorphic as smooth complex bundles by rank and first Chern class. One may therefore compare the operators along paths on the same $N$. The spin energy identity gives injectivity on each finite cover and on the normalizations of nodal covers. The base satisfies $\epsilon(D_a)=(-1)^a$. The oriented finite upper integral is
$$
             F_2(D_a)=-\frac{n}{8}\big(2a^2+g-1\big).                         \tag{3}
$$
For $a=0$ one may use the repeated spin endpoint with its two isotropic axes. For every even $a$ the signed number of ordinary odd crossings from $D_0$ here to $D_a$ on a generic path is therefore $-na^2/4$. The index $0$ on $D_0$ in this paragraph denotes the balanced spin endpoint.

Here is a direct computation of (3). A diagonal Hermitian metric makes rotation $I=(i,-i)$ commute with the pullback operators, including along the homotopy from zero to positive mass. For an ordinary complex-linear family the complex index with respect to $I$ is the usual index on the positive axis plus the conjugate index on the negative axis. The orientation relative to the ordinary Fredholm determinant changes by $(-1)^{\chi_-}$ at zero mass, by complex conjugation on the negative kernel and cokernel. This remains the orientation difference throughout the homotopy. On the base $\chi_-=-a$. On the entire finite upper family the negative-axis index is the sum of its base index $-a$ and its odd index $-a-1$, so the full real cokernel has orientation opposite its $I$-complex orientation. The constant even index has no positive-degree Chern character.

On the ordered parameter square used above let $I_\pm=R\pi_*(L_\pm\otimes L^{-1})$ be the ordinary complex odd indices. For a point class $\omega$ on the target put $H=\pi_*(\omega m)=(X+Y)/2$. The last equality follows by intersecting each of the two universal graph divisors with a fixed value of $z$. Riemann–Roch gives $c_1(I_\pm)=\pi_*m^2/2\mp aH$. Thus
$$
       c_1(F,I)=a(X+Y),\qquad
       \int_{C^2}\operatorname{ch}_2(F,I)=k/4,\qquad k=2-2g.
$$
Indeed the degree-four Chern character of a conjugate complex bundle equals the original one, so the second equality is the calculation for $-I_+-I_-$ above. The $I$-complex second Chern number on the ordered square is $a^2-k/4$. Multiplying by $n/4$ for the square-root choices, ordering and gerbe and reversing the orientation gives (3). At complex mass zero the relative finite pushforward computes the full index also on a nodal double. The homotopy through real operators preserves its $I$-complex index, irrespective of jumps in the intermediate Dolbeault kernel. This calculation uses the same reciprocal action that gives Article V, Theorem 1.1 and §2. In the unrestricted fixed-symbol operator space the axis degree has no bound from a prescribed flat holonomy representation.

In particular, fix a target of genus two and choose $a=2j$. Every generic comparison path as above encounters at least $16j^2$ smooth degree-two cover–parameter pairs in source genus four with an odd **ordinary** kernel. If a fixed line over $c$ is identified with the positive isotropic axis at both endpoints, [Calculation E7](prescribed-polar-crossings-in-arbitrary-source-genus.md) also gives a signed difference of $-16j$ in the one-pole test. These are different incidences: a generic ordinary crossing avoids the prescribed branch value. The degree, both genera and the smooth target bundle are fixed as $j$ varies. The endpoints of the paths vary.

The same endpoints determine all the ordinary signed counts. Let $a>0$ be even, $b_0=g-1$, and let $C_j$ be the forward change in the Bai–Swaminathan smooth double-cover count in genus $2g-1+j$, with the relative (odd) determinant convention. With $T_a$ the Chebyshev polynomial $T_a(\cos v)=\cos(av)$, one has
$$
           \sum_{j\ge0} C_j x^j
              =2(4-x)^{b_0}\big(T_a(1-x/2)-1\big),
            \qquad x=(2\sin(v/2))^2.                                      \tag{4}
$$
Indeed the compact fixed-target rotation and virtual-index proof of Article V, Theorem 1.1 and §2 uses the two axes, their degrees, and the positive mass. It applies to these operators on the stable-map stack for any $a$; no realization by flat holonomy is needed for this Fredholm calculation. For the two partitions of degree two, the local curve formula gives disconnected series $2\cos(av)x^{2b_0}(4-x)^{b_0}$. The base sign is positive at both endpoints, so their degree-one series agree and taking the logarithm leaves the same difference in degree two. Subtract the reference term of Article IV, Appendix D at the two endpoints. The reference terms cancel, and division by $x^{2b_0}$ gives (4). [Bryan–Pandharipande, Corollary 7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411037v3#page=34) · [Bai–Swaminathan, Definitions 5.17 and 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4).

The top occupied genus is $3g-2+a$, with signed count $(-1)^{a+b_0}$ there, since the leading coefficient of $T_a$ is $2^{a-1}$. The coefficient of $x$ is $-4^{b_0}a^2$, also obtained from (3). For $g=2,a=2$ the polynomial is $-16x+8x^2-x^3$. Thus the three nonzero signed counts are $-16,8,-1$ in source genera four, five, six. Varying the endpoint makes the highest forced genus arbitrarily large at projection degree two, with no poles. Vanishing of a signed coefficient permits cancellation of crossings in that genus.
