# E4. The marked double-cover surface at a quartic join

Fix a lower ramification point $r$ of the embedded double graph of [Calculation E3](a-ramified-double-joined-to-an-unramified-cover.md). Write $c=f(r)$, $\ell=\mathbb C\,\partial_z\xi(r)\subset N_c$, and $n=2^{2g}$. One source genus above the first quartic contribution, consider a second, zero-height double cover of arithmetic genus $2g$, with a flag over $c$ that joins it to the lower graph. When the lower cover has at least six simple branch values, these compact marked families exhaust the remaining configurations in the specified incidence of Calculation E2 after the contributions in Calculation E3. The geometry of the family identifies an obstruction to computing its contribution from divisor intersections alone.

## The marked surface and its ramification

Put $M=\operatorname{Sym}^2 C$ and $A=M\times_{\operatorname{Pic}^2 C}\operatorname{Pic}^1 C$, with the squaring map on the second factor. The degree of the étale map $A\to M$ is $n$. A finite double cover is specified by its branch divisor $B$ and a line bundle $L$ with an identification $L^2\simeq\mathcal O_C(B)$. Its algebra is $\mathcal O_C\oplus L^{-1}$, with multiplication given by the branch section. Thus the stack $\mathcal H$ of unmarked covers is a $\mu_2$-gerbe over $A$. For $B=2p$ the normalization is the unramified double of $L(-p)$, including two copies of $C$ for the trivial character. The two points over $p$ are identified. Article V, §§4–6.

Let $D_c\subset A$ be the inverse image of $X_c=\{c+p:p\in C\}$. Write $\mathcal H_c$ for the space of stable markings over $c$ in the universal curve of $\mathcal H$, and $p_A:\mathcal H_c\to A$ for its coarse map. Then
$$
 \mathcal H_c=\sqrt[2]{(A,D_c)}.
$$
Indeed, normalize the Poincaré bundle $L_0$ on $C\times A$ at $c$. Its square and the universal branch line differ by a line from $A$, since their ratio is trivial on each fiber of the proper product. Restriction at $c$ identifies the branch line there with $\mathcal O_A(D_c)$. Thus the universal root has the form $L_0\otimes T$ on the gerbe, with $T^2=\mathcal O_A(D_c)$. In the double-cover algebra a mark over $c$ gives a value $t\in T$ with $t^2=s_{D_c}$. At a node the corresponding stable mark lies on an inserted rational curve with two attaching flags. Both descriptions agree in families by the universal-curve property of stabilization. In particular, the forgetful map $\pi:\mathcal H_c\to\mathcal H$ is representable, finite flat of degree two, even though the generic coarse map to $A$ has degree one. [Universal curve of stable maps, Appendix A](https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/files/re4.pdf#page=40) · [Abramovich–Fantechi, §1.2.3](https://www.math.brown.edu/dabramov/PAPERS/BARBARA/deg-revisited.pdf#page=12).

The reduced ramification divisor $R$ satisfies $2R=p_A^*D_c$. Its coarse curve $D_c$ is a connected étale cover of $C$ of degree $n$, since moving the second point around loops realizes all of $H_1(C;\mathbb Z/2)$. Write $\Delta$ for the pullback to $\mathcal H_c$ of the diagonal of $M$. Then
$$
 \int_{\mathcal H_c}R^2=\frac n4,\qquad
 \int_{\mathcal H_c}R\,\Delta=n,\qquad
 c_1(\mathbb L_{\rm mark})=[R].
$$
On $C^2$ the pullback of $X_c$ consists of the two coordinate curves, each of square zero, meeting once. Thus $X_c^2=1$. The discriminant restricted to $X_c$ has a double zero at $2c$, giving $X_c\cdot\Delta_M=2$. Pushing down along the coarse map and then the étale cover gives the two integrals. For the cotangent class, the derivative at the mark is a section of $\mathbb L_{\rm mark}\otimes T_c C$. On smooth marked branches it vanishes simply along $R$, as follows in a square-root coordinate. The exceptional marks on contracted components form a finite set on a smooth stack surface, so the divisor determines the line there as well.

In the chart at a root over $2c$, take $w(c)=0$ and write the cover locally as $y^2=(w-\nu)^2-\tau$. The marked chart has coordinates $(\nu,t)$, with $\tau=\nu^2-t^2$, modulo $t\mapsto-t$. The ramification divisor is $t=0$. The two branches of the nodal divisor are $t=\pm\nu$. Their intersection with $R$ has length two at the origin and stack weight $1/2$. Each of the $n$ possible roots therefore contributes one to the second integral. The reduced set of trivalent rational components has stack degree $n/2$. This includes the split normalization, whose two ends the involution interchanges.

## The normal operator on the ramification divisor

For the rest of the note assume that the upper normal operator $D_u=u^*D_0$ is injective on every finite double in $\mathcal H$, and that the operators of all étale normalizations are invertible. This is an additional hypothesis at the chosen lower wall. The real index at genus $2g$ is $-4$. Stabilization gives an oriented rank-four cokernel bundle $F$ over $\mathcal H$ and its pullback over $\mathcal H_c$. On an inserted rational bridge the homogeneous operator is the ordinary Dolbeault operator with constant kernel. Matching these constants at its two ends reproduces the original nodal operator. If $F_2(D_0)=\int_{\mathcal H}e(F)$, finite-flat pushforward gives
$$
                  \int_{\mathcal H_c}\pi^*e(F)=2F_2(D_0).
$$

There is a real bundle map $P:S|_R\to\pi^*F|_R$, where $S=T_{\rm mark}\otimes\ell$. Represent an element by a principal part $v/y$ in a coordinate centered at the mark and a holomorphic frame for the complex-linear part of $D_0$. Take the class of $D_u(\chi v/y)$ computed off the pole, with $\chi=1$ near the mark and supported away from the other special points. On $R$ the upper map $u$ has $u'(y)=O(y)$ or is constant on the marked component. Its pulled-back antilinear coefficient is divisible by $\bar y$, so the displayed form extends smoothly. A change of holomorphic frame, coordinate, or cutoff alters the representative by the image of a smooth section with the matching conditions at nodes. This gives the indicated real-linear class. Near the section of the stabilized marked family choose the disk away from the nodes. Divisibility by $\bar y$ holds in families on $R$ by Taylor expansion of the holomorphic upper map. The extended forms are continuous in the stabilized cokernel charts, with the same comparison away from the nodes as for $F$.

At a trivalent rational component, the matching description identifies the cokernel with $N_c^2/\operatorname{diag} N_c$, because the operators on the positive normalization are invertible. Put the two attaching flags at $0,\infty$ and the marking at $1$. The meromorphic section $v/(\zeta-1)$ on the bridge represents the principal part. The difference of its two endpoint values is $-v$. Consequently $P$ embeds the smoothing plane there, and the quotient is a copy of $N_c/\ell$ after choosing an order of the ends. Reversing the order reverses the difference. This calculation gives the map at the exceptional points. Its rank on the remainder of $R$ and its comparison with smoothing in the full stable-map obstruction problem require separate arguments.

## A test for a proposed smoothing equation

There is an elementary formula for the following bundle model. Suppose $j:S\to \pi^*F\oplus W$ is a real bundle injection on $\mathcal H_c$, where $W$ is a fixed complex line. In complex line coordinates assume its $W$ component is $q\mapsto a\,\overline{du(q)}$ for a nonvanishing complex scalar $a$, using the fixed lower tangent line to identify the target. Assume its first component over $R$ is $P$. Orient $S$ by its complex structure, the complement by the quotient orientation, and put $J=\pi^*F|_R/P(S|_R)$. Then
$$
 \int_{\mathcal H_c} e\big((\pi^*F\oplus W)/j(S)\big)
       =-2F_2(D_0)-\int_R e(J).
$$
To see this, intersect $j(q)$ with $(\sigma,0)$ for a generic transverse rational multisection $\sigma$ of $\pi^*F$. Arrange its projection to $J$ to be transverse on $R$ and $\sigma$ to have no full zero there. Off $R$ the zeros have $q=0$ and the conjugate-linear map from the $q$-plane to $W$ contributes a minus sign. Over $R$ the zeros are the zeros of the projected section, with $q\ne0$ uniquely determined in the image plane. The derivative normal to $R$ in the $W$ row is conjugate-linear, again of negative sign. The other normal directions carry the orientations defining $J$. Multisections account for the order-two stabilizer along $R$.

Even knowing the two surface intersections and the map at every bridge cannot force the second term in this model. On a disk of the coarse ramification curve the source and target of $P$ have scalar sign action by the stabilizer. Indeed the invariant cokernel is zero since the invariant operator on smooth even sections descends to the invertible base operator at the level of its index and kernel: the invariant index is zero by deformation to a complex-linear base operator and the invariant family has zero kernel by hypothesis. The complex-linear reference has invariant cohomology equal to the cohomology on $C$ by finite pushforward. An equivariant real injection on this disk is consequently an ordinary two-frame after choosing frames and trivializing the gerbe. Altering the injection relative to the boundary by a generator of $\pi_2(V_2(\mathbb R^4))$ changes the complementary plane's Euler number on the doubled disk by two. This follows from the fibration $SO(2)\to SO(4)\to V_2(\mathbb R^4)$, since the kernel of $\pi_1(SO(2))\to\pi_1(SO(4))$ is $2\mathbb Z$. Extend the change in the first component of $j$ from this disk to a tubular chart using bundle trivializations and a cutoff in the normal direction. Off $R$ the unchanged $W$ component is invertible, so the extended bundle map is still injective. The disk has stack weight $1/2$, so this changes the model integral by one. This is a comparison of topological models. Realization by normal operators is a separate question.

For the actual quartic wall, the next comparison is between the stabilized smoothing equation and $P$ as the attaching point crosses $R$. It must include the three nodes of the stabilized limit when both upper branch values collide at $c$. There one can put the flags at $0,\infty,1$ and write the upper projection in a plumbing chart as $w=c+A(\zeta-1)+B(\zeta^{-1}-1)$, with ramification at the mark when $A=B$. This supplies a local test of both the analytic estimates and the orientation. A signed Euler contribution for this compact family requires that comparison, control of the rank of its polar map, and evaluation of the resulting obstruction class.
