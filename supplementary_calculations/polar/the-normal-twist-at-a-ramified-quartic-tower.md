# E1. The normal twist at a ramified quartic tower

Let $D_t$ be a smooth fixed-symbol path on the normal bundle $N$ of degree $2g-2$ over a genus-$g$ curve $C$, $g\ge2$. Suppose $D_0$ is invertible on the base and $f:Y\to C$ is a smooth connected double cover with $b>0$ distinct simple branch values, a one-dimensional odd pullback kernel, and an embedded graph $\gamma=(f,\xi)$ of a generator. Assume the derivative $W_f$ in the Hurwitz and time directions is an isomorphism, and write $\eta_f$ for its absolutely oriented sign. Use the homogeneous graph almost complex structure of $D_0$ on the total space of $N$, with holomorphic projection, standard vertical complex structure, and holomorphic sections on covers precisely the solutions of the pulled-back linear equation. Denote the normal operator of this particular embedded $\gamma$ by $A$.

Take a connected étale double $p:X\to Y$ of character $\lambda\ne0$ and put $h=fp$. Write $A^\lambda$ for the twist of $A$ by the flat real sign line on $Y$. Its index is zero since the ambient first Chern number on $\gamma$ is zero. The genus of $X$ is $2g(Y)-1$. The graph $(h,p^*\xi)$ has image $\gamma$ with multiplicity two.

**Proposition.** At the smooth map $h$, the pullback $h^*D_0$ has just the inherited kernel line and its full Hurwitz-and-time derivative is an isomorphism if and only if $A^\lambda$ is invertible. In that case the point $h$ is isolated in the fixed-genus projectivized kernel incidence and its local contribution to the change of the ordinary Euler integral is
$$
                 \frac{2\eta_f\,\epsilon(A^\lambda)}
                            {|\operatorname{Aut}_C(h)|}.                 \tag{1}
$$
This is an individual smooth-point contribution at the given genus. The source moduli stack includes other ramified or nodal covers, and a tower can have higher-genus stable maps attached to its image.

**Proof.** Work on the chart with individually identified ramification disks and the complex orientation. For maps to the fixed curve with variable domain the deformation complex $[TX\xrightarrow{dh}h^*TC]$ is quasi-isomorphic, with the usual tangent shift, to its torsion cokernel. Each simple critical point contributes one independent complex velocity in the tangent to $C$ at its image. There is no obstruction. Two critical points have the same branch value here but are distinct on $X$ and give different skyscraper summands. The upper deck involution swaps the two summands over each ramification point of $f$. Its invariant tangent space is $T_f\mathcal H_f$ and its anti-invariant space $T^-$ is the complex $b$-plane of opposite branch velocities. Intrinsically its summands are $T_{f(r)}C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\lambda_r$.

Consider the fixed-time linearized graph equation with variable domain at $G=\gamma p$. Projection to $C$ gives the preceding horizontal complex. Its vertical subcomplex has operator $h^*D_0$, since the graph equation is linear in height. Alternatively $G$ is an immersion and its tangent and domain variations cancel, leaving the normal operator $p^*A$. To see the cancellation, project vector-field variations along $G$ to the normal quotient and use that $dG:TX\to TG(TX)$ is an isomorphism of complex lines. The remaining tangent variations have operator $\bar\partial_{TX}$, with infinitesimal changes of complex structure as its full equation target modulo infinitesimal reparametrizations. Thus this tangent/domain complex is acyclic. The induced normal operator is the pullback of the one along $\gamma$ because $p$ is locally biholomorphic. The description is equivariant for the upper involution. [Bai–Swaminathan, §4.2.1 and Lemma 5.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4#S4.SS2.SSS1) · [Normal operator and infinitesimal deformations, Doan–Walpuski](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2022.104).

Taking the anti-invariant part of the first description and using the second gives the exact sequence, with $F_\lambda=(f^*D_0)^\lambda$,
$$
  0\longrightarrow\ker F_\lambda\longrightarrow\ker A^\lambda
  \longrightarrow T^-\xrightarrow{\delta_\xi}\operatorname{coker} F_\lambda
  \longrightarrow\operatorname{coker} A^\lambda\longrightarrow0.
$$
The connecting map differentiates the equation $a^*D_0u=0$ in the horizontal direction at $u=p^*\xi$ and projects to the vertical cokernel. A different lift or trivialization changes the derivative by an image vector. This is exactly the anti-invariant block of the intrinsic wall derivative evaluated on the inherited generator, up to the conventional simultaneous sign of a connecting map. Its source has even real dimension, so this latter sign has no effect on its degree. If $A^\lambda$ is invertible then $F_\lambda$ is injective and $\delta_\xi$ is an isomorphism. Conversely those two properties force both the kernel and cokernel of $A^\lambda$ to vanish. The complementary invariant block is the entire lower wall derivative, including time. This proves the equivalence.

For orientations the graph complex has the absolute Cauchy–Riemann determinant. In the anti-invariant splitting its determinant is the product of the complex orientation of $T^-$ with the absolute determinant of $F_\lambda$. Canceling the tangent and domain directions gives the absolute determinant of $A^\lambda$. These determinant identifications preserve the orientations: deform the triangular operators to their complex-linear parts, preserving their tangent, vertical and quotient symbols and the upper involution. At the complex endpoint the determinant sequence has the complex orientations. For an invertible $A^\lambda$, the sign of $\delta_\xi$ is consequently $\epsilon(A^\lambda)$. The real dimension of the anti block is $2b$, so changing $\xi$ to $-\xi$, reversing the convention for its connecting map, or moving time past this block does not change its sign. The signed degree of the full derivative is $\eta_f\epsilon(A^\lambda)$.

On a chart of smooth maps the full cokernel at the crossing is real $(4b+1)$-dimensional, the kernel one-dimensional, and the Hurwitz-and-time chart has real dimension $4b+1$. The normalized obstruction column therefore has an isolated transverse zero. On a small linking sphere the cokernel is the quotient by the line spanned by the column, with Euler integral twice its signed local degree since $\chi(S^{4b})=2$. At these smooth fixed-target covers the unperturbed tangential virtual class is the ordinary fundamental class of the Hurwitz chart. Division by its stabilizer gives (1). This use of the linking sphere does not require the map $G$ itself to be simple. [Bai–Swaminathan, Lemma 6.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.01206v4).

There are exactly two maps from $X$ to the specified $Y$ over $C$, differing by its lower involution $\tau$: this follows on function fields since $Y/C$ is quadratic Galois. Hence $|\operatorname{Aut}_C(h)|=2|\operatorname{Stab}_{\langle\tau\rangle}(\lambda)|$. When $\lambda$ is fixed, a lift of $\tau$ squares to the identity: on the two-point fiber over a ramification point of $f$ its square fixes both points, whereas the upper deck involution is free. The three nonidentity elements therefore give a Klein four group. Distinct $\lambda$ exchanged by $\tau$ give one tower over $C$ and equal twist signs; fiber negation carries $\gamma$ to itself over $\tau$ and preserves its absolute determinant. If all the indicated twists are regular, the sum of (1) over the inequivalent étale upper doubles of this fixed $f$ is
$$
                  \frac{\eta_f}{2}
                  \sum_{0\ne\lambda\in H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)}
                          \epsilon(A^\lambda).
$$

## Regular twists under perturbations preserving the lower wall

**Theorem.** Suppose at one simple ramification point the projected antilinear jet of the lower equation is nonzero, in the sense of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. By arbitrarily small smooth fixed-symbol perturbations of the operator on $C$ which preserve $f$ and its chosen kernel section $\xi$, one can make all $A^\lambda$ for $0\ne\lambda\in H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)$ invertible simultaneously. This property is open and dense in a small neighborhood in the perturbation space described below. The lower crossing remains transverse. Thus the sum from the smooth towers is an odd integer times $1/2$ at such a wall; in particular it cannot vanish.

**Allowed variations.** At an unramified value use the normal identification of a graph sheet with the vertical bundle: a graph varying with its projection held fixed satisfies the original equation on this disk. In these coordinates $A$ on either sheet is $D_0$. The normal section $s$ obtained by differentiating real fiber dilations is represented by the sheet height $\xi$ and is nonzero here. The lower involution $\tau$ is induced on the graph by fiber negation; its derivative identifies the normal equations on the two sheets and preserves the global radial section.

Write locally $D_0=\bar\partial_{N}+B$, with $B$ conjugate-linear, in a frame holomorphic for the complex-linear part. Near the chosen ramification point $w=z^2$ and $\xi(z)=zv+O(|z|^2)$ with $v\ne0$. The assumed $[B(0)v]\ne0$ modulo $\mathbb Cv$ implies $B(w)\xi(z)\notin\mathbb C\xi(z)$ in a punctured neighborhood. Fix a relatively compact disk $V$ of regular target values there and choose one of its two sheets. Use real zeroth-order tensors supported in $V$ which annihilate the real vector $\xi(w)$ pointwise. They annihilate $-\xi$ on the other sheet as well. On a smaller disk these variations can realize any smooth tensor on $N/\mathbb R\xi$; multiplication by a bump extends it by zero. They preserve the graph exactly and, on these sheets, produce those same zeroth-order variations of the normal operator. Take a small neighborhood of zero in this perturbation space so that $[B\xi]\ne0$ still holds on a fixed smaller disk and the simple transverse lower crossing persists. A bump in time, equal to one at the crossing, incorporates the perturbation in the original path.

**Noninvariant characters.** Let $\mu\ne0$ with $\tau^*\mu\ne\mu$, and suppose $A^\mu$ has a nonzero solution $u$. On the selected sheet use flat real sign frames for $\mu$ and $\tau^*\mu$. Transport the other-sheet value of $u$ to this sheet by the differential of fiber negation and write it as $u'$. Thus $u'$ globally solves the twist by $\tau^*\mu$ on $Y$, and on the disk $s,u,u'$ solve the same equation. On the common connected étale cover of $Y$ trivializing the two characters they give solutions with three distinct deck characters $1,\mu,\tau^*\mu$. Each is nonzero and they are linearly independent as global real sections.

If $u=a s$ with complex $a$ on a disk, the equation modulo $\mathbb Cs$ gives $(\bar a-a)[Bs]=0$. Thus $a$ is real where $[Bs]\ne0$, and the ordinary Leibniz rule gives $\bar\partial a=0$. It is constant. Unique continuation would make $u$ a constant multiple of $s$ on the common cover, contrary to their distinct characters. Consequently $s,u$ are complex independent on a subdisk. If $u'$ lay in their real span throughout that subdisk, we could write $u'=a s+d u$ with smooth real coefficients. Applying the equation and using complex independence yields $\bar\partial a=\bar\partial d=0$. Constant coefficients again contradict the deck characters after continuation. Hence $s,u,u'$ are real independent on an open disk.

Consider the universal equation on nonzero sections for the allowed operator variations. An adjoint annihilator of its derivative solves the homogeneous adjoint equation by variation of the section. On the last disk, tensors killing $s$ can prescribe arbitrary independent values on $u$ and $u'$. Testing with such tensors forces the adjoint to vanish on both sheets, then everywhere by unique continuation on the connected curve. This proves surjectivity. Fiberwise the source operator is Fredholm of index zero. Dividing by the real scaling of a nonzero solution gives index $-1$ for projection of the universal projective incidence to the perturbation space. Sard–Smale gives generic absence of such solutions.

**Invariant characters.** If $\tau^*\mu=\mu$, lift $\tau$ to the flat sign line. It squares to one, since the square is a parallel automorphism and acts as one on a real sign fiber over a fixed point. The twisted normal operator splits into two real eigensystems. Each has index zero. Indeed at a fixed point of $\tau$ the ambient action has a positive horizontal line and two negative vertical lines; quotient by the vertical tangent of the graph leaves one of each. Twisting by the sign fiber preserves the unordered pair. Deform the normal operator to an invariant Dolbeault operator and take invariant and anti-invariant direct images separately on $C$. For the invariant direct image the pullback evaluation to the twisted normal bundle is an isomorphism away from ramification and has length-one cokernel at each ramification point: in equivariant holomorphic frames with signs $+,-$ its generating sections are $e_+$ and $z e_-$. The anti-invariant case interchanges the roles of the two frames. Each rank-two direct image therefore has degree $(2g(Y)-2-b)/2=2g-2$ and index zero by Riemann–Roch.

For a nonzero solution in one eigensystem, the same complex-collinearity argument for $s,u$ uses the nontrivial character $\mu$ on $Y$. There is a disk where variations killing $s$ have arbitrary value on $u$. On the other sheet the kernel and the adjoint in the same eigensystem transform by the same sign and the same normal symmetry. With equivariant metrics the two integrals in the annihilator test are equal. Thus the adjoint vanishes by the same argument. Projectivized index $-1$ separately in the two eigensystems gives generic absence of both kernels.

These transversality tests can be made in a sufficiently high finite differentiability completion, using $W^{1,p}$ sections for $p>2$ and localized bump variations. Surjectivity follows from the Fredholm range and the adjoint test. Open invertibility permits smooth approximation of the resulting perturbations in the real subbundle of tensors killing $s$. Starting at any smooth admissible perturbation with any prescribed finite collection of smooth seminorm bounds gives density in the smooth topology. There are only $2^{2g(Y)}-1$ nontrivial characters, so simultaneous invertibility is still open and dense. The sum in (1) over those characters uses this odd number of signs with the common factor $\eta_f/2$. It is a local sum in the proper stable-map space at genus $2g(Y)-1$.

For generic paths the simultaneous regularity also follows on the universal lower-wall incidence. Take the regular marked lower-cover chart with a normalized generator of its kernel line. Its projection to the fixed-symbol operator space is Fredholm of index $-1$ (the $b$ complex branch parameters offset the real operator index $-2b$, and projectivization removes one real dimension). Work locally so the upper character, and in the invariant case its eigenspace, are identified throughout the family. The smooth-cover equations give smooth Fredholm families on these charts, using coefficients of sufficiently high differentiability for the normal linearization along the graph. For the universal equation of a second marked kernel line, the fixed-graph variations just tested are already tangent to the lower incidence. They supply the same surjectivity there. Its projection to the lower incidence therefore has index $-1$, and its composite to operator space has index $-2$. Relative parametric transversality for a path avoids this image. Countably many smooth Hurwitz charts, branch profiles and choices of finite character suffice; each simple lower incidence has the projected jet for a generic path by Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. This gives the tower formula at every ramified ordinary double crossing on such a path. It does not require separate transversality of primitive quartic kernel graphs.

The other quartic loci, calculated in Groups E and F, include a double graph joined to degree-two curves at zero height and pairs of nonzero double graphs joined at ramification. There the odd section vanishes at the joins and independent real scalings can match. The direct-node argument for a single line and the smooth-tower comparison above do not yet evaluate those loci.
