# E6. The quartic wall contribution of a marked double cover

We compute the supported normal Euler change at a ramified double graph joined to a two-branch double at height zero. The marked double can degenerate to an unramified normalization with a conjugate pair identified, including the limit where the joining mark lies on an inserted rational component. We use the fixed-target convention and absolute Fredholm orientations of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B.

Let $C$ be a smooth closed curve of genus $g\ge2$, and $D_t$ a smooth fixed-symbol path of real Cauchy–Riemann operators on a complex bundle of rank two and degree $2g-2$, with $D_0$ invertible. Let $f:Y\to C$ be the simple transverse double-cover crossing of [Calculation E2](first-quartic-contribution-at-a-ramified-double-wall.md), with embedded odd kernel graph, $b=2l>0$ branch values and $h=2g-1+l$. Fix a ramification point $r$, its value $c$, and a real generator $k$ of the kernel. Write $w=c+z^2$ and $v=\partial_zk(r)$ in a local frame for the complex-linear part of $D_0$. Assume $[B(c)v]\ne0$ in $W=N_c/\mathbb Cv$. Write $\eta_f$ for the absolute sign of the lower Hurwitz-and-time derivative $W_f$. The separating domain has arithmetic genus $k_*=h+2g=4g-1+l$.

Use the notation of [Calculation E4](the-marked-double-cover-surface-at-a-quartic-join.md). The compact marked surface $Z=\mathcal H_c$ parametrizes an upper double $u$ of arithmetic genus $2g$ with a mark $s$ over $c$. Its reduced ramification divisor is $R$. Assume the upper operators are injective on the entire unmarked finite surface, the normal operators of the étale normalizations are invertible, and the principal-part map $P:S|_R\to F|_R$ is injective. Here $F$ is the pullback of the oriented real rank-four upper cokernel bundle and $S=T_{\rm mark}\otimes\mathbb Cv$. Identify $T_rY$ with $\mathbb Cv$ by $dk_r$. Set $J_r=F|_R/P(S|_R)$ with its quotient orientation. These are conditions on the actual operator at the lower crossing. In particular, positivity of a virtual index or injectivity on ordinary upper sections alone would not imply the last condition.

**Theorem.** The joined family $Z$ is isolated in the projective kernel incidence in genus $k_*$. Its contribution in increasing time to the absolute normal Euler change is
$$
          -\eta_f\left(2F_2(D_0)+\int_R e(J_r)\right),
       \qquad F_2(D_0)=\int_{\mathcal H}e(\operatorname{coker}u^*D_0).       \tag{1}
$$
The formula is per lower branch point. For $l\ge3$, under the stated conditions at every such point, [Calculation E3](a-ramified-double-joined-to-an-unramified-cover.md) gives the full sum in the incidence of Calculation E2 in this genus as
$$
 \eta_f\left\{S_C\left[\frac{b(k_*-2)}{12}+2\binom b2\right]
        +\binom b2-2bF_2(D_0)-\sum_r\int_{R_r}e(J_r)\right\}.                 \tag{2}
$$
Here $S_C$ is the sum of the determinant signs on the connected unramified doubles. A separate simple quartic image lies outside this specified incidence. For $l=2$ the per-marked-family calculation has the same local meaning, and unramified towers on $Y$ may also occur. For $l=1$ the crossing $f$ itself belongs to the upper two-branch surface, so the upper injectivity hypothesis fails. For a lower graph with more than two branch values, [Calculation E5](polar-kernels-on-two-branched-double-covers.md) proves simultaneous generic upper injectivity and polar rank with the prescribed lower graph. In its conventions (1) becomes
$$
       -\eta_f\epsilon(D_0)
       \left(\frac{2^{2g}g(g-1)}8+2W_4+W_2(c,\mathbb Cv)\right).
$$
Here $W_4$ counts the ordinary odd upper crossings from a generic complex-linear reference, and $W_2$ counts the odd polar crossings at this flag on the reference path. In particular, evaluating the real plane term at a particular crossing still requires information about its polar family.

## The fixed-target charts

At a joined map with the distinguished node retained, the lower and upper covers vary independently subject to equality of their marked values. A marking on the lower cover has coordinate $p$ measured from its moving ramification point. Its value is $c'=c_0+p^2$, where $c_0$ denotes the moving lower branch value. The marked upper surface is taken over $c'$. At a smooth upper mark which is ramified one may use, in a target disk on the upper component,
$$
              w_u=c'+y^2+2d y,\qquad s=\{y=0\}.
$$
The moving critical point is $-d$ and $d=0$ is the ramification divisor. The other upper branch position is independent. Replace the two marked disks by $zy=q$ and
$$
              w=w_u(y)+z^2+2p z.                                             \tag{3}
$$
The formula is holomorphic on the total plumbing. At $q=0$ its restrictions are the prescribed normalized maps. At an unramified upper marking one can instead take $y'=w_u-c'$ and use $w=c'+z^2+2pz+y'$. The cubic smoothing coordinate is then $q'=zy'$, whose derivative in the intrinsic node line is $1\otimes du_s$.

Here is a chart where both upper branch values collide at $c$. Put the two upper flags at $0,\infty$ of a projective line with coordinate $\zeta$ and its lower joining mark at $1$. Take
$$
 w_u=c'+A(\zeta-1)+B(\zeta^{-1}-1),\qquad
 z(\zeta-1)=q,\qquad
 w=w_u+G,\quad G=z^2+2pz.                                                    \tag{4}
$$
The other source nodes have equations $(1/\zeta)a=A$ and $\zeta b'=B$. In these charts $A\zeta=a$, $B/\zeta=b'$, and $z=q/(\zeta-1)$ is regular near either end of the bridge, expressed using $1/\zeta$ at infinity. Thus (4) extends across each node of the total family. The critical derivative at the joining mark is $A-B$. Eliminating $\zeta$ gives
$$
          e=A\zeta-B/\zeta,\qquad
          e^2=(w_u-c'+A+B)^2-4AB.
$$
These are precisely the marked coordinates of Calculation E4, after an invertible linear coordinate change in $(A,B)$. The interchange of the ends replaces $\zeta$ by its reciprocal and interchanges $A,B$.

For the passage from disk formulas to maps on closed sources, use outer collars of fixed target size. At each point of a fixed lower source annulus take the root of $w-c_0$ close to the old coordinate $z+p$ there. More generally, on an upper source collar lift the perturbed target value to the unique point near the old source point under the unramified collar covering. Equivalently lift the short homotopy between the old and new target values starting at that point, using a slightly larger collar. The local inverse choices agree on overlaps by uniqueness and give a holomorphic identification. This construction works on a connected quadratic annulus with monodromy in (3). At the outer ends in (4) it preserves the two distinct source collars also when the upper normalization is connected. Shrinking the annuli once, these identifications are embeddings for all small parameters by uniform closeness there to the central unramified collars. Gluing the complements with their original maps constructs the required fixed-target family. In (3) the perturbation on a fixed upper outer collar and its first derivatives have size $O(|q|^2+|pq|)$. In (4) the same bound holds in the outer target coordinate: $G=q^2/(\zeta-1)^2+2pq/(\zeta-1)$ is bounded by this quantity on a larger unramified collar, so Cauchy's estimate applies on the smaller one. At $A=0$ or $B=0$ the regular formulas in $(1/\zeta,a)$ or $(\zeta,b')$ give the same bound on the corresponding outer branch. Smooth cutoffs on smaller collars give identifications for comparing sections and operators. The differential-part error there pairs to $O(|q|^2+|pq|)$ against bounded smooth tests, since the stabilized sections have uniform interior first-derivative bounds on those collars by elliptic estimates.

For completeness, the charts exhaust the fixed-target deformations near this locus. The normalized finite maps have unobstructed deformation complexes: on a smooth positive component the differential is an injection of tangent sheaves and the quotient is supported at the ramification points. On the rational contracted component $H^1(\mathcal O)=0$, and its three special points have no moduli. For the simultaneous fixed-target matching equations at the tripod, varying the two unramified outer flags and the common constant on the rational component surjects onto the three target fibers; the lower branch motion is free. The normalization deformation sequence therefore has zero obstruction and has independent smoothings of the three nodes. In (4) they are $q,A,B$, and $p$ is the additional lower flag tangent. At a smooth ramification on both sides the two normalized branch positions vary their values independently, so the single target matching row is surjective there as well. Coordinates along the nodal stratum are $p,d$, the other upper branch parameter, and the lower Hurwitz coordinates. At an unramified mark the matching is solved by the upper flag, and a self-node away from this flag has its own ordinary finite-double deformation. This also follows from $e^2=(w-a_0)(w-a_1)$ in its disjoint disk, allowing an unordered pair of branch values. The displayed families realize all those tangents, including the independent domain smoothing parameters. Gluing over the unramified complements introduces no further deformation, as the relative cotangent complex there vanishes. Thus the charts are smooth of complex dimension $b+4$, equal to $2k_*-2-4(2g-2)$, and their virtual classes are ordinary fundamental classes. Time is an extra real coordinate. The distinguished divisor $q=0$ is intrinsic near the compact family: its edge separates the genus-$h$ lower degree two component from the genus-$2g$ upper double. At the tripod the other edges lie on a cycle or cut off an individual degree-one component.

## The normalized obstruction

Order the two lower sheets over a lower regular disk away from $c$. This choice extends on small charts, including over an upper cover which branches over the same test disk, since the lower preimages are disjoint smooth disks in the source. The choices give a twofold cover accounting for the lower involution. Normalize the section by a real test there with value one on $k$. After this framing the central kernel is constant over $Z$. By normalization at the distinguished node its cokernel is an extension of $P_f\oplus F$ by $N_c$, where $P_f=\operatorname{coker}f^*D_0$. The lower kernel vanishes at $r$ and the upper kernel is zero. On the contracted bridge the constants cancel the diagonal evaluation, reproducing the cokernel of the unmarked nodal double.

Choose lower odd thick forms representing $P_f$. For the matching fiber use $j_M(a)=D_f(\chi H(w)a)$ on the lower source, where $H$ is a local real frame of $D_t$-homogeneous sections, $H(c)=1$ at the center, and the cutoff $\chi$ is one near the moving mark and has support disjoint from the normalization test. Such a frame is obtained on a small disk by solving the equation for perturbations of a constant real basis and normalizing its value; it can be chosen smoothly for small $t$. The matching forcing is supported on a lower annulus away from the node. Choose upper forms on thick upper disks whose classes give the identity on $F$. Locally such lifts exist by adjoint unique continuation and testing arbitrary endpoint values in the normalization sequence. They can also be seen from the fixed-target dual test below. Average lifts by the finite isotropy and a partition of unity on the compact base; the sum still induces the identity on the cokernel. These are bundle-valued supplements, so no global frame of $F$ is required. Extend the supplements to nearby sources by the charts on their thick support, using a finite cover and partition of unity. The lower forms stay on the lower side of each truncation below and the upper forms stay on the upper side. In particular extracting the upper obstruction does not mix an error proportional to the matching coefficient into that row.

These supplements are jointly minimal on $Z$. The usual continuous stabilization of real pullback operators on nodal curves consequently gives a normalized stabilized section $k'$ and an obstruction vector with rows in $P_f,N_c,F$. It is uniformly bounded in the section sup norm and converges on every compact subset of the smooth part of a limiting source. For the sup bound use the continuous inclusion of Siebert’s weighted first Sobolev bundle into the sup-norm bundle. The extra real zeroth-order term is continuous in the corresponding operator comparison: on a half plumbing collar $uv=\tau$, a target map holomorphic in the total coordinates has bounded $u$-derivative for $|u|\ge\sqrt{|\tau|}$ and bounded $v$-derivative on the other half. The weighted $L^a$ mass on each shrinking half-disk tends uniformly to zero (its area weight is of order $1/|u|$ or $1/|v|$); elsewhere the coefficients converge smoothly. Multiplication by a sup-bounded section therefore contributes a norm-continuous lower-order perturbation. The thick supplements are continuous in the same bundles. Ordinary interior elliptic estimates give convergence of the normalized solution on the thick disks. [Bai–Swaminathan, Appendix B, (B.6)–(B.9)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4#A2) · [Siebert, Proposition 4.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/dg-ga/9608005v2#page=52).

For $q=0$ first solve the normalized lower stabilized equation with just the lower odd supplement and call its section $k_\lambda$, where $\lambda$ denotes the lower branch positions and time. It is odd and vanishes at the moving ramification point. Subtract $\chi H(w)H(c')^{-1}k_\lambda(p)$ on the lower component and put zero on the entire upper component. This solves the joined stabilized problem and preserves its normalization. In these nodal coordinates the upper row is identically zero, the lower row has derivative $W_f$, and the matching row is $\pm H(c')^{-1}k_\lambda(p)$, with derivative $\pm v p$. The supports and these conclusions persist if locally defined extensions are replaced by common sums. Equivalently one first prescribes these nodal lifts on $q=0$ and extends them by partitions of unity only in the thick parts. For moving $t$ the upper operator remains injective uniformly by compactness; its cokernel is identified with $F$ by continuation.

## The upper cokernel through the collision

We first describe tests for the upper row. The invariant part of the upper cokernel is zero: the invariant kernel vanishes by hypothesis and the invariant index is that of $D_t$ by complex-linear finite pushforward. On a target disk write a finite double as $e^2=g(w)$. In the odd polynomial frame $e$, the equation on coefficients over the fixed target has Dolbeault symbol. Its antilinear multiplier is $(\bar e/e)B_t(w)$, together with a smooth complex-linear coefficient when the local frame is kept fixed in time. Off the zero set of $g$ the ratio is independent of the choice of root, has modulus one, and converges pointwise when branch values coalesce. Hence it varies continuously in every finite $L^a$. Use the local analytic frames of the root line, and its smooth identifications on a fixed target, to obtain continuous operators $W^{1,a}\to L^a$ for $a>2$, also for their formal real adjoints. Multiplication by the varying coefficient is norm continuous by the $W^{1,a}$ sup bound. The odd fixed-target index is $-4$, by the degree of $N\otimes L^{-1}$. A coefficient kernel evaluates to a bounded homogeneous solution on the upper source: removability on the normalization applies, both nodal branch values coincide, and constants extend across a contracted bridge. Thus the fixed-target operator is injective. Elliptic regularity, using the bounded coefficients, places adjoint kernels in $W^{1,a}$ for every finite $a$; fixed rank and the continuous stabilized inverse give locally continuous frames in that norm. They have a uniform Hölder bound.

For a fixed-target adjoint coefficient $h(w)\,dw$, the corresponding real Green test on the source is
$$
                    \omega=h(w)\,\frac{dw}{2e}.
$$
Indeed summing its pairing with the two sheets of a forcing tests exactly the odd coefficient, with the factor two from the trace. Thick forcings realize these pairings. Conversely they detect the entire four-dimensional upper cokernel: a nonzero coefficient has a nonzero pairing on an unramified sheet disk, and the ordinary upper cokernel has this same dimension. In (4) the test is $h(w_u)d\zeta/(2\zeta)$. At a remaining node it has opposite leading residues on its two branches. At $A=B=0$ its restriction to the marked bridge is $h(c')d\zeta/(2\zeta)$, which has exactly the required opposite residues at its two ends. One can check this directly in $a=A\zeta$ and $b'=B/\zeta$. On a component detached at $A=0$ or $B=0$, the corresponding differential is $da/a$ or $-db'/b'$ with the same factor $h(w_u)/2$. These tests annihilate the image of the nodal operator by Green's formula: the leading residues cancel on sections having equal endpoint values, and the Hölder remainder tends to zero in the small-circle integrals. On the contracted component the test is holomorphic away from the ends. This verifies the identification also at the trivalent map, without differentiating an adjoint in a nodal parameter.

Denote by $E_U$ the actual upper obstruction row. In a chart at a point of $R$, with the source sewing coordinates in (3) or (4), we claim that, uniformly as all parameters tend to that point,
$$
              E_U=P_{u,s}(qv)+o(|q|).                                         \tag{5}
$$
We choose the common Green sign to agree with the off-pole boundary convention of Calculation E4; reversing both conventions has no effect on the oriented image plane. In (5) off $R$ the right side denotes the extension defined by evaluating the moving upper adjoints at the smooth marked section and pairing with the indicated principal part. This extension is continuous in either chart, including the bridge, by the preceding description. Equivalently one can use any continuous bundle extension which agrees with $P$ on $R$ when taking the limit to $R$. At $q=0$ both sides vanish.

For the estimate compare on the upper side with $u^*D_t$ at the *same* time and nodal-side parameters. Use a fixed small $\rho>0$ in the lower coordinate and truncate the upper domain at $|y|=|q|/\rho$, where $y=\zeta-1$ in (4). The pairing of the upper forcing with $\omega$ gives the coordinates of $E_U$ in a matrix tending to the identity, since the supports stay thick and their limiting classes are dual to these tests. There is no contribution from a lower forcing. For the reference upper operator Green's formula leaves just the boundary term. The coefficient of $\omega$ at $y=0$ will be denoted by $h_s$ in the chosen source frame. It has a uniform Hölder error $O(|y|^\alpha)$ for some $\alpha>0$, including when the mark lies on the rational component. With the real principal-part trace normalization the boundary integral is, up to its common Green sign,
$$
        \operatorname{Re}\left[h_s\,q\frac{1}{2\pi i}
                    \oint_{|z|=\rho} k'(z)\frac{dz}{z^2}\right]
                      +O_\rho(|q|^{1+\alpha}).                               \tag{6}
$$
This notation contracts the covector with the section. For fixed $\rho$ the contour coefficient tends to that of $k$, which is $v+O(\rho)$ by its expansion at $r$. The residue functional at $s$ so obtained is exactly the pairing with the class of the off-pole forcing of $v/y$ in Calculation E4. The local boundary near $s$ also gives this identification on the bridge.

It remains to bound the difference between the two operators in this pairing. On the upper side their target maps differ by
$$
                       G=q^2/y^2+2pq/y.
$$
On $|q|/\rho<|y|<c_1$ the adjoint coefficient is bounded. The derivative of the quadratic-pole term has integral $O(\rho |q|)$. The change of the smooth zeroth-order factor multiplied by $dw_u$ contributes
$$
        O\big(|q|^2(1+\log(1/|q|))+|pq|\big)
$$
since $|dw_u/dy|$ is uniformly bounded there. These are absolute estimates using the uniform bound on $k'$. The remaining derivative $-2pq/y^2$ requires angular cancellation. Freeze the smooth coefficient and the adjoint at the current target and marking values before this estimate. The cost is $O(|pq|)$: the adjoint error is $O(|y|^\alpha)$, and the oscillation of the target coefficient is bounded by a constant times $|y|+|z|^2+|p||z|$ with $z=q/y$. Each of these factors has bounded integral against $d|y|/|y|$ on this annulus. The constant matrix paired against this derivative sees only the Fourier modes of order $2$ or $-2$ of $k'$ or its complex conjugate. To bound them put $t'=\log |y|$ and let $a_j$ be a Fourier coefficient of $k'$ in the fixed target frame. There is no forcing in the annulus. Its equation gives
$$
       (\partial_{t'}-j)a_j=F_j,\qquad
       |F_j|\le C\left(|y|+|q|^2/|y|^2+|pq|/|y|\right).
$$
The right side has uniformly bounded integral in $t'$. For $j>0$ integrate the scalar equation backwards from the outer circle and for $j<0$ forwards from the inner one. The exponential kernel has integral at most $1/|j|$, so
$$
       \int |a_j|\,dt'\le
          (\|k'\|_\infty+\|F_j\|_{L^1(dt')})/|j| .
$$
The frozen term therefore contributes $O(|pq|)$, without a factor of the neck length. This estimate permits an arbitrary simultaneous approach in $p,q$.

Away from this annulus the comparison on a smooth upper chart costs $O(|q|^2+|pq|)$ by the collar construction. At the collision use (4). Outside a fixed disk about $\zeta=1$, $G$ is $O(|q|^2+|pq|)$ and $dG$ is bounded by this quantity times $|d\zeta|$ at zero and $|d\zeta|/|\zeta|^2$ at infinity. Pairing $dG$ with $|d\zeta/\zeta|$ thus has the same uniform integral bound. For the variation of the coefficient use $dw_u=(A-B/\zeta^2)d\zeta$. Between the two outer collars the relevant radial integral is bounded by a constant times
$$
                \int_{|B|/c_2}^{c_2/|A|}
                         (|A|+|B|/r'^2)\,dr'\le 2c_2.
$$
Use the separate limiting branches when either parameter vanishes. On a detached outer branch $G$ is constant, and the integral of the bounded variation times $|dw_u|\,|da/a|$ or $|dw_u|\,|db'/b'|$ is finite with the same type of bound. At nodes the Stokes terms for the reference operator cancel as above. The outer collar comparisons are again $O(|q|^2+|pq|)$. Dividing by $|q|$, taking the limit first with $\rho$ fixed and then with $\rho\downarrow0$, proves (5). Compactness gives the corresponding uniform lower bound for the upper row on a smaller tube of $R$ whenever $q\ne0$. More precisely, for any chosen extension $P'$ of $P$ to a tube, one can make $|E_U-P'(q)|/|q|$ arbitrarily small by shrinking this tube and the normal disks. This is the form of (5) used below.

## The matching equation off the ramification divisor

On a compact subset of $Z\setminus R$ the upper branch values stay a positive distance from $c$. A fixed smaller disk at $c$ contains a cubic joining factor and a separate ordinary sheet. Choose $y'=w_u-c'$ there. The interpolation calculation of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B gives a mass proportional to $\overline{q'}\log(1/|q'|) B(c)v$ in the matching fiber. Its singular primitive is the one on the lower branch of the cubic factor extended by zero on *both* upper sheets. Thus it projects to neither normalized component's cokernel. In particular the presence of the nonzero upper cokernel $F$ introduces no additional logarithmic column.

We detail uniformity when the upper double is already nodal elsewhere. For the normal calculation hold the underlying unmarked upper cover fixed on $q=0$. Its mark moves by the inverse unramified branch as the matched value $c_0+p^2$ varies. The finite algebra near the selected value is the cubic polynomial algebra of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B times a free sheet. Over the complement the lower and upper finite maps remain separate, with independent polynomial frames if their branch disks have the same target center. On an upper collision disk the quadratic frame $1,e$ has conjugation coefficient $\bar e/e$, uniformly bounded with convergence in every finite $L^a$ when the nodal double varies. Thus for $2<a<4$ the fixed-target operators are continuous $W^{1,a}\to L^a$. The only singular varying factor in the *normal* smoothing difference is the selected cubic. Varying lower branch centers off that disk uses lower-sheet translations as in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B, leaving the upper factor fixed. Translations and identifications on the collars of the joining disk are smooth because both upper sheets there are unramified. The $t$-variation is smooth in operator norm, uniformly on the disjoint upper factors.

Evaluation of the fixed-target stabilized line agrees with the actual line. At a central nodal map its polynomial coefficient solution is the ordinary lower odd generator and zero on all upper sheets, and the two indices agree by the finite projection formula. Evaluation of a coefficient solution with thick forcing is injective by interpolation, boundedness and removability on the normalization; a common root at a node has a single value. The two cokernels at the central map are therefore of equal dimension. The matching, lower and upper thick supplements span both: for the matching forces this follows also by using the lower cutoff section with prescribed mismatch, and for upper forces by the preceding odd-coefficient test. To justify the matching cutoff in the polynomial presentation, with $w=z^2$ and a free upper root at $z=0$, a constant-valued mismatch is represented distributionally by $z^2 H(w)a/w$ as in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B; its off-pole cutoff derivative is exactly the matching class. These forms may be chosen on regular sheet disks or annuli, and their extensions may be taken in common there. Surjectivity persists and both stabilized lines have rank one, so evaluation and the common normalization identify their obstruction coefficients. This is the stabilization comparison of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B with a further quadratic summand away from the selected disk.

For each upper center take its own central normalized generator and dual obstruction tests. They range in continuous finite-dimensional bundles over the compact subset. On the joining disk the central multiplier is bounded in the polynomial frame and the tests have uniform Hölder norms by interior elliptic estimates. The joining matrix difference has $L^1$ norm $O(|p|+|Q'|)$ with $Q'=q'\log(1/|q'|)$; against such tests its only $q'$ term of this order is the mass above, with remainder $o(|p|+|Q'|)$. These are the disk estimates of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B, independent of any off-disk collision. The stabilized generator difference tends uniformly to zero in $C^0$ by fixed-target $W^{1,a}$ continuity, so its product with this $L^1$ variation has the same little-oh bound. The smooth principal-part differences on collars use convergence in $W^{1,a}$. In the central adjoint testing the image of the unchanged operator drops out. Thick forms whose lifts vary contribute their variation times obstruction coefficients tending to zero; equivalently a continuous change of supplement multiplies the reduced germ by its central invertible matrix plus $o(1)$ in this comparison. For the full normal difference the other terms are the ordinary lower branch/time derivatives. The normalized central generator is supported on the lower component, so there is no first-order operator variation on the upper zero section from these directions. These arguments are uniform along sequences with varying upper central parameter: the relative comparison is made with the central operator for that same upper cover, not with an operator at a different point of the upper discriminant.

Fix the splitting $N_c=\mathbb Cv\oplus\mathbb C B(c)v$. Using $S=T_rY\otimes T_{\rm mark}$ in the smoothing row, put $L(q)=\log(1/|q|)$ for small positive $|q|$ in a fixed Hermitian metric. In the ordered rows $P_f,\mathbb Cv,W,F$, the resulting expansion off $R$ is
$$
   (W_f\lambda,\ \pm vp,\ a\,\overline{du_s(q)}L(q),\ 0)
                +o\big(|\lambda|+|p|+|q|L(q)\big).                            \tag{7}
$$
Here and below the fixed lower tangent and $[B(c)v]$ identify the two fixed lines in this expression, with $a$ a nonzero constant in those frames. Indeed the coefficient in the $y'$ frame is the same cubic residue at every upper point, and $q'$ to first order is $du_s(q)$ in that frame. Replacing the norm inside the logarithm on a compact set off $R$ changes the expression by $O(|q|)$. The attaching sign has positive real determinant, regardless of the convention for subtracting endpoint values.

For clarity about coordinates in (7), local charts can first be used in the coordinates of the cubic computation. On $q=0$ the tangents normal to the zero section of this stratum are canonically the lower Hurwitz/time directions and the lower mark direction, and they may be split using the nodal calculation. Tubular coordinates extending this splitting can differ from the cubic coordinates in these directions by $O(|q|)+o(|\lambda|+|p|)$; this does not change (7). The node parameter itself changes by a nonzero smooth factor with the prescribed tangent $T_rY\otimes T_{\rm mark}$. The tangent-coordinate change contributes $o(|q|L(q))$ beyond its intrinsic leading term. At the tripod the same distinguished divisor gives the corresponding change of $q$ by a unit. For (5) one only needs this multiplicative change and continuity of the upper tests and their frames. The supports chosen above ensure that a change of extension on overlapping charts cannot introduce an uncontrolled multiple of the matching forcing into the upper pairing.

## Comparison of the localized classes

The ambient fixed-target stack is smooth along $Z$. The nodal stratum has the compact smooth subfamily $Z$ at $\lambda=p=0$; variation of $c'$ preserves the smooth marked charts, including (4). Choose smooth orbifold tubular coordinates respecting the distinguished nodal divisor. For example first take a tubular neighborhood of $Z$ inside that divisor, splitting its normal directions by the lower parameters and attaching mark, then take a tubular disk normal to the divisor. The derivatives identify the last line with $S$ as above. Bundle rows can be pulled back to these neighborhoods along the same retractions. The determinant orientations are obtained by deformation to complex normalization. The lower Hurwitz/time row has sign $\eta_f$, $p\mapsto\pm vp$ has positive real sign, and the remaining factors have the absolute orientation of $F$ and the complex orientations of $W$ and $S$. All complex evaluation and gluing factors have even real dimension.

Choose concentric neighborhoods $V_0\Subset V_1$ of the compact divisor $R$, with $V_1$ small enough for (5), and an extension $P'$ there whose least singular value is bounded below. Such an extension exists on an orbifold tubular neighborhood by bundle retraction and a metric. Arrange, by reducing $V_1$ and the normal radius, that the upper error divided by $|q|$ is less than half this lower bound throughout $V_1$. Independently (7) holds uniformly on $Z\setminus V_0$ with an injective leading block in the first three rows, bounded below there by a positive multiple of $|\lambda|+|p|+|q|L(q)$. The two choices are compatible since the normal radius can be reduced after the base neighborhoods have been chosen. At $q=0$ the nodal lower and attaching blocks are transverse uniformly on all of $Z$. These bounds already show that $Z$ is the entire projective zero set in a neighborhood at the prescribed genus.

Here is a homotopy of the obstruction on that neighborhood relative to its zero-free boundary. Extend $P'$ to a global map $P_{\rm ext}:S\to F$ with a cutoff supported in $V_1$ and equal to $P'$ on a smaller neighborhood containing $\overline V_0$. First interpolate the upper row to $P_{\rm ext}(q)$, leaving the other rows unchanged. On $V_0$ the interpolated row is nonzero for $q\ne0$ by (5); off $V_0$ the first three original rows suffice by (7). At $q=0$ the upper row stays zero. Next interpolate the first three rows to the leading expression in (7), keeping the new upper row. On $Z\setminus V_0$ (7) keeps this interpolation nonzero in the normal directions. On $V_0$, $q\ne0$ is excluded by the upper row alone; for $q=0$ the ordinary lower and attaching derivatives give the same conclusion. The expression $\overline{du_s(q)}L(q)$ extends continuously by zero at $q=0$, including over $R$. Finally interpolate the scalar $L(q)$ to one by positive multipliers. Off $R$ the matching quotient still detects every nonzero $q$, and on $R$ the upper row does. Thus this homotopy preserves the supported Thom class and isolation, including configurations with unsmoothed upper nodes. It does not require a uniform cubic logarithmic expansion as $du_s$ tends to zero.

After the two transverse blocks the remaining oriented normal equation is the injection
$$
              j(q)=\big(P_{\rm ext}(q),\ a\overline{du_s(q)}\big)
                                  \quad\text{in }F\oplus W.
$$
The localized Euler class of this equation is the Euler class of the quotient by $j(S)$: choose an oriented complementary bundle, use the Thom isomorphism on the image plane, and restrict the remaining Euler class to its zero section. The calculation of [Calculation E4](the-marked-double-cover-surface-at-a-quartic-join.md) gives its integral as $-2F_2(D_0)-\int_R e(J_r)$. The minus signs there use the conjugate-linear matching row off $R$ and its conjugate-linear transverse derivative along $R$; equivariant rational multisections retain the isotropy weight on the marked divisor. The projective link for a real kernel line gives twice this localized pairing. The two choices of lower label divide it by two. The upper isotropies are already included in the integral over the marked stack, including the involution at the tripod. The lower orientation contributes $\eta_f$, proving (1).

The families for distinct $r$ are compact and disjoint because their separating marked values are distinct. The isolating neighborhoods just proved are in the full stable-map charts, so excision permits addition to the other compact sectors in this genus. The classification and already evaluated contributions in the last section of [Calculation E3](a-ramified-double-joined-to-an-unramified-cover.md) give (2) for $b\ge6$. Determining the real plane Euler numbers, or relating them to counts at other operators, requires information about the family of polar maps beyond the marked surface's divisor intersections.

## The four-branch case and the elementary coefficient

The same calculation finishes the next source genus of the specified incidence when $l=2$ and the étale twists on the lower graph are regular as in [Calculation E1](the-normal-twist-at-a-ramified-quartic-tower.md). Write $A$ for the normal operator of the lower kernel graph in the homogeneous target. A connected degree-two cover of that graph has genus at least $2h-1$. This equals $k_*$ precisely when $l=2$. Equality requires an unramified tower and excludes any contracted decorations. Two positive normalizations at nonzero height already have total genus at least $2h>k_*$ for $l\ge2$. Thus at $l=2$ the only additional contribution to (2) is the sum
$$
                \frac{\eta_f}{2}\sum_{0\ne\lambda\in H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)}
                      \epsilon(A^\lambda).
$$
This is the smooth-point degree of Calculation E1 summed with its actual tower automorphisms. Its compact isolated points are disjoint from the joined families. The twist, upper-operator and polar regularity conditions are simultaneous: the two open dense perturbation arguments in Calculation E1 and 099 can use a common test disk near a good lower jet and tensors killing the lower height.

There is a corresponding integral coefficient after removal of the proper-divisor term. Put $k_0=k_*-1$, $n=2^{2g}$ and $N_-(A)=\#\{0\ne\lambda:\epsilon(A^\lambda)=-1\}$. With $x=(2\sin(v/2))^2$, first subtract from the connected degree-four change the contribution of the known degree-two factor of exponent $\eta_f$ and primitive genus $h$. Its degree-four term starts at $2h-1=k_0+l-1$, with coefficient $\eta_f(2^{2h}-1)/2$ as in [Calculation E2](first-quartic-contribution-at-a-ramified-double-wall.md). The connected genus-$k_0$ change in the specified incidence is $-b\eta_f S_C$ for $l\ge2$. Subtracting that multiple of $x^{k_0-1}$ removes the elliptic-tail term in (2), since the next coefficient of $x^{k_0-1}$ is $-(k_0-1)/12$. Consequently the contribution of this incidence to the next primitive degree-four coefficient, with that proper-divisor correction removed, is
$$
 \eta_f\left[(2S_C+1)\binom b2
 -\epsilon(D_0)\left(\frac{bng(g-1)}8+2bW_4+
                   \sum_{r}W_2(f(r),\mathbb C\,dk_r)\right)
 -\mathbf1_{\{l=2\}}N_-(A)\right].                                      \tag{8}
$$
Here $\mathbb C\,dk_r$ denotes the image line of the complex-linear fiber derivative, and $W_4,W_2$ are the signed smooth two-branch counts of [Calculation E5](polar-kernels-on-two-branched-double-covers.md). One generic reference and path suffice for the finite set of flags. At $l=2$ subtracting the first term of the degree-two factor cancels the half-integral part of the tower sum and gives $-N_-(A)$ inside the brackets. Every term of (8) is integral. This triangular subtraction is in the fixed-target connected series of the specified support. Any distinct simple quartic crossings must be included separately for a full path or universal product.

For $g=2$, $b=6$, the full supported connected genus-ten change is
$$
       \eta_f\left[34S_C+15-\epsilon(D_0)
                       \left(24+12W_4+\sum_r W_2(f(r),\mathbb C\,dk_r)\right)\right].
$$
Removing the genus-nine sine term replaces $34S_C$ by $30S_C$. The parity of the resulting integer is $1+\sum_r W_2(f(r),\mathbb C\,dk_r)$ modulo two.
