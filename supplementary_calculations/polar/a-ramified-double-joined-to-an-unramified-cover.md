# E3. A ramified double graph joined to an unramified cover

Let $D_t$ be a family of real Cauchy–Riemann operators on a rank-two complex bundle $N$ of degree $2g-2$ over a fixed closed curve $C$ of genus $g\ge2$. Assume $D_0$ is invertible. Let $f:Y\to C$ be a transverse double-cover crossing at zero as in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. Thus its $b=2l>0$ ramification points are simple with distinct images, $h=g(Y)=2g-1+l$, and the pullback kernel is generated by a real odd section $\xi$ whose graph $\Gamma$ is embedded. The variation of branch positions and increasing time gives an isomorphism onto $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb R\xi,\operatorname{coker} f^*D_0)$ of absolute sign $\eta_f$. Assume the projected antilinear jet at the joining points is nonzero, in the sense of the hypothesis of its Theorem 3.1 (the condition $[B(x_i)\partial_z\xi(r_i)]\ne0$ in $N_{x_i}/\mathbb C\partial_z\xi(r_i)$). The meeting points of $\Gamma$ and the zero section are exactly the $b$ ramification points: at any other zero the two points over the same value would have identical graph images.

Fix a connected unramified cover $u:T\to C$ of degree $m\ge1$ with $u^*D_0$ invertible. Write $a=|\operatorname{Aut}_C(u)|$, $\epsilon_u=\epsilon(u^*D_0)$ and $x=(2\sin(v/2))^2$. Take a nonempty subset $I$ of $j$ ramification points $r_i$ and points $s_i\in u^{-1}(f(r_i))$. The curve $X$ obtained by identifying $r_i$ with $s_i$ maps finitely to $C$ in degree $m+2$. Its arithmetic genus is $H=h+g(T)+j-1=h+m(g-1)+j$. Denote by $G_{I,s,k}$ the maps obtained from this finite map by adding contracted subcurves at smooth points and inserting contracted subcurves between the branches of joining nodes, in arithmetic genus $k$. Marked points may specialize onto contracted components. This is a specified part of the projective normal kernel incidence on the original support.

**Theorem.** For each $k\ge H$, $G_{I,s,k}$ is compact and isolated in the projective incidence near time zero. Its contribution to the change, in increasing time, of the absolute normal Euler integral is
$$
  (-1)^j\epsilon_u\eta_f\,[v^{2k-2}]x^{H-1}.                                      \tag{1}
$$
The compact loci from inequivalent data are disjoint. If the jet hypothesis holds at all $b$ points, summation over all such loci with this fixed $u$ gives the connected series
$$
  \frac{\epsilon_u\eta_f}{a}\,x^{h+g(T)-2}\big((1-mx)^b-1\big).                    \tag{2}
$$
The intervals and perturbations used for (1) may depend on $k$. These are supported contributions; the full wall may have additional components.

## Separating the sheets over the zero section

Fix the height of $\Gamma$ by a real normalization of the generator. A small neighborhood $U\subset N$ of $\Gamma\cup C$ admits an $m$-sheeted covering $p:\widetilde U\to U$ that is trivial along $\Gamma$ and restricts to $u$ along $C$. To construct it, use tubular neighborhoods of the two embedded curves meeting in disjoint small contractible neighborhoods of their $b$ crossing points. The two tangent complex lines at such a point are distinct. They span a real four-plane, and local coordinates straighten the two embedded disks to coordinate disks. Equivalently a regular neighborhood retracts to the two surfaces glued at the $b$ points. Glue the trivial cover of the graph tube and the pullback of $u$ on the zero-section tube by a bijection on each overlap. At $r_i$ send a distinguished copy $\Gamma^0$ to $s_i$; the bijections can be chosen independently. Transitivity on the fiber over $C$ gives connectedness. The lifted zero section is an embedded $T$. It meets $\Gamma^0$ in precisely one point above each ramification point, with distinct tangent lines, even though $m$ different source sheets in the original zero section coincide in $N$. The construction uses a neighborhood of the *union*. A lift of $f$ to the target curve $T$ is unnecessary.

The central graph map and every contracted decoration have a unique lift whose nonzero component lies on $\Gamma^0$. On the zero component this lift is the identity parametrization of the chosen copy of $T$. At the first join its value is prescribed, and path lifting fixes it everywhere. Constant connected blocks lift constantly with the values prescribed at their ends. This gives one choice also around the cycles made by several joins. A deck automorphism of $u$ fixing any one point is the identity. Thus replacing the tuple $(s_i)$ by its simultaneous deck translate gives an isomorphic finite map, and different orbits give distinct maps. The lower deck involution fixes all $r_i$ and acts by sign on the central line.

We use this lift locally on the space of **normalized** sections. On a chart of stabilized maps with universal domain $E\to B$, take the graph of the chosen lifted map in $E\times\widetilde U$. The projection $1\times p$ is a local diffeomorphism in the target variable and is injective on this compact graph, since the domain point is retained. It is injective on a neighborhood of the graph: otherwise two different points with equal images approach the compact graph and converge, up to a subsequence, to the same point, contradicting local injectivity. In the relative graph topology a nearby map, including one with smoothed nodes, has graph in the image of this neighborhood, hence it has a unique nearby lift. One can also lift the short-pointwise geodesic homotopy from the central map composed with a domain collapse. Both descriptions preserve the original map to $C$. Forgetful maps, automorphisms preserving the normalized lift, and transition maps act on the lifts by uniqueness. This identifies local mapping charts, including their stabilized equations when the slices and forms are transported by $dp$. It is not an integration over all lifts of an unpointed map. A nonregular $m$-sheeted cover need not give $m$ such lifts. [Swaminathan, definitions of the relative topology and local chart construction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12264#page=10).

## The degree at the finite map

Over a joining disk the finite-map algebra is the cubic nodal algebra $zy=q_i$, $w=w_i+(z+p_i)^2+y$, together with $m-1$ disjoint smooth factors. Over the other ramification disks it is the ordinary quadratic branch together with $m$ unramified factors. The idempotents of the local direct products lift under small deformations, and the normalized étale map has acyclic tangent complex. The finite-map deformation space is therefore smooth of complex dimension $b+2j$. Its coordinates are the lower branch positions and the independent attaching and smoothing parameters $(p_i,q_i)$. The equality $2H-2-(m+2)(2g-2)=b+2j$ also gives its fixed-target virtual dimension.

The matching sequence gives kernel $\mathbb R\xi$ and cokernel $P$ in an exact sequence
$$
 0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}N_{f(r_i)}
 \longrightarrow P\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}f^*D_0\longrightarrow0.
$$
Indeed the kernel on $T$ is zero and $\xi(r_i)=0$. The local logarithmic calculation in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B applies on each cubic factor. Extend its local residue and the correction form by zero on every other local factor. The representative of the matching fiber is supported on the selected joining branches. Thick forms on the normalization stabilize the global inverse; replacing a degree-one zero component by the invertible $u^*D_0$ adds no cokernel. On disjoint joining disks the estimates sum, including strata with only some of the $q_i$ nonzero. After the oriented radial substitution $Q_i=q_i\log(1/|q_i|)$, the linear part of the normalized obstruction section is block triangular with diagonal blocks $W_f$, $p_i\mapsto\pm v_i p_i$, and $Q_i\mapsto c_i\bar Q_i[B(f(r_i))v_i]$ in the quotient by $\mathbb C v_i$, where $v_i=\partial_z\xi(r_i)$ and $c_i$ is a nonzero real constant. The remainder divided by the sum of the sizes of these coordinates and the branch/time displacement tends to zero, by the same thick inverse and disjoint neck estimates. This gives isolation and local degree $(-1)^j\eta_f\epsilon_u$ in the sheet-labelled chart. Its determinant contains exactly the one invertible zero component, with its absolute sign. The finite map has automorphism group of order two, generated by the lower involution. The Euler link supplies twice this signed degree, and the quotient by that involution divides by two. This proves (1) in genus $H$. The argument at one join in degree four agrees with [Calculation E2](first-quartic-contribution-at-a-ramified-double-wall.md).

## Contracted components and the virtual comparison

Fix $k$. Properness of $G_{I,s,k}$ follows by gluing evaluation fibers of the degree-one maps to $Y$ and $T$, with the prescribed marks, and proper spaces of two-pointed contracted curves of positive arithmetic genus at any bridged join. The remaining components are included in the two degree-one map spaces. Distribute $k-H$ among the factors and take their finite union. In the lifted graph the two positive images are embedded with multiplicity one. A connected constant block with two positive ends can only join the two different components. Its image determines its joining point. The same holds for direct nodes. These facts recover $I$ and the tuple modulo deck equivalence, also at colliding attachment marks. Restricting sections to the normalized components and matching on each constant block gives exactly the original real kernel line.

Here are the modifications to the displacement and virtual argument in Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. Choose a disk of regular values away from the $b$ branch values, and label the two sheets of $Y$ there up to the lower involution. The selected flags identify the $T$ component with a fixed pointed cover, so there is no ambiguity from its deck group after fixing that identification. Choose an ordering of its $m$ sheets over the disk once in this pointed model. Over nearby fixed-target maps the ordering extends locally by following the disjoint degree-one preimages of a slightly larger disk, including contracted components there. On the compact central family the selected orderings are locally constant; shrinking the neighborhood extends them as a two-sheeted labelling cover. The two choices are for $Y$. Source automorphisms on the ghosts are retained. An area integral on a distinguished $Y$ sheet supplies a real test $\ell_a$ with $\ell_a(\xi)=1$ after normalization. It is unchanged by contracted attachments. Here $a$ in a subscript denotes a stable map.

First take zero displacement. For a sequence of projective kernel maps converging to the proposed locus, use this normalization and the chosen local lift. In the cover of the image neighborhood take the stable limits separately of the smooth positive normalizations after forgetting their nodal flags. For the energy bound use a symplectic form on a bounded disk bundle in $N$ taming the original structure near the compact graph. One obtains such a form from the fiberwise Hermitian symplectic form with a unitary connection by adding a sufficiently large multiple of the base area form. Its class on the disk bundle is pulled back from $C$. On a relatively compact lifted tube its pullback tames all sufficiently small displacements, and its integral on a closed graph is fixed by the projection degree. Each positive normalization thus has bounded area. Their combined nonconstant limit consists of $\Gamma^0$ and $T$ once each. A component in a fiber would project into a vector space and be constant there, hence would be constant in the cover. There are at most two positive normalizations. A contracted block in one of their unmarked limits would require a critical attaching branch, a pair of equal complex tangent lines, or three positive branches at one image point by ghost bubble censorship. The lifted embeddings exclude all three alternatives. The theorem applies locally to convergent maps; a smooth parameter family can be placed in the product with a complex parameter disk carrying the block-diagonal structure. [Ekholm–Shende, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.05835#page=2).

With two positive normalizations, each converges smoothly to one embedding. Each nearby pair has at most one common image near each central intersection: projection of the difference of the two parametrizations to the real span of the two distinct tangents has invertible derivative. There are no intersections elsewhere on a compact complement. Thus deletion of one-ended contracted blocks and replacement of two-ended blocks by nodes produces a finite-map solution. With a single positive normalization its unmarked limit is the union of the two embeddings joined at some nonempty subset of their intersections. With two normalizations the compression recovers the prescribed joining data: distinct intersection flags on either component remain distinct, and marked stabilization can insert a contracted tree at a flag without changing the joining value. With a single normalization, every joining node of its unmarked limit persists on restoring the marks, possibly replaced by a chain. Gluing the other marked components cannot remove that connection. Its bare joining set $J$ is therefore a nonempty subset of $I$, with the same lifted flags at those sites. A self-identification at a remaining intersection before displacement is considered below. At zero displacement the finite-map calculation for each such bare joining subset and restricted tuple excludes any nearby different finite zero tending to that bare map, irrespective of the added flags. A nearby single smooth positive normalization tending to a reducible bare union is therefore impossible. In the two-normalization case there are only the finite joins themselves with contracted decorations. This proves isolation before introducing an affine perturbation.

On the labelled fixed-target charts use the homogeneous finite-rank perturbation
$$
        L_{a,t}v-\ell_a(v)j_a(e),\qquad L_{a,t}=a^*D_t.
$$
Here $j_a(e)$ consists of smooth forms pulled back separately on the $m+2$ source pieces. On a nodal value in their support, subtract the pullback of a local primitive on its own sheet and change the scalar row by its integral, as in the nodal stabilization of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. The remaining forcing is supported away from the neck; the usual stabilized inverse and the area bound on the scalar integral give continuity. The projective incidence is thus a homotopy of the same supported Thom class. On the slice $\ell_a(v)=1$ the equation is affine. Each of the $m+2$ sheets has a **different height point upstairs** over the test disk. Independent fiber cutoffs there realize all these forcings by a single ordinary almost complex structure in $\widetilde U$ with holomorphic projection to $C$. It agrees with the pullback structure off these neighborhoods. The source-sheet perturbation is defined downstairs on the labelled chart. Its target realization is needed only in the chosen lift. None of the forcings is required to descend as a target tensor on $N$.

At a bare chart the sheet forms span the stabilized cokernel by adjoint unique continuation on the two positive normalizations and independent evaluation at the joining flags. Apply the same marked value and first-derivative tests on smooth covers as in Article IV, Appendix B.3.2: an adjoint annihilating the thick tests vanishes off the marked points; prescribing endpoint values and first jets kills the remaining point distributions. Regular small displacements therefore have only transverse smooth embedded finite graph zeros in the lifted target and avoid the strata of persistent nodes and repeated branch values. At any fixed genus bound only the finitely many nonempty subsets $J\subseteq I$ with the restricted flags can occur in these limits. The jet hypothesis holds there. The same sheet forcings over the regular test disk can be made regular on each of these finitely many bare charts. Their signs sum at each bare chart to its finite local degree.

For clarity, consider the possible single positive normalization for a sequence of displaced solutions as $e\to0$. Its unmarked central limit is a bare joined union. The fixed genus of that normalization is the arithmetic genus of this union, since ghost censorship has excluded contracted blocks *in the unmarked limit*. It therefore belongs to the corresponding finite chart and, at the regular displacements, is an embedded graph upstairs. This excludes a self-node and a contracted block with distinct attachment points on this same normalization. On adding back the remaining one-ended subcurves and taking their marked limits, a bridge can form at a node of the bare limit. Marks approaching distinct points over an unjoined intersection remain at distinct points after stabilization; a single attached curve cannot create a link between them. This proves the required agreement of the bare joining set, including the case where one initially allowed an additional self-identification before displacing. In the two-normalization case deletion and replacement of constant blocks give a finite *nodal* solution, excluded at a regular displacement. Conversely the expansions of each smooth displaced graph solve the same equation. Their compact marked limits belong to the chosen gluing locus. These arguments take place in nested relatively compact tubes in the finite cover; all relevant limiting supports lie in the interior.

Finally, on a common stabilized chart of maps to $C$ and maps to the lifted target, keep the same stabilized domain, incidence slices and horizontal obstruction row $s_C$. Near a displaced graph the vertical homogeneous operator $L_{a,t}$ is injective, since its underlying smooth finite map belongs to the isolating bare chart away from the original nodal zero. Restriction and matching extend this to its degree-one expansions. If $B w$ is a minimal vertical supplement, the affine equation $L_{a,t}v-Bw=j_a(e)$ has a unique solution $(v,w)$ on the horizontal thickening near its center, with the affine horizontal lift used in Article IV, Appendix B.3.4. The projection $p$ is a local diffeomorphism, so it identifies tangent rows, stabilizing forms (including those on ghosts) and matching fibers with the lifted rows wherever the original structure is used. Near the displaced sheets the cutoffs are constant and the vertical equation is the indicated affine one. The normalized residual equations are $s_C=0$, $w=0$, $\ell_a(v)=1$. Their product of Thom classes computes the localized projective pairing. Adding markings and common supplements gives the same rows up to stabilization and triangular operations, preserving their orientations. No comparison of virtual classes for a globally non-descending perturbation is used.

For each displaced embedded graph the augmented normal operator with time and the integral constraint is invertible. As in Article IV, Appendix B.3.5 a small height-linear perturbation supported on an unramified lifted sheet and vanishing on the graph makes its index-zero fixed-time normal operator invertible, preserving the isolated constrained degree. In a smaller tube around the embedded graph the projection of any positive component has degree a positive multiple of $m+2$. Hence this local sector in total degree $m+2$ consists of one normalization of multiplicity one with one-ended attachments. Ghost censorship and the implicit function theorem identify the local unconstrained family with the degree-one expansions; the integral constraint cuts its time interval at a simple zero. The normal first Chern number of a genus-$H$ embedded graph is $2H-2$, because $p$ is locally an isomorphism of the original tangent bundles and the small almost complex perturbation preserves $c_1$. To apply the compact-target comparison after slicing, take a symplectic tube of this one embedded curve. By the symplectic neighborhood theorem it is a tube of the zero section of its symplectic normal bundle $V$. Embed a smaller such tube in $\mathbb P(V\oplus\mathbb C)$ using a unitary connection, a fiberwise Fubini–Study form of sufficiently small fiber area and the area form of the curve on the base. The zero sections have the same symplectic normal data (rescaling the fiber identification if necessary). Transport the given tame structure, and extend it from a smaller tube using the contractible space of tame structures. The local insertions and fixed-time obstruction germs agree by restriction to the common tube. This supplies the compact ambient required by Zinger and only uses the one isolated embedded graph. The fixed-target comparison and the degree-one Hodge integral give the fixed-time connected series equal to its determinant sign times $x^{H-1}$. The orientation of the slice times this determinant sign is its signed bare degree, as seen in genus $H$. The real horizontal virtual dimension and vertical index parity are even in every source genus, so the calibration persists. As at the finite map the projective link yields two and the source labelling has degree two. Choosing the distinguished lifted graph adds no covering factor. This proves (1). Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B · [Bai–Swaminathan, §6.2 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4) · [Zinger, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/0807.0805v3) · [Pandharipande, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811140).

For a fixed subset there are $m^j/a$ orbits, since the deck action on its first selected fiber is free. There are $\binom bj$ choices of the subset. Summing (1) gives (2). For $m=1$ this recovers the reducible cubic formula of Article IV, §§2–3 and Appendices A–B. The equality is coefficientwise for each $u$ satisfying the hypotheses. The general wall may also contain other configurations.

## The quartic contribution one genus above the minimum

Assume the projected jet condition at every ramification point of $f$. Put $m=2$, $H_1=h+2g-1$ and $S_C=\sum_{u}\epsilon_u$ over connected unramified doubles, assumed all invertible at the crossing as in Calculation E2. At genus $H_1+1$ the one-join term per $(r,u)$ is $\eta_f\epsilon_u(H_1-1)/12$. Indeed $x^{H_1-1}=v^{2H_1-2}(1-(H_1-1)v^2/12+O(v^4))$ and the bare sign is negative. This includes a contracted elliptic tail moving to the joining point and every stable two-ended genus-one bridge there. Two direct joins at distinct ramification points also contribute in this genus. They have two flag orbits per pair for each $u$. The total for connected unramified zero components is therefore
$$
 \eta_f S_C\left(\frac{b(H_1-1)}{12}+2\binom b2\right).                         \tag{3}
$$
There is also an isolated finite configuration consisting of two zero-height identity components, each joined directly to $Y$ at a different ramification point. The genus is $h+2g=H_1+1$, so there are no contracted decorations in this configuration. Its finite chart has two independent cubic nodal factors, the same branch/time block, and two invertible normal components of sign $\epsilon(D_0)$. Their product is $\epsilon(D_0)^2=1$, and the two smoothing blocks also have positive total sign. The stabilizer is exactly the lower involution: the two identities are distinguished by the different target values of their joins. There is one map for each unordered pair. This adds $\binom b2\eta_f$ to (3). For $g=2$, $b=6$, one has $H_1=9$ and this **combined supported contribution** in genus ten is $\eta_f(34S_C+15)$.

For $l\ge3$ the remaining positive-degree configurations at this genus, in the specified incidence of Calculation E2, can be described uniformly. A cover of $Y$ of degree two has normalization genus at least $2h-1>H_1+1$ and two nonzero double components cost at least $2h$. With one nonzero copy of $Y$, a smooth zero-height double of genus $2g$ has two simple branch values and can join it at one point over a chosen $f(r)$. In the closure the two branch values may coalesce. The normalization of the zero-height nodal double is then either a connected unramified double or two copies of $C$, with one conjugate pair identified over the collision point. If the selected mark is this node, stabilizing the mark inserts a trivalent rational component, with the other two flags the distinct branches of the zero double. In particular the configuration with flags at $r$ and at *both* points of $T$ above $f(r)$ lies outside the compact one-join sector of (1). The two zero points have different images in its chosen target lift. If the mark is smooth the zero-to-zero node is retained. Conversely, after the already counted loci are removed, genus additivity admits only these possibilities: with an étale normalization one excess either decorates one join, provides another direct join, or identifies its conjugate points; with two identity normalizations the dual graph must be a tree with two direct edges or one trivalent contracted vertex. Thus the remaining locus is the family of finite double covers of branch degree two with a marking evaluated at $f(r)$, on the stable marked compactification. Its absolute Euler change, especially when the marking is ramified, has yet to be evaluated. When $l=2$ smooth unramified towers on $Y$ also occur, and for $l=1$ the upper double-cover and two-nonzero-component sectors enter. The formula above makes no assignment of a separate virtual weight to an individual ghost stratum.
