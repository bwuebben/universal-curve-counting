# E7. Prescribed polar crossings in arbitrary source genus

Fix a smooth compact curve $C$ of genus $g\ge2$, set $n=2^{2g}$, and let $N\to C$ be a complex rank-two bundle of degree $2g-2$. For $k\ge1$ prescribe $m=2k-1$ distinct points $c_1,\ldots,c_m$ and complex lines $\ell_i\subset N_{c_i}$. Consider double covers $u:Y\to C$ simply branched over $c_1,\ldots,c_m$ and one further point $p$. The source genus is $2g-1+k$. At the ramification point $r_i$ over $c_i$, allow an odd solution of $u^*D$ to have a simple pole in direction $\ell_i$, for a real Cauchy–Riemann operator $D$ on $N$. In a holomorphic frame for the complex-linear part this means $s=v_i/y_i+\text{regular}$, with $v_i\in\ell_i$, in a coordinate centered at $r_i$. An odd solution changes sign under the double-cover involution. Real projectivization identifies solutions differing by a nonzero real factor.

Let $E^{\rm odd}_k(D)$ be the integral of the Euler class of the odd augmented cokernel over the compact parameter gerbe defined below, whenever the odd augmented operator is injective. The orientation continues the complex determinant orientation, with the complex orientation on the added principal-part domain. This choice removes the independent sign of the even operator on the base. A positive spin operator on $a\oplus b$ means the direct sum of the real spin operators for specified theta characteristics $a,b$, with $a^2=b^2=K_C$. Its symmetric form $Q$ is the sum of their square forms. At a spin endpoint assume each $\ell_i$ is $Q$-isotropic. For the repeated positive spin $a=b$ use identical metrics and write $\kappa_i=+1$ on the line $(v,-iv)$ and $\kappa_i=-1$ on $(v,iv)$.

**Theorem.** For two regular endpoints, the difference $E^{\rm odd}_k(D_1)-E^{\rm odd}_k(D_0)$ equals the signed count of projective odd polar solutions along a generic fixed-symbol path between them. The solutions have smooth sources, real kernel dimension one, and nonzero principal part at every prescribed mark. The following endpoints are regular and have the indicated values:
$$
\begin{aligned}
 E^{\rm odd}_k(D_{\rm hol})&=\frac{n(g-1)}4
     &&\text{for a generic complex-linear operator},\\
 E^{\rm odd}_k(D_{a,a})&=\frac{n}{4}(-1)^k
         \left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_i\right)
         \left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_i\right),\\
 E^{\rm odd}_k(D_{a,b})&=0 &&\text{for }a\ne b.
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{1}
$$

In particular choose all marked points in one disk, identify the isotropic axes there at the two spin endpoints, and choose the $+1$ axis at every point for the repeated spin. A single path from the repeated to the distinct positive spin can be chosen generic for each initial segment of length $2k-1$ of an infinite sequence of distinct points in the disk. In every genus $2g-1+k$ its signed count is $(-1)^{k+1}(2k-1)n/4$. It has at least $(2k-1)n/4$ projective solutions in that genus. This counts time, cover, and kernel line together. Different covers may occur at the same time. The covering degree is always two and the number of prescribed poles grows with $k$.

## The compact curve and the collision at a marked point

Put $B_0=\sum_i c_i$. The coarse parameter is
$$
 D_k=C\times_{\operatorname{Pic}^{2k}C}\operatorname{Pic}^k C,\qquad
                 p\longmapsto\mathcal O_C(B_0+p),
$$
using squaring on the Picard factor. The map $\rho:D_k\to C$ is the connected étale cover of degree $n$ corresponding to the kernel of reduction of $H_1(C)$ modulo two. This follows from the Abel map on the moving point. Write $\mathcal R_k\to D_k$ for the gerbe of square-root identifications $M^2=\mathcal O_C(B_0+p)$. The branch section defines the double-cover algebra $\mathcal O\oplus M^{-1}$. Over the open set $p\notin B_0$, each specified marking is forced. At $p=c_i$ the corresponding finite cover has a node over $c_i$. Its normalization is a double cover with the other $2k-2$ simple branch values. For $k=1$ this includes a split normalization. Stabilize the mark $r_i$ with a rational component between the two preimages $e_+,e_-$ of $c_i$. The remaining $m-1$ prescribed poles stay on the normalization. This compact marked family has stabilizer $\mu_2$ even at a collision. For the colliding branch values one may use the chart of [Calculation E4](the-marked-double-cover-surface-at-a-quartic-join.md), retaining the other simple branches away from its disk. The construction is also the pullback of the universal stable marked curve.

Write $S_i=T_{r_i}Y\otimes\ell_i$ on the stabilized marked family. The upper second derivative at $r_i$ defines a section of $\mathbb L_i^2\otimes T_{c_i}C$, with $\mathbb L_i=T^*_{r_i}Y$. The first derivative vanishes on the whole family, so this is intrinsic. It is nonzero at every simple branch marking, including when a different branch pair collides. In a chart at $p=c_i$, take $w$ centered at $c_i$, $z=1$ at its mark, and
$$
 w=A(z+z^{-1}-2),\qquad d=A(z-z^{-1}),\qquad w(p)=-4A.
                                                               \tag{2}
$$
Here $d$ is the local odd square root with $d^2=w(w+4A)$. The second derivative at $1$ is $2A$, so every one of the $n$ points over $c_i$ is a simple zero, counted with stack weight $1/2$. This proves
$$
                         \int_{\mathcal R_k}c_1(S_i)=-n/4.       \tag{3}
$$

The real index of the unmodified odd operator at a smooth cover is $-4k$. Indeed its complex reference is $\bar\partial_{N\otimes M^{-1}}$ of complex index $-2k$. Adding the $m$ complex principal parts gives real index $-2$. A cutoff principal part has smooth off-pole image because the antilinear coefficient pulls back with the factor $\overline{u'(y_i)}$, divisible by $\bar y_i$. Changing the holomorphic frame, coordinate, or cutoff changes this image by a regular image. One may take odd cutoffs and coordinates, or project equivariantly. All marked disks in the stabilized family stay away from the nodes. The regular off-pole form varies continuously there, also when the upper map becomes constant on the rational component. This follows by factoring $u'(y_i)$ by $y_i$ on the family of smooth marked disks, including (2). Adding the finitely many principal parts to the usual stabilized odd Fredholm charts therefore gives a continuous Fredholm reduction on the compact family, smooth on each stratum. [Nodal stabilization](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01206v4#A2) · [Two-branch polar equation](polar-kernels-on-two-branched-double-covers.md).

At the exceptional fiber the normalization with its other $2k-2$ principal parts has odd index $-4(k-1)+2(2k-2)=0$. The odd meromorphic functions on the bridge with at most a simple pole at $1$ are the multiples of $(z+1)/(z-1)$. Their endpoint values are opposite, and the allowed coefficient lies in $\ell_i$. Eliminating the bridge imposes the additional condition $s(e_+)\bmod\ell_i=0$ on the odd normalization, of real target dimension two. Indeed regular constants on the bridge have zero odd part, its Dolbeault cokernel vanishes, and its added odd principal part covers exactly the $\ell_i$ direction in the endpoint difference. This gives the same net index $-2$. If the normalization splits, the deck transformation exchanges the two positive components and an odd solution is determined on either one.

## The signed count

Use the real projectivized incidence of nonzero odd solutions for a path. A universal zeroth-order variation supported on a disk away from marked and branch values can have arbitrary value on a nonzero solution there. In the adjoint annihilator test the two sheets contribute equally by oddness. Variation of the regular section gives the adjoint equation away from the special points, so unique continuation forces the adjoint to vanish. On a nodal normalization the same reasoning applies to its punctured positive part. The remaining multiplier of the matching condition vanishes by varying endpoint values of the regular odd section freely. A kernel could have no positive part only if a bridge section vanished at both ends, which forces its principal part to vanish. These arguments prove surjectivity of the universal equation on both strata; variations of the path can be localized in time in its interior.

The expected real dimension on the smooth stratum is $-2+2+1-1=0$, accounting for the index, the parameter curve, time, and real projectivization. At each possible collision there are two fewer real parameters. Omitting any one pole also lowers the index by two. On a collision where the omitted pole would have been on the bridge this amounts to the full condition $s(e_+)=0$; otherwise the omitted pole lies on the normalization. The same adjoint test applies to these restricted equations. Sard–Smale regularity thus excludes all collision and omitted-pole incidences on a generic path. A kernel of dimension at least two would have a positive-dimensional projective fiber, excluded by regularity of the dimension-zero incidence. Properness for each fixed $k$ follows from compactness of the parameter curve and time interval and the continuous finite-dimensional Fredholm reductions, which realize projective kernels as closed sets in projectivized finite-dimensional spaces. The regular incidence is consequently finite.

At a simple crossing the parameter and time derivatives map isomorphically to $\operatorname{Hom}(\ker,\operatorname{coker})$, of real dimension three. Give the sign by the oriented cokernel Euler integral on the link divided by $2$ on a local atlas, with increasing time. The ordinary reduced cokernel on this link is the plane perpendicular to the transverse image of the radial line. Its Euler integral is $+2$ or $-2$, since $\chi(S^2)=2$. The constant stack stabilizer $\mu_2$ divides it by two. Excision gives the signed endpoint difference with these conventions. An independent even base kernel along the path makes no difference to the odd family.

## The three endpoints

For a complex-linear reference $E$, take the positive elementary modification $E^+$ allowing a simple pole in $\ell_i$ at each $c_i$. On the fixed curve $C$ the odd augmented cohomology is precisely that of $E^+M^{-1}$. On a regular disk an odd section is $d\,v$ for an even coefficient $v$. At a prescribed branch it may additionally have coefficient proportional to $d/(w-c_i)$ in $\ell_i$. On the bridge in (2) this ratio is $(z+1)/(z-1)$. It extends the two limiting values of the normalization section and is uniquely determined by them. This proves the sheaf description at collisions as well. It also compares cokernels, since test forms supported on regular disks suffice for elliptic stabilization of the cohomology on both sides.

The degree of $E^+M^{-1}$ is $2g-2+m-2k=2g-3$, so its complex index is $-1$. The complex parameter $D_k$ has dimension one. For any nonzero section, complex-linear variations on a regular disk detect its entire adjoint annihilator, as in the real test. Complex projectivization gives expected complex dimension $-1$. Thus a generic holomorphic structure has zero kernel on the whole compact parameter curve.

For the index degree choose a basepoint $c_*\notin B_0$. Normalize the Poincaré root $M_0$ there, so $M=M_0\otimes T$ with $T^2$ the evaluation of the universal branch line at $c_*$. On $D_k$ that evaluation has degree $n$, from the $n$ simple occurrences of $p=c_*$. If $P$ is the polarization pulled back from $\operatorname{Pic}^k C$, the mixed Poincaré class in curve Riemann–Roch gives $c_1(-R\pi_*(E^+ M_0^{-1}))=2P$. The virtual rank is one. Consequently $c_1(J)=2P-c_1(T)$. Since $[2]^*\Theta=4\Theta$ and the Abel polarization has degree $g$ on $C$, $\int_{D_k}P=ng/4$. The gerbe integration factor $1/2$ gives the first line of (1).

For either positive spin pair, a polar solution on the smooth double satisfies the spin energy identity on the surface punctured at the marks. In the integral of the derivative of $du\,Q(s,s)$ the boundary term at $r_i$ tends to a constant times $Q(v_i,v_i)=0$. This uses the simple zero of $du$ and $s=v_i/y_i+O(1)$. On a kernel solution the derivative, after a fixed normalization of the area form, is a density of one sign, proportional to the squared mass term. Thus $s$ vanishes first on a regular disk and then everywhere. On the nodal normalization the same proof uses the other $2k-2$ isotropic poles. The bridge in (2) has vanishing endpoint values once the positive part vanishes. This proves injectivity on the compact family. It also proves injectivity when the mass is multiplied by a smooth nonnegative function positive on a regular disk of each positive component.

At the repeated spin the unmodified ordinary cokernel on the compact family is $V\oplus V$ for the real rank-$2k$ spin cokernel $V$. The invariant index-zero operator is invertible, so the cokernel is entirely odd. The full determinant orientation of this repeated operator agrees with the odd orientation: any sign from a single even spin component occurs twice. Rotation $I(s_1,s_2)=(-s_2,s_1)$ makes this cokernel $V\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ with vanishing rational first Chern class. Its $I$ orientation differs from its product orientation by the sign of interleaving two blocks of length $2k$, namely $(-1)^{(2k)(2k-1)/2}=(-1)^k$. The direct sum of the polar maps injects $\bigoplus_i S_i$ as an $I$-invariant complex subbundle because the augmented kernel is zero. On the $i$th summand the complex orientation of its image relative to the principal part is $\kappa_i$, since $I$ acts by $\kappa_i i$ on its pole. Formula (3) gives
$$
 \int_{\mathcal R_k} c_1(J,I)=-\sum_i\kappa_i\int_{\mathcal R_k}c_1(S_i)
         =\frac n4\sum_i\kappa_i.
$$
The quotient Fredholm orientation relative to the $I$ orientation is $(-1)^k\prod_i\kappa_i$. This proves the second value in (1).

For distinct spins the symmetric form extends to a nondegenerate form on $E^+$ with values in $K_C(B_0)$. At each modification use an isotropic frame with first vector spanning $\ell_i$ and replace that vector by its quotient by $w-c_i$. Thus $U=E^+\otimes M^{-1}$ on $C\times\mathcal R_k$ has a symmetric holomorphic map $\sigma:U\to K_C U^*$ from its form multiplied by the branch section. It vanishes simply in both directions at $p$ and is invertible elsewhere. This holds also at $p=c_i$ by the elementary modification at $c_i$.

The odd augmented real cokernel identifies, up to orientation, with the cokernel of the spin equation for this $\sigma$ on $C$. To see this, deform the mass upstairs by a nonnegative cutoff zero on neighborhoods of all fixed branch values and of $p$, retaining a regular disk of positive mass. This is possible continuously for the family, including collisions, since the number of prescribed points is finite. The isotropic-residue argument preserves injectivity. The equation now descends by $s=d\,v$ off the branch disks. In this frame $\bar d/d=\overline{d^2}/|d^2|$, so the descended conjugate mass is that determined by $\sigma$ and a positive Hermitian metric. Near the branch disks the equation is Dolbeault and the preceding holomorphic transfer applies, with (2) at any collision. For forms supported in a regular disk the stabilized inhomogeneous solutions coincide under this transfer. Using the same finite-dimensional supplements and the adjoint test therefore identifies the continuous cokernel bundles. Extend the metric smoothly through the smaller zero-mass neighborhoods and deform to a positive smooth mass everywhere. The symmetric energy identity gives injectivity on the fixed curve throughout. The Dolbeault resolution of $[U\to K_C U^*]$ now identifies the real cokernel with a positive real form of the orthogonal plane $(K_C U^*)|_{w=p}$ with its residue pairing as in Article V, Theorem 4.1.

Put $\alpha=b/a$. The two axes of $E$ are ordered on the connected étale double $\widetilde C\to C$ of $\alpha$ and are both isomorphic there to the pullback of $a$. The deck transformation interchanges them. Each modification increases the degree of the first axis at one lift of $c_i$ and of the second at the other. Their degrees in $E^+$ on $\widetilde C$ remain equal, independently of the choices of the $\ell_i$. The moving $p$ factors through $\widetilde C$ on $D_k$. Consequently the two isotropic lines in the residue plane have equal degrees on $\mathcal R_k$, since all other factors from $K_C$, $M$, and duality are common. The perfect residue pairing identifies them as dual lines. Both degrees vanish. Up to sign either is the Euler number of the oriented real form. This proves the final value in (1).

## One path for all the genera

For any finite $k$ the regular reference structures and the paths just used form open dense sets in the corresponding smooth spaces. Openness follows from the compact projectivized finite-dimensional reductions for $k$ fixed, absence of zeros on excluded strata and smooth transversality near the remaining finitely many points. For density in the smooth topology, the adjoint tests provide finitely many smooth perturbation directions spanning the missing equations in local reductions around any compact zero locus. First arrange avoidance on the compact nodal locus, then work on a compact complement of its zero-free neighborhood, doing the same for omitted poles before the full smooth incidence. Arbitrarily small linear combinations give the required regular values by finite-dimensional parametric transversality. Equivalently one can use Sard–Smale in sufficiently high finite regularity followed by smooth approximation; the regularity order can be taken above any finite set of seminorms specified in a smooth neighborhood.

The smooth Dolbeault connections form an affine Fréchet Baire space. Intersecting the regular sets gives one complex reference for any prescribed countable list of finite tests. The space of smooth fixed-symbol paths with two specified regular endpoints is also Baire, and interior perturbations suffice. Given an infinite sequence of distinct $c_i$ in one disk, identify the isotropic frame pairs of the repeated and distinct spin bundles there. The identification extends as a smooth bundle isomorphism, since the two rank-two bundles have the same first Chern class and a disk change of frame is nullhomotopic on its boundary. Thus the same flags give regular endpoints in every genus. The intersection of the countably many regularity sets supplies a single path. Apply (1) with all $\kappa_i=1$ to obtain the final assertion of the theorem. No uniform lower bound on the distance between successive singular times is needed. The calculation concerns the augmented odd Fredholm equation of the covers for the prescribed poles; a multiple-join contribution to the quartic virtual class would require its own local comparison.

## A general orthogonal normal bundle

Let $N$ have a holomorphic structure with a nondegenerate symmetric form $Q:N\otimes N\to K_C$. For a Hermitian metric $h$, let $D_{Q,h}=\bar\partial_N+c_h Q$ be its positive spin operator, where $c_h:K_C N^*\to\Lambda^{0,1}T^*C\otimes N$ is the conjugate identification supplied by $h$. Prescribe either isotropic line at each $c_i$. The two isotropic lines in each fiber form an étale double cover of $C$, which may be disconnected.

**Corollary.** The augmented odd operator is injective on $\mathcal R_k$ for every $h$. If the isotropic-line cover is connected, then $E^{\rm odd}_k(D_{Q,h})=0$. If it is disconnected, let $N=L_+\oplus L_-$ be its two global isotropic lines, $d_\pm=\deg L_\pm$, and $m_\pm$ the numbers of prescribed poles in those lines. In this case
$$
 E^{\rm odd}_k(D_{Q,h})=
 (-1)^{d_-+m_--k+1-g}\,\frac n4
                  (d_+-d_-+m_+-m_-).                              \tag{4}
$$
In particular the value is independent of the ordering of the axes. At fixed target, source genus, and marked values, its absolute value can be arbitrarily large as the holomorphic orthogonal bundle varies.

**Proof.** The energy argument used for a spin pair integrates the derivative of $du\,Q(s,s)$. It uses only holomorphicity, symmetry, positivity of the Hermitian metric, and isotropy of the principal parts. It gives injectivity for any such $Q$, including on the normalization at $p=c_i$ with its remaining prescribed poles. The bridge argument is unchanged. This proves regularity on the compact family, also during homotopies of Hermitian metrics or nonnegative cutoffs that retain a regular disk of positive mass. In a local isotropic frame the form pairs the two axes with each other, so the modified bundle $N^+$ again has a nondegenerate form with values in $K_C(B_0)$. Consequently the fixed-curve reduction above applies with $U=N^+M^{-1}$ and identifies the polar cokernel up to orientation with a positive real form of the orthogonal plane $(K_C U^*)|_{w=p}$. The comparison uses the same local factor $d/(w-c_i)$ at a colliding pair, and thick inhomogeneous terms on regular disks.

When the cover of axes $\widehat C\to C$ is connected, its deck involution exchanges the two axis lines on $\widehat C$. Their degrees are equal. Each of the prescribed modifications increases the degree of one axis at one lift of $c_i$ and of the other axis at the opposite lift, so equality persists. The map $\rho:D_k\to C$ factors through $\widehat C$, since it kills the sign character defining that cover. The two ordered isotropic lines in the residual orthogonal plane have equal degrees on $\mathcal R_k$ and pair perfectly with each other. Their degrees are zero, which proves the vanishing with either possible determinant orientation.

When the axis lines are globally ordered, the pairing gives $L_+\otimes L_-=K_C$, hence $d_++d_-=2g-2$. Deform $h$ to a diagonal metric for this splitting. Then $I=(i,-i)$ commutes with each polar operator along the homotopy $\bar\partial_N+t c_hQ$ for $0\le t\le1$: the antilinear term interchanges the two axes. The evaluation and matching conditions at nodes and all principal-part domains respect $I$, so these odd Fredholm operators are complex-linear for $I$ over the whole compact family. Define $B_\pm=\sum_{\ell_i=(L_\pm)_{c_i}}c_i$ and $G_\pm=L_\pm(B_\pm)M^{-1}$. At $t=0$, in the original fiber complex structure the odd cohomologies are those of $G_+\oplus G_-$ on $C$. This holds as an index-bundle identification in families: use common complex-linear supplements of $(0,1)$-forms on regular disks. The transfer $s=d\,v$ identifies the stabilized holomorphic solution equations and their complex orientations. At a marked node (2) transfers the one allowed principal part to the corresponding axis. In the unaffected axis ordinary odd values must match at the node, exactly as for an unmodified odd coefficient. Finite-dimensional stabilization thus gives the same complex index on the boundary as on the interior.

The complex structure $I$ retains the first Dolbeault cohomology complex and conjugates the second, both on kernels and cokernels. Write
$$
 \chi_\pm=d_\pm+m_\pm-k+1-g.
$$
At $t=0$ the original determinant orientation differs from the $I$-complex determinant orientation by $(-1)^{\chi_-}$, since conjugation reverses the real determinant of a complex vector space of odd dimension and $\dim H^0(G_-)+\dim H^1(G_-)\equiv\chi_-\pmod2$. This comparison of determinant lines extends along the entire mass homotopy by continuity, including values where kernel dimensions jump. At $t=1$ the kernel is zero, so it compares the desired orientation of the real cokernel plane $J$ with its $I$ orientation.

For its $I$-complex Chern class, the negative family index is the sum of $-R\pi_*G_+$ and the complex conjugate of $-R\pi_*G_-$. Normalize $M_0$ at $c_*\notin B_0$ as above and put $t_0=c_1(T)$ for $M=M_0\otimes T$. Curve Riemann–Roch gives
$$
 c_1(-R\pi_*G_\pm)=P+\chi_\pm t_0,\qquad
                \int_{\mathcal R_k} t_0=n/4.
$$
Indeed the mixed Poincaré square gives $P$ in each rank-one calculation, and the negative index of rank $-\chi_\pm$ is tensored by $T^{-1}$. Subtracting the negative-axis Chern class from the positive-axis class gives $\int_{\mathcal R_k}c_1(J,I)=(\chi_+-\chi_-)n/4$. Multiplication by the determinant sign proves (4). Since $\chi_++\chi_-=-1$, interchanging axes changes the signs of both factors. For the repeated spin, $d_\pm=g-1$ and $(-1)^{m_-}=\prod_i\kappa_i$, recovering (1).

Finally use $N=L\oplus(K_C L^{-1})$ with its off-diagonal perfect pairing and arbitrary integer $\deg L$. Its underlying smooth complex bundle has the same rank and first Chern class for every choice. Smooth bundle identifications can be chosen to carry the selected axes at the finitely many flags to any prescribed lines in the reference bundle. In particular one can arrange $m_+=m$ and $m_-=0$. Then the absolute value in (4) grows without bound with $\deg L$. A fixed positive mass with connected cover of axes and the same isotropic flags has zero endpoint value; for example use the distinct-spin pair under such an identification. By the signed-count theorem, generic paths between these endpoints have arbitrarily large absolute signed counts with all $g,k,c_i,\ell_i$ fixed as the ordered-axis endpoint varies. This statement concerns real normal operators with their prescribed polar problems.
